What the mathematics results teach (read before designing; cite in the memo): the Erdős-problem wave of 2025-26 (DeepMind's Aletheia, OpenAI's unit-distance counterexample, GPT-5.4 Pro on #1196; Quanta, 3 Aug 2026; arXiv 2601.07421) fell to (1) a curated, machine-readable problem repository that anyone could attempt, (2) cheap mechanical verification (Lean) that let many attempts be judged without a human, (3) breadth: one agent resolved 9 of 353 open problems at a few hundred dollars each, so the win rate was about 2.5 percent and the method was to try everything cheaply, (4) literature search first, because the commonest failure was rediscovering a result already in print (Erdős #333, solved by Erdős himself in 1977), which is our N0/N1 failure, (5) fresh-instance verification chains (a solution handed to a fresh session to check, repeated), (6) problem selection toward domains where the model is strong and formally stated, away from the hardest famous items, and (7) persistence over simple hypotheses (Urquhart's Cyphral Distich fell in 44 minutes to "the key is the host text", LESSONS.md). Our pipeline already has (4) and (5) as rules 1, 7 and 10 and the solver/verifier split; it lacks (1), (2), (3) and (6) as practice.
