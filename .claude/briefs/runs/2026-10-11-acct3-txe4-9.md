# TXE4-9 CALIB: a reader calibrates on two known-answer pages of the same Dinteville hand, sees only which of its own signs were wrong, then reads a third page

Status: DESIGNED 11 Oct 2026 (workflow wf_436351dc-099: three designers, two judges), NOT LAUNCHED -- waiting for the owner's go. Estimated cost USD 12.5.

For the owner: Has a reader first transcribe two other pages of the same Dinteville hand whose answers are known, shows it only which of its own signs were wrong, then has it read a third page. That page is scored against its period decipherment; the control gets the same extra pages but no corrections.

WHY.
- TXE3-4: the agreed-wrong rate (0-16% of signs) is a property of the hand. No second reader and no err_2reader can see it; only a known answer in the same hand reaches it.
- TXE3-3: readers share errors on letter-shaped signs.
- TXE-H: pair hints derived from tile statistics fixed 11 and broke 3 against pass A (p 0.057), but 7 of the 11 fixes were on a pair the hints never covered. The statistics missed the readers' actual confusions; feedback uses the reader's own.
The owner's rule is that a known answer may score a result but is never the tool's input on the scored item. So the calibration comes from two OTHER benchmark items of the same hand, and the scored item is a third. BENCHMARK-TX's hand column puts all three in one cluster: fr.3619 f.89/f.98v/f.113, the Dinteville office hand of Nov 1591. This replaces the proposal's same-item design (Vivonne train lines feeding Vivonne test lines), which met only the spirit of the rule.

TOOL CHANGE. New tools/tx_calibrate.py, with an offline test. It has three subcommands:
- build: writes a calibration call's file list and task.
- feedback: scores the calibration read with tools/tx_bench.py's align against the calibration items' truth. It writes one row per wrong UNFLAGGED scored position: item, line, the reader's own label sequence with 3 neighbours on each side, and [your label -> allowed labels]. It names labels only, never a letter or a key value. Flagged positions give no feedback. Insertions and deletions are given as 'extra sign here' and 'a sign missed here'.
- target: assembles call 2. That is the calibration crops plus the reader's own calibration read, plus either the feedback (mode F) or no feedback (mode C), then the target crops.

METHOD.
1. Checks.
   - Diff the label tables of the three items' briefs, report the result, and use the label table of blind_pass_brief_f89.md for every call.
   - Count read-free, without truth, how often the label pairs on which the committed f98v and f113 passes split occur in the committed f89 passes. That is the stated power of the transfer check.
   Unit: 15 minutes.
2. Reads. Run 2 independent passes. In each pass:
   - Call 1 (Sonnet, fresh context) reads the 17 calibration crops, and the script builds the feedback.
   - Calls 2F and 2C are each a fresh Sonnet call: the calibration crops plus the reader's own call-1 read (plus the feedback, for F) plus the 14 f89 crops. The output follows the f89 brief's format.
   That makes 6 calls in total. Unit = one call, about USD 0.5 and 8 minutes. Beside, only if under 80%: one plain f89 read per pass with no calibration (T0).
3. Commit the reads, the feedback files and the sha256. Score each once: tx_bench --item dint-f89-gloss --exclude-flagged --strict --ci, plus --paired F vs C per pass. If the item carries no flags, state that the two masks are identical.

GATE. PRIMARY: each arm's mean unit-cost edits over its 2 passes. Calibration feedback is adopted if all of these hold:
- mean(C) >= 1.2 x mean(F) as measured;
- the gap > the maximum within-arm pass spread;
- the flagged-excluded direction does not reverse;
- the transfer check: in each pass, the confusion pairs named in that pass's feedback occur less often in F's target read than in C's, in both passes. Use pair counts from tx_bench's top confusions, or --pair if TXE4-6 has landed it.
Otherwise the result is 'not distinguishable'.
CONTROL AND WHY IT CAN VARY. Arm C gets identical calibration crops and its own calibration read in the same context, without the corrections. That isolates the content of the feedback from the extra context and the extra examples of the hand, and its edits vary freely. The transfer check can fail differently from the edit total: a generic gain would show without a fall in the named pairs.
DECLARED LIMITS. The gloss is visible on f.89, so the absolute level is an upper bound and the gate is per item. This is a dev item. The readers are Sonnet; the committed Opus passes are beside only.
