## Which errors a presentation change can reach

"Every reader, every presentation" (all-same-wrong) is 8 of 29 eval errors and 6 of 37 dev errors, and it is concentrated: on
eval it is the Spinelli h dropped by all three passes (crop, 2), four single Spinelli misreads every pass shares (i <- EIGHT,
e <- SIX, p <- THREE, n <- PHI) and no.87's o <- T70 and z <- T60 (one of the two a band-cut crop); outside the pools,
f178r's s <- T70; on dev the Dinteville i <- x digit
look-alike (2) and no.87 e <- T36, t <- T90. These are not to be re-attacked by another rendering, order or compare layout: they
are a truth/key question (a floor-audit candidate), a hand that really sits on the other sign, or a crop no pass saw -- the
fix is the truth audit or the cut, not the reader. The reader-split mass is the larger part: no pass right but the passes
disagree (all-wrong-split, 11 eval / 12 dev, e.g. Spinelli r <- SIX x4, Ceppo n <- ? x3, all three no.87 thin errors) is where
the readers are unsure, so a disagreement flag can find it but a vote cannot fix it; and some pass right (majority, minority:
10 eval / 19 dev, 34% / 51%) is where a second look can help, led by no.87's d <- T98 (7 positions over both pools, never
all-same-wrong) and l <- T64, and Ceppo o <- S88 and a <- S65. The look-alike class is almost entirely reader-split (eval 0 of
11 all-same-wrong, dev 3 of 14): it is the class a compare or second-look instrument targets; the "other" class on eval is the
one where all-same-wrong dominates (5 of 8). On the first campaign's evidence a presentation change (rendering, order) moves
the reader-split share at most; the 8 eval positions every pass shares are its floor.
