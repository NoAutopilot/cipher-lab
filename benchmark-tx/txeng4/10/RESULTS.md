## Control (rule 3)

(e) can vary and did: 66-74 of about 600 boxes differ in geometry from (d), and its err_true differs by 0.06-0.08. The
label-permutation null on each arm's own boxes removes box count from the comparison (VERIFY-6's lesson): the gains are
0.144-0.175 across all nine arms, the recognition split's the lowest. The box-count effect itself is visible again: (d) has
the most boxes (663) and the highest err_true; its SER insertions (55) are the highest of the nine; its deletions (33) are
in the split arms' range. The recognition rule over-splits (30 of 67 wide boxes into 3 or 4 pieces): the Laplace vote
posterior rates a sliver as confidently as a whole sign, so "looks like a known sign" is true of almost any piece, and the
cut set that wins is the one with the most confidently-labelled pieces, not the one at the sign boundaries. The (e) seeds
split less (594-614 boxes) because permuted labels flatten the posteriors, and read better for it -- more evidence that at
this reader strength fewer boxes beat better-placed cuts.

## Limits

- Dev result, one leaf, 13 lines; the hold-out is TXE3-6's within-item line split; no eval item in a joining hand is unfrozen.
- The labels' join failed its bound (above); the kNN trained on them nevertheless reads above chance on every arm, so the
  labels are mostly right, but their one-position slop is unmeasured against truth and the verdict is capped accordingly.
- Weak classifier by design (read-free): a kNN on 158 machine-consensus-labelled exemplars. A reader at product strength
  (S2: 0.15 on this hand) could use recognition-guided cuts differently; this is not a test under the product pipeline.
- The objective is the brief's (summed log-posterior minus L per piece). A posterior that can say "this piece is no sign"
  (a distance-aware reject class) was not tested; it would be a different instrument, not a re-tuning of L (rule 3's
  third-attempt clause).
- Value-level truth: a homophone swap is invisible; every figure is a lower bound on sign error.

## For the owner (three plain sentences)

Asking the computer to cut a joined shape where the pieces look most like known signs did not find the sign boundaries on
the Vivonne letter: it read worse than leaving the shapes whole, worse than cutting at the thinnest ink, and worse than
cutting at random, on all thirteen test lines where anything changed. The classifier used was a small nearest-neighbour
reader trained only on labels the six machine passes agreed on; it reads well above guessing, but it is confident about
almost any fragment, so it cut too much. Because the step that attached those machine labels to boxes failed its own
alignment check, the result is logged as "untested with this tool" rather than as a proven negative, and only a better
classifier (TXE4-3's) or a reader that can say "this piece is not a sign" would reopen the question.
