William Stamford to Thurloe's office, Calais, 13 March 1655 (Birch 1742 vol. 3 pp.188-189): keyed reading of a letter printed without its decipherment

The claim

Birch (1742) prints William Stamford's letter from Calais of 13 March 1655 with its cipher numerals and no decipherment. We read its cipher with the key given by Birch's printed decipherments of Stamford's letters of 20 and 30 March 1655. Thurloe's office deciphered this system in 1655, and Tomokiyo has since reconstructed and published it (Cryptiana, 'William Stamford (1655)'). Our table agrees with his on every value that occurs in this letter. By token: H 64, C 338, M 16, U 6 of 424, and eleven lines of p.188 are still partly incoherent. No prior decipherment of this letter located in Birch and its indexes, British History Online, CSPD 1655, the Clarendon calendar vol. 3, the Nicholas Papers, Abbott, the Clarke Papers, Gardiner, Underdown, the Bodleian's catalogues, Tomokiyo's pages, the solver repositories, DECODE, Google Books or Internet Archive full text (searched 24 Sept 2026, AUDIT.md). The original leaf, Bodleian MS Rawl. A. 24, has not been seen.

The verifier's safe sentence, quoted from AUDIT.md.

Stamford writes in cipher that the matter came to his knowledge 'by meere chance' and 'without the least injunction of secrecy'. (interpretation: the verifier's English paraphrase of the read passage)

The proof

Open each line for the evidence. Nothing is hidden; the repository holds every file.

Signs read, by grade: H 64, C 338, M 16, U 6 (of 424)

H read from a key of the time or a published key; C from a known plain text; S by cryptanalysis that beat a matched control; M uncertain; I inferred or repaired; U not read.

Counted by the decode script over every cipher token; the reading and its per-token grades are in the repository.

How the reading regenerates
  • Key: key_blake.tsv, key_blake_extended.tsv, key_burton.tsv, key_butler.tsv
  • Reading: https://github.com/NoAutopilot/cipher-lab/blob/main/ciphers/thurloe-printed/reading_P11-13.txt

Run python3 ciphers/thurloe-printed/decode.py --check from a clone of the repository: it rebuilds the reading from the transcription and the key, and exits with an error if the committed reading is stale.
