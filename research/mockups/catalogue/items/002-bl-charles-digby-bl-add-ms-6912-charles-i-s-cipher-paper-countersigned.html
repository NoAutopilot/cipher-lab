What can be said

BL Add MS 6912 is the cipher of Charles I's instructions to Stephen Goffe for the Prince of Orange (May 1644, countersigned by George Digby) that Charles Wheatstone deciphered and printed in 1862 (N0, key published); the same instructions had already been printed in clear from the Orange archive by Groen van Prinsterer in 1859.

Register line

The British Library's catalogue does not say that Add MS 6912 (seven pages of numerals, each signed C.R. and countersigned George Digby) is the cipher Charles Wheatstone deciphered and printed in 1862 for the Philobiblon Society, 'Instructions pour le Sieur de Goffe' on the 1644 Orange marriage proposal. We made the identification from Ellis's 1858 description and Wheatstone's covering letter, and a separate verifier confirmed it against the British Museum's 1849 Index of Additional Manuscripts (6912 is the only Charles I cipher paper in the 1783-1835 accessions) and found the instructions themselves printed in clear from the Orange side by Groen van Prinsterer in 1859. Class N0: nothing here is new to the world, only to the shelfmark; the contribution is the shelfmark-to-publication link, which the verifier's AUDIT.md gives with its search log.
