# N4 readings: what each says and why it matters

Written 24 Sept 2026 (clock read 17:13 UTC) for the owner's question of 17:10 UTC: "any historically significant work
in the N4s?" Fifteen items in seven folders. Everything below comes from each folder's NOTES.md, AUDIT.md and reading
files as of main at c22b076. Nothing was fetched, decoded or reclassified. Where a sentence gives historical context
that is not in the folder's own sources, it is marked *(context)*.

Rule 10 applies throughout. N4 means "no prior decipherment located after a logged search", with principal editions,
catalogues and project pages covered. It does not exclude unpublished or archival work. Every reading here is partial.
Grades: H from a key source, C from known plaintext, S cryptanalytic with a control, M uncertain, I inferred, U unkeyed.
Each section ends with a `rating:` line and a `links:` line for the board (added 24 Sept 2026, 17:25 UTC, at the
parent's request). The links are built from identifiers recorded in each folder and were not re-fetched for this file.
In quotations, `·` or `[nnn]` is an unread sign, `?` marks my own uncertainty about a word division, and *italic
glosses* are paraphrase, not reading.

The honest answer to the owner: none of the fifteen readings, as they stand, changes a known event. In every case
the event is already in print from another letter, a reply, an endorsement or a clear passage. What the cipher adds is
who told whom, when, and a few particulars. Three items carry the most weight. **Gramont f.30** is a 1,969-sign dispatch
to Francis I on Florence during the siege, and only about a third of it reads as continuous French, so it could still
move up once the rest is read. **Stamford P4** is a Royalist informer's warning, about a week before the March 1655
rising, of "a more general rising" with "a thousand armes" ready. **Saxony 57** is Elector August's secret note to
Orange that the Emperor had asked him to elect Maximilian King of the Romans. They are details that sharpen known
events. None of them is a discovery.

## ciphers/fr2980-gramont f.29r no.21 -- Gramont to Villandry, Rome, 20 May [1530]

**Letter.** Gabriel de Gramont, bishop of Tarbes (created cardinal 8 June 1530), to Jean Breton de Villandry, the
king's secretary. Rome, 20 May [1530]. BnF fr.2980 f.29r, no.21 (Gallica ark btv1b9059991d). No printed edition of the
letter. Its existence and date are in the 1868 BnF catalogue and the *Catalogue des actes de François Ier* IX.
Mixed letter: about 7 clear lines, 14 cipher lines, clear signature. Key: Gramont 1530 (Tomokiyo; Lasry 2023).

**Reading** (`reading.txt`; 568 tokens: H 533, M 30, U 5; one reconciled transcription). The solver's word division,
lines 1-3 and 8-12:
> "il y baille a ce porteur ... article que j'ay mis a part, et i[l?] faict l'adresse de dessus a vous, combien que ce
> soit au roy ... et luy ay monstre tout re[com]tenu pour le contenter et oster ... de suspecon, qui est cause que j'ay
> faict ledit article a part pour vous do[nn]er cognoissance de tout, mais je vous prie advertissement(?) ... ledict
> seigneur et tous aultres ... soit mene ... secretement"

*He has handed the bearer an article that he set apart, addressed on the outside to Villandry although it is meant
for the king. He showed [someone] the whole content to satisfy him and remove suspicion, which is why he made the
article separate: to let Villandry know everything. He asks that it be handled secretly.* Only L10-L11 and parts of
L02, L08, L09 and L12 are continuous French. L01, L03-L07 and L13 have probable sign misidentifications. "du vingtiesme"
(L07) fits the date.

**Already known.** The clear part (grade M, read by eye): Gramont sends a copy of the letters he wrote "lundi" [16 May]
in case this courier reaches Villandry before the packet to the king; "la satisfaction de nostre Sainct Pere"; "les
affaires du Roy par deca". Tomokiyo named the letter as readable with the key, and Bourdeau catalogued it (no.328).
Neither gave a reading.

**What the cipher adds.** Procedure and secrecy. It shows a two-channel dispatch: an open text shown to a third party
to allay suspicion, and a separate article for the king sent under cover to his secretary. That article is probably
f.30 (an inference, grade I, in AUDIT.md). There is no political news in the readable part.

**Moment.** Rome, May 1530 *(context)*: Charles V had been crowned at Bologna in February. The imperial and papal army
had besieged Florence since October 1529. Francis I's sons were still hostages in Spain under the Treaty of Cambrai.
Gramont was in Rome from April to June 1530 on the king's business and on Henry VIII's divorce. The consistory of
8 June made him cardinal.

**Significance: confirms or adds detail to what the edition gives.** It adds a detail of diplomatic practice, the
routing of the secret article. The letter's substance lies in f.30.

rating: confirms or adds detail -- the readable cipher is procedure (a secret article routed to the king under cover), no political news

## ciphers/fr2980-gramont f.30r-v no.22 -- Gramont to Francis I, Rome, 20 May 1530

**Letter.** Gramont to the king (the address is inferred from "Sire" and "vostre commandement" in the cipher, grade I,
strong). Rome, 20 May 1530 (clear dateline). BnF fr.2980 f.30r-v, no.22. Entirely in cipher. No edition. The nearest
print is a different, clear letter: Gramont to Francis I, April 1530, *Archivio storico italiano*, Appendice I,
doc. XXXVIII, pp.473-481.

**Reading** (`reading_f30.txt` with the published key alone: 1,969 tokens, H 1,502, M 231, U 236.
`reading_f30_extended.txt` with 11 signs added cryptanalytically: H 1,486, S 181, M 239, U 63; matched control run).
Parts of 32 of 55 lines read as continuous French. The top eight lines of f.30r are unread. Decoded runs quoted in
AUDIT.md:
> "la declaration de la liberte de Florence" (f.30v L02-03, L10); "la ville et la force entre vo[s] mains" (f.30v L09);
> "qu'il ve[u]lt aller en Avi[g]non" (f.30r L32; the G sign is unchecked, so "Avignon" is uncertain); "pour recouvrer
> ce que ses predecesse[u]rs ... ont perdu" (f.30r L33-34); "il n'a aucune desliberation" (f.30v L01); "l'ambassadeur
> ... et aultres ses ministres" (f.30v L12); "tres [hu]mbl[em]ent, Sire" (f.30v L05-06)

*He reports on a declaration of Florence's liberty, the town and its force coming into the king's hands, someone who
wants to go to Avignon [?] to recover what his predecessors lost, and someone who has come to no decision. An
ambassador and "other ministers" of a third party are mentioned.* Who "he" is in each run is not established.

**Already known.** The April letter prints the plan: the Florentines lay down arms, the papal camp withdraws, and "la
force de la ville demourast entre voz mains" under the king's protection (ASI App. I p.477). The negotiation is in
Pastor X and De Leva III. A string test shows f.30 is not a cipher copy of the April letter: its longest shared run is
a formula of 18 letters, against control texts at 9-11.

**What the cipher adds.** The readable runs show the April plan for Florence still alive on 20 May, now framed as a
formal "declaration of the liberty of Florence", and they mention a party pressing to recover lost ground. The unread
third may hold the particulars.

**Moment.** *(context)* The siege of Florence was in its eighth month. The city surrendered on 12 August 1530 and the
Medici were restored. A French scheme to put Florence under the king's protection was one of Francis I's last levers
in Italy after Cambrai. It came to nothing.

**Significance: confirms or adds detail to what the edition gives.** In the readable runs, the subject and wording
continue the printed April letter. This is the one item likely to move up: a complete reading of a 1,969-sign
ambassadorial dispatch on Florence, three months before the fall, would be a substantive source. That depends on the
unread top lines, the 63 unkeyed signs and the doubtful signs (eh T/D, the L/G sign in "Avignon").

rating: confirms or adds detail -- readable runs continue the printed April 1530 plan for Florence; a third is unread and could move it up

## ciphers/fr20140-danzay-1557 f.35-36r -- Danzay to the Cardinal of Lorraine, [Copenhagen], 27 Jan 1557

**Letter.** Charles de Danzay, French envoy in Denmark, to the Cardinal of Lorraine. [Copenhagen], 27 Jan 1557 (the
year style, 1557 or 1558 n.s., is unresolved). BnF fr.20140 f.35r-v, continuing on f.36r. No edition. The only
contemporary letter without a decipherment among the four January 1557 letters (f.16, f.24 and f.30 carry one). Key:
Tomokiyo 2026, built from those siblings.

**Reading** (`reading.txt`: 638 cipher tokens, H 509 of which 61 are nulls, M 59, U 70; 152 clear-hand words.
`reading_f36.txt`: 67 cipher tokens, H 37, M 26, U 4; 13 clear words). About 23 of 37 lines give continuous French.
Clear-hand words are in CAPS:
> V9-V10 "tenu DAULTRE PART le chancelier ... peult ... beaucoup"; V2 "chancel[l]i-r ... Danoys ... quelque"; V5
> "[r]oyaume ... a promis"; V17-V19 "passions ... ET affections de ceulx desquels ... me ayder ... la resolution des
> affaires ... pend"; V23 "de leur volonte"; V24 "en doubte"; V25-V28 "au mo[y]en ... de faire ... quelque ...
> Augsbourg par ... les marchans de Lion ... car de Augsbourg on le pourra"; f.36r "a vec [q]ues peu de perte pour le
> change ET EN TELLE SORTE VOUS SEREZ ASSEURÉ DE FAIRE ... paye[?]ment par deca"

*The Danish chancellor can do much. Something concerning the kingdom was promised. Danzay depends on the goodwill of
people whose will is still in doubt. He proposes a way of doing something through Augsburg and the merchants of Lyon,
"with little loss on the exchange", so that the Cardinal can be sure of making a payment in Denmark.* R1-R4, R7, V1,
V3, V4 and V29 do not read.

**Already known.** Tomokiyo's key page and the three deciphered sibling letters of 10 and 27 January (manuscript, not
printed). The clear-hand passages of this letter give its frame: "affin que vous peussiez congnoistre l'estat des
affaires de ce royaulme"; "la promesse qui de long temps m'a esté faicte"; "attendant plus certain et expres
commandement".

**What the cipher adds.** The names and the mechanism: the chancellor as the man who counts, and a money transfer
through Augsburg with Lyon merchants, at little loss on exchange, to pay something in Denmark.

**Moment.** *(context)* January 1557. The Truce of Vaucelles had just broken down, and Henri II was back at war with
Philip II. France was courting Christian III of Denmark. Danzay began a posting at Copenhagen that lasted into the
1580s.

**Significance: confirms or adds detail to what the edition gives.** There is no edition of this letter. Against its
own clear text and the siblings, the cipher adds the channel and the chancellor, not a new policy.

rating: confirms or adds detail -- adds the chancellor's role and an Augsburg-Lyon money channel to the letter's own clear frame

## ciphers/thurloe-printed P4 -- William Stamford to [Kelsey or Thurloe], Calais, 13 March 1655 N.S.

**Letter.** William Stamford, a Royalist offering his services, from Calais, 13 March 1655 N.S. (endorsed "13/3 March
1654/5"). Recipient unnamed. Macray's Rawlinson index points to Col. Thomas Kelsey. "Your friend" is the Protector.
Bodleian MS Rawl. A. 24 (leaf unseen). **Printed:** Birch, *Thurloe State Papers* III (1742) pp.187-189, with the
cipher as numerals and no decipherment, and also on British History Online. The endorsement is printed: "His desire of a
correspondence, and promise of performing some emminent service (in case my lord protector will engage to reward him)
namely in discovering of the plott, &c."

**Reading** (`pool_1654/reading_P4.txt`: 424 tokens, H 64, C 338, S 0, M 16, U 6). The key comes from Birch's
printed decipherments of Stamford's letters of 20 and 30 March 1655 (cross-letter control 92-94%). The runs are
mechanical; my restorations follow in italics:
> "it is come to my knowledge by {meerechance} and {withouttheleastiniunctionof} secrecy {thepersonigoti(q?)o(w?)tfrom}
> taking it {forgranted} upon his meeting {meeasheethoug(x?)t} stealing out of whitehall {<england?>} that i was not only
> perticularly informed of {thematerbutalso} employed in it yet since it is the absolute breaking of
> {apothhathaibeeneahtching} by {thewholepartieof_} ... render my selfe {irreconciiablkwiththemaslongaailite}"
> ... "the {qualitie} i am of the familiar acquaintance i have amongst all the {<thecavaliers?>}" ... "it is a more
> {<generalrising>} ... in which more {peopleareengagedtheninanioneof} the {formerones(w?)hatsoeutr} ...
> {<have>notla} ... {de<designs>tosurp} ... thousand armes {<which>l} ... {readitobeemaceuse} and {whentheirebusines}
> {eshalberipeforit} and which makes mee beleeve the time appointed for it is not farre off"

*By chance, and under no promise of secrecy, he learned of a plot that "hath been a hatching" by the whole party of
[143]. His source assumed he was in it. Revealing it will make him irreconcilable with them for life. He has a wide
acquaintance among the cavaliers. It is a more general rising, with more people engaged than in any former one. There
are designs to surprise [places], a thousand arms ready for use when their business is ripe, and the time appointed is
not far off.* Eleven lines of p.188 (L51-61) stay partly incoherent. "England" and "the cavaliers" are M.

**Already known.** The clear prose asks for a reward and protection and offers to come to London unseen. The
endorsement summarises the offer. Stamford's later letters of 20 and 30 March are printed by Birch with their
decipherment and also speak of the "general rising". Tomokiyo has since reconstructed the system. Our table agrees with his on every value that occurs in this letter.

**What the cipher adds.** The substance behind the endorsement's "plott": a warning dated 3 March O.S. that a general
Royalist rising was imminent, bigger than earlier ones, with arms ready. It is the earliest of Stamford's warnings in
the printed series.

**Moment.** The Royalist rising of March 1655 *(context for the dates)*: the general rising was planned for 8 March
O.S. and fizzled almost everywhere. Penruddock's party seized Salisbury on 12 March O.S. and was beaten at South Molton
on 14 March O.S. Stamford wrote about a week before. The Protectorate's foreknowledge of the rising from informers is
well known. This letter is one more such source, not the first.

**Significance: confirms or adds detail to what the edition gives.** Birch prints the letter and its endorsement, and
Stamford's deciphered letters of 20 and 30 March cover the same rising. P4 adds the earliest warning and its
particulars (scale, a thousand arms, imminence).

rating: confirms or adds detail -- Birch prints the letter and its endorsement, and Stamford's deciphered 20 and 30 March letters cover the same rising; P4 adds the earliest warning and its particulars

## ciphers/huntington-blathwayt-madrid-1728 mssBLA 186 -- the Abbé Pareti to the Earl of Marchmont, Madrid, 13 Sept 1728

**Letter.** "Letter of intelligence", Madrid, 13 Sept 1728, signed "N". The writer is identified as the Abbé Pareti
from Rose's print. Huntington mssBLA 186 (pointer 61211). **Printed:** Rose, *Marchmont Papers* (1831) ii 414-415, as an
English extract dated there 3 Sept 1728, with the cipher line shown as ". . . (Cypher.) . . .". The second cipher line
is outside Rose's extract.

**Reading** (`reading.txt`; 24 tokens: C 20, U 4). Key from contemporary glosses of other letters in the run:
> p1 (after "s'il se trouve sur leurs estats"): "l'ambassadeur [73] a été fort [470] [778] [190]te affaire" (*"[cet]te
> affaire"* is an inference)
> p3 (after "pour empescher les gallions de passer en Europe"): "Monsieur de Patigno m'en a [849 = 'monsieur', doubtful]
> ce soir"

*The ambassador [of ?] was very [...] in this affair [Ripperda's escape]. Patiño told me this evening [about the
squadron?].*

**Already known.** Everything around the lines: Ripperda escaped from Segovia castle on 2 September, and Madrid heard
on the 10th. The court wrote to all foreign ministers. The governor was a relation of the Marquis de la Paz. A new
Spanish squadron was to stop the galleons reaching Europe.

**What the cipher adds.** Two fragments: that an ambassador (unnamed, 73 unkeyed) was deeply involved in "this affair",
and that Patiño himself was the writer's source that evening.

**Moment.** *(context)* In 1728 Spain and Britain were at odds after the siege of Gibraltar (1727), while the Congress
of Soissons talked. Ripperda, the fallen minister, had fled first to the British ambassador's house in 1726. His escape
in 1728 took him to England and then to Morocco.

**Significance: confirms or adds detail to what the edition gives.** Two lines against a printed letter. The one
unkeyed group (73) that would name the ambassador is the detail that matters, and it is unread.

rating: confirms or adds detail -- two cipher lines in a letter Rose printed; the ambassador's name code (73) is unread

## ciphers/huntington-blathwayt-madrid-1728 mssBLA 191 enclosure (a) -- [agent] to 'milord', Port Ste Marie, [1729]

**Letter.** Undated cipher enclosure from Port Ste Marie, forwarded from Cessnock to the Duke of Newcastle on 8 Aug
1729. Huntington mssBLA 191 (pointer 61008, p5). The writer is not named. Pareti is probable for the run but not
established for this item. No print. The copy sent to Newcastle has not been traced.

**Reading** (`reading.txt`; 141 tokens: C 106, M 19, U 16). Rendering from NOTES.md, where the joins are interpretation:
> "L'[805] que je vous ai, milord, est bien grande pour votre lettre du [6] ... comme je [285]is agit apres le depart du
> [214], car Monsieur Keene m'a[i]me, mais il n'a aucun ordre des [1019][711]s pour moi, quoyque dans l'affaire passee je
> lui ai fait [1118] bon [1052]ce, sans qu'il m'ait jamais dit ce que je pouvois fournir pour le mieux a l'accommodement
> ... et je serai inutile dans l'[585=ig]norance. Mandez-moi donc quelque ordre pour ma [222]."

*He thanks "milord" for his letter. Keene (the British minister) likes him but has no orders about him. He gave Keene
good service in "the past affair" but was never told what he could contribute to the accommodation. He will be useless
without instructions, so please send orders.* "milord", "serai" and "ignorance" rest on M or I values.

**Already known.** BLA 192 (Port Ste Marie, 11 Aug 1729) is a clear letter on the same theme, with bracketed passages
that went into cipher ("je n'ai pu trouver aucun secours dans Monsieur Kenne"). It is a different text.

**What the cipher adds.** An agent's complaint about his standing with Keene during the "accommodement".

**Moment.** *(context)* In summer 1729 Britain and Spain were negotiating the settlement signed as the Treaty of
Seville (November 1729). The court was at Seville and Port Ste Marie (El Puerto de Santa María).

**Significance: confirms or adds detail to what the edition gives.** There is no edition. BLA 192's clear text already
carries the complaint, so the cipher adds the agent's own framing and no diplomatic news.

rating: confirms or adds detail -- an agent's request for orders; BLA 192's clear text already carries the complaint about Keene

## ciphers/lodewijk-van-nassau-1573-74 WVO 4610 -- Lodewijk van Nassau to Orange, 3 June 1573

**Letter.** Louis of Nassau to his brother William of Orange. 3 June 1573, no place ("Pour Hollande", duplicate).
KHA A 11/XIV D/13a, WVO 4610. No edition. Orange's reply of 17 June 1573 (Leiden) is printed: Groen van Prinsterer,
*Archives* I/IV, Lettre CDXXVII, pp.156-160.

**Reading** (`reading_4610.txt`; 1,545 tokens including clear words: C 1,098, M 101, I 58, U 288. Numerals only:
1,222, C 1,073). Key aligned from the contemporary decipherments of 4613/4615. Words in braces are clear text or word
signs; lower-case runs are decoded numerals with no word breaks. Readable fragments:
> "Il fault que [vous?] pardonne de ce ... qui depesche ... ce lundy"; "de la rechute"; "semployer pour rapaiser";
> "electeurs ... je luy ay propose une conuocation ... des electeurs"; "aduisez aux conditions ... proposees ... affin
> [de] pouuoir ouurir ... la negotiation"; "leduc dalencon"; "trois cens cheuaux"; "capiteines frencois";
> "strabourg"; "delapaix"

*An apology for a delayed messenger. His relapse [of fever]. Efforts to appease. He proposed a convocation of the
Electors and asks Orange's view of the conditions, so as to open a negotiation. The Duke of Alençon, 300 horse, French
captains, Strasbourg, peace.* Most lines are fragmentary.

**Already known.** Orange's reply answers it: "Vostre lettre du 3e jour de ce mois ... la fiebvre vous a de rechief
quicté", the Emperor's answer to the princes about pacification, and three cavalry companies.

**What the cipher adds.** Louis's side of it: the proposal to convene the Electors, and the French contacts (Alençon,

## ciphers/lodewijk-van-nassau-1573-74 WVO 4616 -- Lodewijk van Nassau to Orange, camp at Weeze, 12 April 1574

**Letter.** Louis to Orange, "au camp de Weess" (Weeze), near Goch, 12 April 1574. KHA A 11/XIV D/13a, WVO 4616. No
edition. Orange's reply of 15 April 1574 (Gorinchem) is printed: Groen IV, Lettre CDLXXXIV, pp.368-369 (WVO 4503;
our re-reading of 4503's cipher agrees with that print, N0).

**Reading** (`reading_4616.txt`; 261 tokens: C 207, M 27, I 1, U 26. Numerals: 226, C 200). The opening is in clear
(WVO incipit):
> L04 (clear): "Monsr frere, nous sommes [cest soer icy arivé] aupres de Goch et sommes"
> L05-L17 (numerals): "forcenpeingpod·enlogier / pourdemainca·noouu·· / ... / la·ependan·ou·auise / ron ... ogerpar /
> dec·urle·deducdeclen / ... / loge·emainobereunidor / asset {nous}{Verrons} demainl·uel"
> L18-L21 (clear): "de vous supplie que nous puissions avoir de vous nouvelles ... au plustost que sera possible ...
> Vostre bon frere et serviteur ... au camp de Weess, le [xii] Avril"

*Arrived tonight near Goch. [Forced? in difficulty?] to find lodging for tomorrow. Cependant nous aviserons [?]. [Loger
par? ... le duc de Cle[ves?]]. Lodged tomorrow [?]. We shall see tomorrow. Send news as soon as possible.* Only "pour
demain", "loger" and "nous verrons demain" are firm. "duc de Cle[ves]" is my division of `deducdeclen` and is
uncertain.

12 April, looking for quarters for the next day.

**Moment.** Mookerheyde was fought on 14 April 1574. Louis and his brother Henry were killed and their army

Orange read it.

## ciphers/august-van-saksen-1561-64 WVO 53 -- Orange to Elector August, Breda, 24 Oct 1561

**Letter.** William of Orange to Elector August, Breda, 24 Oct 1561. Autograph postscript, partly in cipher. Dresden
HStA Loc. 9941/3 f.266r-v, WVO 53. No edition (Japikse's edition ends in September 1561). WVO summary of the clear
letter: safe arrival at Breda, news of Spain and France, dogs and ferrets sent.

**Reading** (`reading_53.txt`; 364 tokens: S 238, M 126. **Cryptanalytic**: key recovered without a key source,
matched control 99.3%):
> "neiuuer zeittung hab ich itc malsnicht zuberzuschreiben dan das dem printzen zu hispanien seines hern vatters
> schuuester ehlich uermahlet uuerden und hieruber diesse lande zu uuregieren khomen sollen und dan ein gemein
> geschret ist ... uuolle der hertzog uon uandosmen sein konigreich nauarra mit der gute oder krieg uuiederholen
> mrch dunck aber die uueil der konnig zu franckreich noch so iungist es uuerde keinen furgang geuuinnen"

*Of new tidings I have nothing to write except that the Prince of Spain is to marry his father's sister and come to
govern these lands. There is also a common rumour that the Duke of Vendôme will recover his kingdom of Navarre by
agreement or by war. But I think that, the King of France being still so young, it will come to nothing.*

**Already known.** Both rumours are in print from other senders: the 1561 talk of a match between Don Carlos and his
aunt Juana, and Antoine de Bourbon's Navarre claim.

**What the cipher adds.** Orange's own judgement on the Navarre rumour, and the fact that he passed the Spanish
rumours to August in cipher.

**Moment.** Orange married Anna of Saxony, August's niece, at Leipzig in August 1561 *(context for the date)*. The
couple reached Breda on 4 October (Demandt's regest of letter 57). This postscript comes three weeks later, as the new
in-law alliance begins its correspondence. Rumours that Don Carlos would come to govern the Netherlands matter here,
because Orange sat on the Council of State.

**Significance: confirms or adds detail to what the edition gives.** The news is known. What it adds is Orange's
opinion. It is a cryptanalytic reading with 35% M, and the second page (about 90 signs) is not fully read.

rating: confirms or adds detail -- the Spanish and Navarre rumours are printed from other senders; the cipher adds Orange's own judgement (cryptanalytic reading, 35% M)

## ciphers/august-van-saksen-1561-64 WVO 57 -- Elector August to Orange, Torgau, 18 Nov 1561

**Letter.** Elector August of Saxony to Orange, Torgau, 18 Nov 1561. Cipher block on p3. KHA A 11/XIV B/41-6, WVO 57.
The clear letter is summarised in Demandt's regest (*Hessisches Jahrbuch für Landesgeschichte* 38, 1988, nr. 113): the
arrival at Breda, the King of Sweden's English suit, the Öttingen-Schertlin feud. The regest does not mention the
cipher's content.

**Reading** (`reading_57.txt`; 300 tokens: C 247, M 53; key of Orange's 1562 cipher with its contemporary decipherment):
> "auff freundtlich hochuertrawen wollen wir E.L. nitt bergen, das der Keiser fur wenig tagen durch seine stadtliche
> gesanndthenn bei uns suchen lasen, seinen sohn König Maximilianum noch bei seinem des Keisers leben zu einem
> romischen Könige zu erwelen, konnen erachten solchs werde beiden andern Churfursten gleicher gestalt auch gesucht
> werden, welchs E.L. bei sich inn geheim werden zu halten wisenn"

*In friendly confidence we will not hide from Your Grace that a few days ago the Emperor sent a formal embassy to ask
us to elect his son Maximilian King of the Romans in the Emperor's lifetime. We expect the other Electors are being
asked likewise. Your Grace will know to keep this secret.* The reading is coherent German throughout.

**Already known.** Ferdinand I's approach to the Electors in autumn 1561 is printed (Goetz 1891; Kluckhohn I
nr. 148). It led to Maximilian's election at Frankfurt in November 1562.

**What the cipher adds.** That August told Orange, in secret and within days of the imperial embassy, and that he
expected the other Electors to be approached.

**Moment.** The Saxon-Orange marriage alliance three months old *(context)*. August was the leading Lutheran Elector,
and his vote mattered for a Habsburg succession. Confiding it to a Catholic-raised Netherlands grandee shows how he
treated the new in-law.

**Significance: confirms or adds detail to what the edition gives.** The approach is printed. The cipher adds the
confidence and its timing. August's Dresden minute, with a 'Zettel', is unseen and may carry the text in clear.

rating: confirms or adds detail -- the Emperor's approach about Maximilian's election is printed (Goetz 1891); the cipher adds that August confided it to Orange, in secret, within days
