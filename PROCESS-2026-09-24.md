# Process memo, 24 Sept 2026: what the AI-mathematics results justify changing here

Written by the survey worker (claude-fable-5-1, session_013GkMQP9y84gHGG4pSSwaQj) at the end of
`UNSOLVED-SURVEY.md`. Proposals only; nothing below is applied. Each is a concrete diff for the orchestrator or a

## What the results teach, in one paragraph

The Erdős-problem wave (Aletheia's 700-problem evaluation with four autonomous resolutions; Aristotle and GPT-5.2 Pro
on #728 with a Lean proof, arXiv 2601.07421; #1196 with Tao and Lichtman, May 2026; Quanta, 3 Aug 2026) and the
Urquhart solve (Vals AI, 31 Aug 2026, `LESSONS.md`) share seven features. (1) One curated, machine-readable problem
repository. (2) A mechanical judge that lets many attempts be graded without a person. (3) Breadth: many cheap attempts
at a low hit rate rather than one long campaign. (4) Literature first, because the commonest failure was a result
already in print (Erdős #333 had been solved by Erdős in 1977; our N0/N1). (5) Fresh-instance re-derivation of every
claimed result. (6) Problem selection toward formally stated items where the model is strong, away from the famous
ones. (7) Persistence over simple hypotheses (the key is the host text). This repository already has (4), (5) and
(7) as rules 1, 7, 10 and the solver/verifier split. It lacks (1), (2), (3) and (6) as practice: every target folder
has its own layout, no script says pass or fail, campaigns run one target for $60-100 (LANE R5), and the board carries
what a scout scored highest rather than what a cheap first test can move.

## Proposal 1: a breadth lane (LANE B) that runs the cheap first test across every spec at a fixed cap per item

Diff to `CLAUDE.md`, section "Pipeline", after item 3:

```
3a. **Breadth lane.** Before any target gets a campaign (a cap above $10), it gets a spec (`specs/<slug>.json`:
    ciphertext as transcribed with source and date, alphabet, constraints, cheap tests in order, a `judge` block) and
    one Sonnet worker runs its first cheap test at a cap of $3, with the matched control, and writes the two numbers
    into the spec's `cheap_test_done`. A breadth worker takes the next spec whose first test is unrun, never a second
    test on the same spec, and never a campaign. The orchestrator promotes to a campaign only a spec whose first test
    moved it (judge PASS, found-solved, or a control-backed negative that names the next test). Ten first tests at $3
    beat one campaign at $30: the mathematics results resolved 4 of 700 and 9 of 353 by trying everything cheaply.
```

New brief template `.claude/briefs/breadth.md` (copy of the common tail plus): "Spec: `specs/<slug>.json`. Run
`cheap_tests_in_order[0]` and its matched control, nothing else. Write both numbers into `cheap_test_done`. Judge any
candidate with `tools/judge_plaintext.py`; paste its output. Cap $3. Report in five lines; do not run test 2."

## Proposal 2: every claimed reading passes the judge and a fresh-instance re-derivation before it leaves the solver

Diff to `CLAUDE.md`, rule 7 ("Reproducible readings"), append:

```
   A reading on a target that has a spec is reported only with the output of `tools/judge_plaintext.py <spec>
   --file <reading>` pasted into NOTES.md (a FAIL may still be reported, as a FAIL). Before the orchestrator moves
   the target to stage 9, a fresh session that has seen only the spec and the key re-derives the reading with the
   target's decode script and `--check`; a re-derivation that differs by more than the M-graded tokens sends the
   reading back. The judge and the re-derivation are the Lean of this repository: they say "worth a verifier",
   never "right" (rule 10).
```

Diff to `.claude/briefs/solver.md`, end of the reporting paragraph: "Paste the judge output. Do not describe a reading
the judge failed as a reading; describe it as a candidate that failed check X."

## Proposal 3: a problem-selection rule

Diff to `CLAUDE.md`, section "Pipeline", item 3 ("Orchestrator promotes to the board"), append:

```
   Selection rule (24 Sept 2026): rank by expected value = P(the first cheap test moves it) x value / cost, not by
   fame or by scout score alone. Prefer items with a transcription on disk, a formal constraint (a known key family,
   a crib, a host text, a form), a language with a corpus in tools/data, and no published matched-control negative.
   Famous items (Voynich, Kryptos K4, Zodiac Z13/Z32, Beale, Rohonc, Dorabella) enter only with a named untried
   cheap test; `UNSOLVED-SURVEY.md` is the reference ranking for the public list and is re-ranked when a row's
   status changes.
```

## Proposal 4: what to stop doing

- **Stop campaigns without a first cheap test.** LANE R5 spent about $40 on three Nevers leaves before the palaeographic
  blocker was named (ROOM.md, 24 Sept 2026, F1-F3: "sign identification, not the key"). A $3 breadth test (apply the
  key to one pass, judge) would have named it first. Diff: `.claude/briefs/runs/2026-09-24-lane-r5-common.md`, add
  "no leaf is bought before the previous leaf's reading has a judge output".
- **Stop re-transcribing when the judge would fail anyway.** Two passes at 37-54% agreement (R5 F1-F3) cannot pass a
  language gate; a third pass does not help. Diff to `.claude/briefs/transcription.md`: "if pass agreement is under
  60%, stop and report the blocker; the next step is a glyph atlas, not a third pass."
- **Stop scoring famous items into the queue by fame.** `QUEUE.md`'s dropped table already says so for most of them;
  `UNSOLVED-SURVEY.md` gives the ranking with the reason per row. Diff to `.claude/briefs/scout.md`: "a Top-50 item
  enters the queue only with its survey rank and the named cheap test."
- **Stop reporting a negative without both numbers in the same line** (already rule 3; still missing in most
  ROOM.md done lines). Diff to the common tail: "a negative's done line carries target and control numbers side by
  side, or it is not a negative."

## Proposal 5 (owner's decision): fund the breadth lane from the campaign budget, not on top of it

Ten breadth workers at $3 is $30. The survey's top ten specs are ready for that today (ranks 1, 3, 6, 7, 8, 9, 10 have
their ciphertext on disk; ranks 2, 4, 5 need one image fetch each first). The recommendation is to take the $30 from the
next campaign cap rather than add it, because the mathematics results say the win rate per attempt is low (about 1-3
percent) and the wins came from breadth, and because a campaign that starts without a first test is the pattern the
ledger already shows failing (R5, solvEX). This changes the spend, so it is the owner's call.

## What is not proposed

No change to rules 1, 3, 6, 9, 10 or to the verifier brief: those are the parts the mathematics results confirm. No
change to the good-citizen rule: breadth workers hit no host at all when the ciphertext is in the spec.
