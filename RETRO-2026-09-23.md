## 4. The owner's question: are we burning tokens on one pattern?

**For.** On 23 Sept 15 of 25 workers ran the same shape (scout a catalogue by keyword, then check-solved in batches
of three or four), across five lanes in about five hours. Each lane was announced as "the lane that is ours" and each
returned mostly solved or blocked material: DECODE neighbours 97.5% named by Bourdeau; non-DECODE 7 of 15 dropped, the
other 7 all copy orders; digitised 4 of 7 dropped. Of about 64 items checked since 19 Sept, 39% were already solved or
being read, and only 3 (5%) are open and copy-free today. The stage-2 targets that do survive are mostly copy orders,
which the goal block itself rates at "one solve a month at best", so more sweeping of those lanes adds rows to the
owner's card (ASKS 12-16: 9 targets on 5 rows, plus 9 board cards at stage 2 "you") rather than solves. Zero unique
solves on 23 Sept, and the only two to date came from a lane the scouts later dropped.

**Against.** The sweeps are not where the tokens went. Check-solved cost $1.0 per verdict and scouts $3.20 each: $41
of $105.5 for 30 verdicts and about 1,220 records screened. The expensive items were three Opus solvers and one Opus
verifier ($54, 51% of the day), two of whose targets were chosen from the stale 19 Sept queue, and above all the
orchestrator ($2,042 in four days against $330 of workers). Check-solved also paid for itself: each found-solved drop
before transcription saves $15-40 (Randolph's transcription was started beside its sibling sweep and stopped at $3; the
full job would have been about $40). And the copy-free digitised lane is yielding 3 of 7, far better than the 0 of 15
for the copy-order lanes.

**Verdict.** Partly yes. The pattern is sound as a filter; what repeats wastefully is (a) opening further copy-order
lanes while the owner's card is already full, (b) spending the strongest model on targets whose best case is below N3,
and (c) never testing a route that does not start from a catalogue's word for cipher. LESSONS §1 says the largest
single route to the September solves was a printed ciphertext nobody had applied a key to, and not one of our 23 Sept
lanes looked at printed ciphertext.

### Hypotheses with a one-day test

**A. Printed ciphertext in Internet Archive full text (recommended first).** 17th-19th-century documentary editions
print cipher passages as figures inside clear letters, sometimes with a decipherment and sometimes without. A script
marks OCR lines where most tokens are 1-4 digit numerals, clusters them, and keeps clusters with a high repeat rate
(ciphers repeat values, page indexes rarely do) and clear prose beside them. Probe on Thurloe vol. 1
(`collectionofstat01thur`, 73,241 OCR lines): 111 clusters; with repeat rate 0.3 or more, 15 or more numerals and
5 or more prose words beside them, 10 clusters, every one of which reads at a glance as cipher figures inside a
letter (e.g. "at present here 7. 7. 17. 24. 7. 6 ...", line 43,962). Not checked against the page, and Aymeloglu has
already worked Vande Perre's letters in this volume, so these are the positive control, not candidates. Page-level
numeral share from HTRC Extracted Features is too diluted to use (CSP Scotland vi: no page above 12% numerals), so the
unit is the OCR line, not the EF page.
Test: 300 public-domain editions (Thurloe 1-7, Nicholas, Clarendon State Papers, Carte, Birch, Winwood, Sawyer, HMC
reports, Lettres missives de Henri IV, Avenel, Négociations, Archives de la maison d'Orange-Nassau, CODOIN, Politische
Correspondenz, the Catinat Mémoires 1819 and Rommel 1840 as controls). Cost: script and fetch about 1 GB at 1.5 s per
item, then one Sonnet session to judge about 300 clusters; about $5-8 in all. Success: the two controls and the Thurloe
clusters are recovered, and at least 10 clusters are printed ciphertext with no decipherment printed beside them that
none of Bourdeau's folders, Aymeloglu's catalogues or Tomokiyo's pages name. Failure: fewer than 3 such clusters, or
precision below 1 in 5 in the top 50.

**B. Gallica volumes swept by image, not by description.** Bourdeau's whole-volume habit found unlisted cipher leaves
(Ségur, fr. 16127). Build 4-up contact sheets from IIIF thumbnails of ambassadorial volumes (Dupuy, Clairambault,
Cinq Cents de Colbert) and let Sonnet flag leaves with numeral or symbol runs. Controls: the five leaves we already
know (Dupuy 452 f.20, Dupuy 468 f.28, Lorraine 377 ff.95-96, Dupuy 155 item 41, Dupuy 111 about f.34). Cost: about
120k image tokens per 300-leaf volume, 20 volumes, about $8-12 and 2.5 h of polite fetching. Success: at least 4 of the
5 controls flagged and at least 3 cipher leaves the finding aid does not mention. Failure: fewer than 3 controls
flagged (thumbnails too small) or nothing outside the catalogue.

**C. Discovery's non-TNA holdings.** County record offices and university libraries catalogued in Discovery
(`heldBy` other than TNA) hold diplomats' family papers. Cost about $3, one hour. Success: 3 or more items with a key
in the same collection, not named by either repo. Caution: almost certainly a copy-order lane, so it runs only if
proposal 2's card cap allows.

**D. A result the owner might also accept (for the person).** Keep unique solves as the goal, but report two leading
counts beside it so a day like 23 Sept is visible as progress or waste: copy-free targets at stage 2, and contributions
handed on. 23 Sept produced at least six catalogue corrections nobody has sent anywhere (N8 date, N11 miscatalogued
items, M6 "nine" is two, Randolph's DECODE key pointer R4930, N4 conflation, the Bowes sign table for Tomokiyo);
batching them to TNA, BnF, DECODE and Tomokiyo costs about $2.

### 5. One-day test of the printed-ciphertext detector (for the person: moves a day's spend to an untried lane)

Motivated by: LESSONS §1 (printed ciphertext plus an unapplied key is the largest solve route), 23 Sept's five
keyword lanes with 0 unique solves, and the Thurloe vol. 1 probe in section 4. New brief and new tool:

```diff
--- /dev/null
+++ b/.claude/briefs/detector-test.md
+# Detector test: printed ciphertext in Internet Archive full text (Sonnet, cap $8, one day)
+Read CLAUDE.md (rules 1, 3, 10; Access playbook good-citizen rule) and RETRO-2026-09-23.md section 4 A.
+Build the list of public-domain editions named there (identifiers from archive.org advancedsearch, one call per
+title). Run `tools/ia_numeral_runs.py` over all of them into sources/ia-fulltext/runs.tsv. Controls first: Thurloe
+vol. 1, the 1819 Catinat Mémoires and Rommel 1840 must show their printed cipher; if not, stop and report. Keep
+clusters with repeat_rate >= 0.3, numerals >= 15, prose_words >= 5; judge each from its context line only, marking
+cipher-with-decipherment, cipher-without-decipherment, table, noise. For each cipher-without-decipherment cluster,
+grep sources/cryptiana/, and fresh clones of both solver repositories, for the edition and the letter's date. Write
+QUEUE.md section "Printed ciphertext (detector test of <date>)" with one row per surviving cluster; report
+precision in the top 50, control recall, and survivors. Never promote, never solve. + common tail.
--- /dev/null
+++ b/tools/ia_numeral_runs.py
+#!/usr/bin/env python3
+"""Find runs of numerals inside otherwise clear printed text, in Internet Archive full text (_djvu.txt).
+
+  python3 tools/ia_numeral_runs.py IDENTIFIER [...] [--cache DIR] [--min-tokens 6] [--share 0.7] [--tsv OUT]
+
+Downloads IDENTIFIER_djvu.txt once per item (cached), marks lines with at least --min-tokens tokens of which at
+least --share are 1-4 digit numerals, groups marked lines fewer than four lines apart, and scores each cluster:
+numerals, distinct values, repeat rate (cipher repeats; index and table columns mostly do not) and prose words of
+4+ letters within three lines either side. One TSV row per cluster with 240 characters of context. Scripts read,
+models judge. Lending-only items answer 403 and are reported, not retried. Exit 0 always.
+"""
+import argparse, os, re, sys, time, urllib.request, urllib.error
+
+NUM = re.compile(r'^\d{1,4}[.,;:]?$')
+WORD = re.compile(r'^[A-Za-zÀ-ſ]{4,}[.,;:]?$')
+
+def fetch(ident, cache):
+    path = os.path.join(cache, ident + '_djvu.txt')
+    if os.path.exists(path):
+        return open(path, encoding='utf-8', errors='ignore').read(), 'cached'
+    url = 'https://archive.org/download/%s/%s_djvu.txt' % (ident, ident)
+    req = urllib.request.Request(url, headers={'User-Agent': 'Mozilla/5.0 (cipher-lab ia_numeral_runs)'})
+    try:
+        with urllib.request.urlopen(req, timeout=120) as r:
+            text = r.read().decode('utf-8', errors='ignore')
+    except urllib.error.HTTPError as e:
+        return None, 'HTTP %d' % e.code
+    os.makedirs(cache, exist_ok=True)
+    open(path, 'w', encoding='utf-8').write(text)
+    time.sleep(1.5)
+    return text, 'fetched'
+
+def clusters(lines, min_tokens, share):
+    out = []
+    for i, l in enumerate(lines):
+        tk = l.split()
+        if len(tk) >= min_tokens and sum(1 for t in tk if NUM.match(t)) / len(tk) >= share:
+            if out and i - out[-1][-1] < 4:
+                out[-1].append(i)
+            else:
+                out.append([i])
+    return out
+
+def score(lines, c):
+    nums = [t.rstrip('.,;:') for i in range(c[0], c[-1] + 1) for t in lines[i].split() if NUM.match(t)]
+    seen, rep = set(), 0
+    for n in nums:
+        rep += n in seen
+        seen.add(n)
+    ctx = [lines[i] for i in range(max(0, c[0] - 3), min(len(lines), c[-1] + 4)) if i not in c]
+    prose = sum(1 for l in ctx for t in l.split() if WORD.match(t))
+    return len(nums), len(seen), rep / len(nums) if nums else 0.0, prose
+
+def main():
+    ap = argparse.ArgumentParser()
+    ap.add_argument('ids', nargs='+')
+    ap.add_argument('--cache', default='sources/ia-fulltext')
+    ap.add_argument('--min-tokens', type=int, default=6)
+    ap.add_argument('--share', type=float, default=0.7)
+    ap.add_argument('--tsv', default='-')
+    a = ap.parse_args()
+    out = sys.stdout if a.tsv == '-' else open(a.tsv, 'w', encoding='utf-8')
+    out.write('identifier\tline\tn_lines\tnumerals\tdistinct\trepeat_rate\tprose_words\tcontext\n')
+    for ident in a.ids:
+        text, how = fetch(ident, a.cache)
+        if text is None:
+            print('%s: %s, skipped' % (ident, how), file=sys.stderr)
+            continue
+        lines = text.split('\n')
+        cs = clusters(lines, a.min_tokens, a.share)
+        print('%s: %s, %d lines, %d clusters' % (ident, how, len(lines), len(cs)), file=sys.stderr)
+        for c in cs:
+            n, d, r, p = score(lines, c)
+            ctx = ' / '.join(lines[i].strip() for i in range(max(0, c[0] - 1), min(len(lines), c[-1] + 2)))
+            out.write('%s\t%d\t%d\t%d\t%d\t%.2f\t%d\t%s\n' % (ident, c[0] + 1, len(c), n, d, r, p,
+                                                             ctx[:240].replace('\t', ' ')))
+
+if __name__ == '__main__':
+    main()
```
