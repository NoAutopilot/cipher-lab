Frederick I of Prussia's Transposition Cipher

BL Add MS 61575 contains some specimens of transposition cipher.

Frederick I of Prussia (1)

F.91-92 (DECODE R8772) is a letter signed "Frederic R." (King Frederick I of Prussia) and the name "Il Capitaneo Moyse Ceffis" in the endorsement is probably the recipient.

2-7-8
cel cier Ray en suf je sou le en ne me fer se hayt cy cor que mon et roys ant je eu vous trant vous tous un vient ne aves des as jours bon vous foy eue crie seu recog voya re de avec re re naissent ge par mer la moy en que vous
adieu cher capitaneo Moyse Ceffis et sourene vous tousjour de un Roy quil a affection pour vous
Frederic R.

This is in a transposition cipher and its reading is shown in f.95 (R8774).

From this, the key "2-7-8" at the beginning of the ciphertext may indicate that the decipherer should fill a matrix of 7 rows and 8 columns, vertically column by column, starting from the 2nd column.

But the next piece indicates the meaning of the first digit "2" may be more complex.

Frederick I of Prussia (2)

F.93-94 (R8773) is also a letter from King Frederick of Prussia, endorsed "Pour le Seignoro Capitano Moyse Ceffis".

3-14-8
neo es ren coup a ette plein cy que hait cor hait seure recog que la criet deue de he jes je je te eune te que nais vous vos de et ple reu vos vous songe fort foy eun je sent maves tre Made je sier se tre peu souvent de pen hereu sere pour donne ta ves biens bau ge vous di rer sou en sou as vie fer pi ma ette avec voya que ce seu je voir vous vous ma suf Ca vous ma pries vostre mies sen as et res je et te bau cher que bourg ap que or ab biens a vous vous dant voyage tou le et suis
Vostre bon Roy
Frederic

Although the key "3-14-8" is written at the beginning, if the key is applied with the interpretation above, the resulting text does not make sense. However, connection of some syllables is obvious (e.g., "sou-hait", "bau-coup", "as-seure", "recog-nais-sent", "pen-dant", "ap-pries", "voya-ge") and the correct arrangement of the columns can be found without much trouble. (Some adaptation was required because of some cells encompassing two segments ("a ette", "a vous").)

As can be seen, "3" in the key does not simply mean starting from the 3rd column. It may have been a preconcerted code number (or actually a graphical symbol looking like "3") specifying the column order.

The plaintext begins with "Cher Capitaneo", addressing the recipient. The last two words "et suis" do not fit in the transposition matrix, but this should be part of the complimentary ending "et suis Vostre bon Roy, Frederic".

Report to Queen Anne?

F.88-90 (R8771) include another piece of transposition cipher in Italian, related to "Anna Regina D'Ingilterra", (plus another simple cipher).

Sacra M'ta di ANNA Regina D'Ingilterra &
30 - 49 - 71, questo no e la chiave per descifrare.

Ces ue à sua pon in mano le li ce tra ser ne nis al ta aria nir in di dersi sie del co se sa mae iue gotij tri Tro Ber mi qui ser ga li me li se cre ria sta ra che sap no tani ha da gnar bi ast sen se che tari sap con all non pia di ca ta di esta fra coris ti per solo che sta uos ra li mi chino es es es dato ma ci per cre sar pero le mae de cif ator li in ma ma man tra la to so pas tari uo sua on ta per che m' tra sua com uos li net ari za cre non di ino ques im uol et uos
Di V'ra Mta

Vmilis[si]mo: Deuotis[si]mo: Ossequios[si]mo sere fedelle
Il Capitano Moyse Ceffis, secretario della cifra di Cabinetto di S.M'ta Imperiale

This seems to be from Captain Moyse Ceffis, secretary of cipher of the "Cabinetto di S.M'ta Imperiale", to Queen Anne.

Although "30-49-71" is said to be the key ("chiave"), it cannot be applied in the way of King Frederick's cipher. The number of segments in the ciphertext is 129, which may, if I included an extra space, actually be 128=27. So, the size of the matrix may be 16*8 or the like. (As it turned out, I typed an extra "es", and the actual number is 128.)

F.88 says "Mando a V.Eza[?] questa lettera scritta in cifra accio neda[?] la qualita della cifra di Cabinetto di sua maesta Cesaria". From this, this seems to be a specimen of an Imperial cipher provided to the Queen (or actually her decipherer, Blencowe).
