I am checking whether four sixteenth-century cipher letters have ever been printed or deciphered. Please answer
only with exact citations: author, title, volume, year, and page or letter number. Say plainly when you do not
know. Do not guess.

The letters are from Count Louis (Lodewijk) of Nassau to his brother William of Orange. They are originals in
the Koninklijk Huisarchief, The Hague, shelfmark A 11/XIV D/13a, and are numbered 4610, 4611, 4612 and 4616 in
the Huygens ING database "Briefwisseling van Willem van Oranje". All four are mainly in a numeral cipher, with
French words written in clear between the numbers.

- 4610, 3 June 1573, duplicate, addressed "Pour Hollande". It opens: "Il fault que me pardonnez de ce que ne vous
  ay depesché ce message le lendemain".
- 4611, 2 July 1573.
- 4612, 6 March 1574, from Meer. It opens: "J'é receu hier vostre lettre du xxime de febvrier, et veu ce que
  mandez de la".
- 4616, 12 April 1574, from Weeze near Goch, two days before the battle of Mookerheyde. It opens: "Nous sommes
  cest soer icy arivé aupres de Goch et sommes".

Already checked, and none of the four found in them: Groen van Prinsterer, Archives de la maison d'Orange-Nassau,
1st series t. III-V and the Supplément; Gachard, Correspondance de Guillaume le Taciturne t. III and
Correspondance de Philippe II t. II-III; Kervyn de Lettenhove, Les Huguenots et les Gueux t. III; Blok, Lodewijk
van Nassau (1889). Groen prints William's replies to 4610, 4611 and 4616.
