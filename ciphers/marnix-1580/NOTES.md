# Filips van Marnix van St. Aldegonde to Willem van Oranje, 17 December 1580

QUEUE row: WV4 (`QUEUE.md`, "Willem van Oranje correspondence: unsolved cipher letters (LANE N harvest of 24
September 2026)"). Brief `.claude/briefs/runs/2026-09-24-lane-n-csWV.md`.

## Source

WVO briefnr **10260** (https://resources.huygens.knaw.nl/wvo/app/brief?nr=10260), 17 December 1580, from
Courtras (Coutras), Filips van Marnix van St. Aldegonde to Willem van Oranje: news of the peace negotiations in
France (the Treaty of Fleix) and troop movements. WVO's Opmerkingen field, read directly: "De brief is
vercijferd geweest. Met een postscriptum. De contemporaine kopie Stadsarchief Gent heeft alleen het
postscriptum. De edities Groen van Prinsterer, Archives d'Orange-Nassau en Lacroix, Correspondance zonder
postscriptum." (The letter WAS enciphered. With a postscript. The contemporary copy at Stadsarchief Gent has
only the postscript. The editions Groen van Prinsterer and Lacroix, Correspondance, are without the postscript.)
No "onopgelost"/"oplossing"/"ontcijferd" word for the main letter -- it describes the manuscript's cipher status
in the past tense ("vercijferd geweest") without saying whether or how it was resolved.

**WVO's own Brongegevens (source/edition) field lists four items for this one letter, all fetched and read
directly this pass:**

1. Het Utrechts Archief, Staten van Utrecht 704-1, pp.343-350, "kopie contemporaine" (contemporary copy), with
   a free PDF (`resources.huygens.knaw.nl/media/wvo/images/10000-10999/10260.pdf`, 19.9 MB, 8 pages).
2. Groen van Prinsterer, *Archives d'Orange-Nassau*, 1re série, **VII, 461-463, no. MXIII (onv)** -- "(onv)" =
   onvolledig, incomplete, the same flag pattern documented for WV1/WV2/`ciphers/la-garde-1577/`.
3. Muller, P.L., "Brieven van prins Willem I en van zijne derde vrouw, Charlotte van Bourbon, aan haren broeder,
   François de Bourbon, prins-dauphin van Auvergne", *Bijdragen en Mededeelingen van het Historisch Genootschap*
   11 (1888), 509-520, with a free PDF of the relevant pages
   (`resources.huygens.knaw.nl/media/wvo/images/edities/MBWI/10260_ed.pdf`, 12 pp., 910 KB).
4. Gerlo, Aloïs, and Rudolf De Smet, eds., *Marnixi Epistulae. De briefwisseling van Marnix van Sint-Aldegonde.
   Een kritische uitgave*, 3 dln. (Brussel 1990-1996) -- the modern standard critical edition of Marnix's
   correspondence, with a free PDF of the relevant pages
   (`resources.huygens.knaw.nl/media/wvo/images/edities/GSME/10260_ed.pdf`, 9 pp., 621 KB).

## What the sources actually show

**The "contemporary copy" fetched and viewed (`images/10260_p1.png`, `wvo_10260_original.pdf`) is not the
original ciphered dispatch -- it is itself a contemporary DECIPHERED extract.** Its heading reads (own
transcription, 16th-c. Dutch/French mixed hand): "Extract wenden [den] xv.en der maent Januarij 1581. Sijn
gelesen de overgesette brieven vanden heer van St. Aldegonde, insonderde het succes vande pr[i]jsse in
Vranckrijck, en oock het secours van den hertoghe van Anjou tot onderstant van dese landen versocht [...].
Continuerende het geheel werck de cijffers --" (Extract, read on 15 January 1581: the *translated* letters from
the lord of St. Aldegonde ... continuing/resuming [after] the whole cipher-work). The body then runs in
continuous plain French ("Monseigneur. Ma derniere fut par monsr de Villers...") -- word-for-word the same
opening later printed by Muller 1888 (below). This is a States-of-Utrecht clerk's contemporary transcript of
the *already-deciphered* text, made within a month of the letter, not the enciphered original as sent.

**Muller's 1888 edition** (`fulltext_10260.txt` extracted from the PDF, not committed -- see Requests) prints
the letter in full as "DLXVI, 17 décembre 1580, Marnix au prince d'Orange, Coutras", continuous French prose
matching the Utrecht extract, with a footnote at the top: "1) Dépêche chiffrée. Voyez la lettre précédente."
(Ciphered dispatch. See the previous letter.) -- i.e. Muller himself flags that the *original* dispatch was in
cipher, and prints its already-deciphered content regardless.

**Gerlo & De Smet's modern critical edition** (`Marnixi Epistulae`, no. 181, "80 12 17 O¹") gives the letter a
full critical apparatus with three manuscript witnesses (c1 Algemeen Rijksarchief Staten-Generaal 11.099 f.108r-
109v; c2 = the Utrecht extract above; c3 = Stadsarchief Gent, postscript only) plus Muller-Diegerick's print (d),
and a Dutch regest. No witness listed is the original numeral cipher; all are contemporary copies or later
transcripts of the deciphered text.

## Verdict

**Status: found-solved (F0).** The plaintext of this letter has been in continuous print since 1888 (Muller,
*Bijdragen en Mededeelingen*), reprinted with full critical apparatus in the modern standard edition of
Marnix's correspondence (Gerlo & De Smet 1990-96), and the WVO database -- the specialist database for this
very correspondence -- already links the manuscript to both editions in its own Brongegevens field. Per
README's F-grade vocabulary, this is **F0**: the specialist database already links the item to its print; no
contribution. The QUEUE.md WV4 row's characterisation of this as an open recovery candidate (on the strength of
Groen's edition being flagged "(onv)" incomplete) did not follow through to the *other* two editions WVO's own
record cites for the same letter, which do carry the full deciphered text.

**No original ciphertext located.** What survives and is digitised (the Utrecht "kopie contemporaine") is
itself a contemporary decipherment, not the enciphered dispatch as sent; no numeral-cipher manuscript for this
specific letter was found in any of the four cited sources. This target is not a candidate for solving even
setting the found-solved verdict aside -- there is no ciphertext on hand to read.
