## Editions-first check (24 September 2026)

**HMC Salisbury (Cecil Papers, Hatfield) volume 6** (`archive.org/details/calendarofmanusc06grea`, 1895,
covers 1596). Fetched the full `_djvu.txt` to disk (one request, 2.5 MB) and grepped for "Wroth": two
mentions, neither the target letter — "often both I and this bearer, Mr. Wroth, have spoken and sent for it"
(Wroth named as the bearer of someone else's letter, not the sender) and its matching index entry. No cipher
mentioned near either. This volume does not calendar the Wroth-to-Cecil cipher letter.

**CSP Domestic, Elizabeth, 1595-1597** (ed. Mary Anne Everett Green, 1869;
`archive.org/details/sim_great-britain-public-record-mary-elizabeth-i-and-james-i_1595-1597`, not access-
restricted, full `_djvu.txt` fetched to disk, one request, 2.35 MB). Two Wroth entries found, both useful
context but **neither is the cipher letter at SP 81/7/239 and neither is calendared as deciphered**:

> A Signet Office docquet: "Warrant to pay to John Wroth, the Queen's servant, 70l. for his charges when the
> Earl of Lincoln was sent to the Landgrave of Hesse, at which time he was sent on the Queen's special
> service to the Count Palatine, and other princes of Germany." [Docquet, bis.]
>
> No. 65, Hen. Wotton to Sec. Cecil: "I hope in this employment not to be disgraced by great inequalities
> between myself and Mr. Wroth... I enclose a note of the princes to whom I desire to be sent, leaving to
> Wroth the highest person and those next him in that part of Germany." Enclosure 65.1, "List of German
> princes for Mr. Wroth: the Emperor, Dukes of Saxony, Pomerania, Brunswick, and the rest in those Eastern
> parts. For Mr. Wotton: the Palsgrave, Landgrave, Catholic Electors, Duke of Wirtemberg, and the rest of
> the Western princes."

These two entries establish Wroth's mission (a 1596-97 embassy to the German princes east of the
Palatinate, alongside Henry Wotton's parallel mission to the western princes) but are Signet Office/Domestic
business about the appointment, not the Foreign-series letter itself; the actual SP 81/7/239 letter (a
report from Wroth to Cecil, carrying a cipher key) is not calendared or quoted in this Domestic volume. No
sentence in either edition identifies, quotes, or describes the content of SP 81/7/239 specifically — the
condition in CLAUDE.md rule 10 and the check-solved brief for withholding "open" is not met here, unlike
sp81-palavicino-1590 in this same batch.

The "List and Analysis of State Papers, Foreign Series" (R.B. Wernham), the modern calendar that should
cover 1596 German-States material properly, was searched for on Internet Archive by title: not found
digitised there (consistent with the sp81-palavicino-1590 finding in this batch — Wernham's volumes are
likely still in copyright and not on IA). Not checked further this pass.

## Verdict

**open**, stage 2 verified unsolved (conditional on the two named editions checked above; List and Analysis
and Google Books outstanding). No source in this sweep identifies, quotes, or describes the content of SP
81/7/239 itself. Wroth's 1596-97 mission and his division of German princes with Henry Wotton are
established from Domestic-series docquet/enclosure material (M-grade context, not a reading of the cipher
letter), which is useful background for anyone who later works this target (an image, once ordered, should
be checked against Wotton's parallel dispatches from the same embassy for a possible sibling decipherment,
per LESSONS.md's "look for the sibling" pattern) but does not itself amount to a prior reading.
