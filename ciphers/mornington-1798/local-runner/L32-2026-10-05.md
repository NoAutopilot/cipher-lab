## Answer

Read in the owner's desk browser on 4-5 Oct 2026 (UTC), in the archive.org BookReader only. The item twoviewsofbritis0000melv (Ingram ed., *Two Views of British India*, Bath: Adams & Dart, 1970) was borrowed for one hour and returned when done; the page then showed "Borrow" again. No download. Page numbers below are the printed ones. The book is in copyright, so only headnotes, footnotes and the enclosure/memorandum sentences are quoted; the rest is summarised.

### (1) Private letter no. 6, 6 July 1798: text known, printed in Ingram 1970 pp. 51-63

- Headnote (p. 51): "VIII The Earl of Mornington to the Rt Hon. Henry Dundas" / "6 July 1798, Fort William Private: no. 6"
- Opening (pp. 51-52): "With my letter no. 5 dispatched overland I transmitted to you a copy of the proclamation issued by the governor of the Isle of France so long ago as the month of February last." This is the same opening as Martin 1836 Vol. 1 No. XXII p. 80 (our copy D623-5_candidate_martin1836_v1_p80.txt).
- Ending (p. 63): "...were to produce an effort in his favour from France. / Believe me,... / M[ORNINGTON]"
- **Comparison with Martin.** It is the same letter, and Martin's version is abridged. Martin ends at "...the only effectual barrier against the irruption of France into India would be our possession of the whole of Tippoo's maritime territory. Iam, &c. MorNINGTON." Ingram reaches that point and then goes on for several more paragraphs on the Mauritius and the colonial assembly before closing. (Opening and closing words compared; the full text was not collated line by line.)
- **Footnotes seen in the letter.** p. 55: "1 Wellesley, i, 59; this letter was not sent." The OCR does not show where the marker sits. It stands in the passage where Mornington says he has called on Tipu for a public disavowal and has written to Poona and Hyderabad, so it appears to annotate the letter or remonstrance to Tipu (printed in Wellesley, *Despatches*, i, 59). (The p. 51 footnote on Hyacinthe Gabrielle Roland belongs to the preceding item VII.)
- **Cipher:** not found. Neither the headnote nor any footnote seen says that this letter, or the D623/4-5 despatch, went in cipher.

### (2) Private letter no. 15 [recte 16], 7 June 1799: text known, printed in Ingram 1970 pp. 156-163

- Headnote (p. 156): "XLIII The Earl of Mornington to the Rt Hon. Henry Dundas" / "7 June 1799, Fort St George Private: no. 15 [recte 16]"
- Opening (p. 156): "My separate dispatch of this date to the court of directors will apprise you of the events which have happened in Mysore since the date of my last private letter (no. 14), the postscript of which was closed on the 18th of May. Nothing can be more favourable than the state of affairs in that quarter."
- Martin Vol. 2 No. XV p. 35 opens "Nothing can be more favourable than the state of affairs in Mysore". Ingram's second sentence is the same, with "in that quarter".
- Ending (p. 163): "...and who are afraid of their own strength. / Believe me, . . / MORNINGTON". This is the same closing as Martin ("afraid of their own strength. Believe me, my dear Sir, ever yours, ... MorninoTon.").
- **Comparison with Martin.** It is the same letter. Martin leaves out at least the opening sentence, and Ingram adds a PS and an N.B. that our Martin copy does not have. The full text was not collated.
- **Enclosure and memorandum sentences:**
  - p. 157: "I enclose two papers, no. 1 an account of the revenues of the late Tipu Sultan for the year 1797/8, taken from authentic records found in the palace at Seringapatam, and explained by Purneah Dewan to the late Sultan. No. 2 is a memorandum containing the general heads of the settlement which I propose to carry into effect without delay. I do not transmit this paper to you as perfectly accurate in all its details, but merely as being sufficient to furnish you with a general view of my plan."
  - p. 161: "I transmit to you by this dispatch a set of the French papers found in the palace at Seringapatam."
  - PS, p. 163: "PS. The account of revenue no. 1 has been found to be inaccurate. I do not, therefore, send it. In examining the paper marked no. 2, you will observe, that the rajah of Mysore's portion is not specified. It consists of whatever is not ceded to the nizam, the peshwa, or the English. I perceive that I have omitted to mention in its proper place, that a munificent allowance for Tipu Sultan's family and for all his principal sirdars will form a leading feature of the intended settlement. M."
  - N.B., p. 163: "N.B. By a mistake, two private letters numbered 12 have been sent to you. This is therefore 76, though numbered 15." The "76" is the OCR's reading, presumably of "16".
- **Is the memorandum printed?** No. Ingram does not print it, and the text layer showed no footnote to the letter about it. p. 164 begins the next item, "XLIV The Rt Hon. Henry Dundas to the Earl of Mornington / 14 June 1799, Wimbledon Private: no. 13".
- **Cipher:** not found. No headnote or note seen says the letter or the memorandum (D623/24) went in cipher.

### (3) The page with the footnotes "1 On 21 June 1800." and "2 Add. MSS. 13751, f. 77. Wellesley, ii, 311": p. 279

These footnotes were found with search inside for "13751", which gave 2 hits: p. 266 ("1 Qn 9 May 1800. Add. MSS. 13751, f. 48.") and p. 279.

The two footnotes belong to the end of the letter on p. 279, not to the letter that starts below it.

- **The letter they belong to:**
  - Headnote (p. 277): "LXXXVI The Marquis Wellesley to the Rt Hon. Henry Dundas" / "13 July 1800, Fort William Private: no. 29 (Secret)".
  - Opening: "My private and secret letter no. 28 of this date will prove to you the great difficulty, amounting nearly to an impossibility, of our making any effort from India, on an extended scale, to dislodge the French from Egypt."
  - It ends on p. 279 with "I remain, . . . / WELLESLEY".
- **The letter that follows on the same page:** "LXXXVII The Rt Hon. Henry Dundas to the Marquis Wellesley" / "15 July 1800, Wimbledon Private: no. 28". Its opening page ends "...that the whole of the Company's European infantry ought". This is the body text named in the row; it has no footnote marker.
- **Footnote 1** (the marker is on p. 279, line 2, as seen on the page image) annotates: "...I have deemed it an indispensable duty to suspend for the present all preparations for the expedition against Batavia.¹" The note "1 On 21 June 1800." gives the date of that suspension.
- **Footnote 2** annotates: "I enclose for your information extracts from my letters to Admiral Rainier signifying my determination on this subject.²" The note is "2 Add. MSS. 13751, f. 77. Wellesley, ii, 311."
- **Other footnotes in LXXXVI** (p. 277-278), for context:
  - "1 Memorandum by Stuart for Wellesley, 6 July 1799, W.O. 1/357; f. 319."
  - "1 John Philip Morier; private secretary to Elgin; on 22 December 1799 sent on a mission to the camp of the grand vizier. Vide Add. MSS. 13791, f. 46."
  - "2 Add. MSS. 37282, f. 214." This is on the enclosed extracts of letters to Lord Elgin and Sir Sidney Smith.
- **Cipher:** not found in LXXXVI's headnote or footnotes.
- **Cross-reference (an observation, not a conclusion).** The NRS has GD51/3/101, "Extract from a coded letter. [Enclosure in letter, 13 July, 1800, from Lord Wellesley]", dated 21 Jun 1800 (see row L34). Ingram's letter LXXXVI is Wellesley's letter of 13 July 1800. Its footnote 1 dates the Batavia suspension to 21 June 1800, and it encloses extracts of letters to Rainier (footnote 2) and to Elgin and Smith. The GD51/3/101 extract may be one of those enclosures. This was not checked: Ingram does not print the enclosures, and the NRS record does not name the addressee.
