# Leads, not verdicts

## 1. Known keys, codebooks, or personal shorthand

- The FBI's 2011 account says family members reported coded notes from childhood and that investigators wanted another sample of his system for comparison. Christopher Tritto's direct 2012 interviews instead record his mother and cousin denying that he wrote in code, while CRRU chief Dan Olson remained confident he wrote these notes. This is an evidentiary conflict, not a basis to assume a particular key. Highest-value lead: locate authenticated ordinary handwriting or earlier notes and ask whether the letter forms, spellings, repeated groups, and circled items match. The FBI's 2009 questioned-document report in the released Vault packet says no indented writing was found on the submitted note pages; that is not a handwriting-authorship finding. ## 4. What to try first

1. **First recover independent evidence of the writer's habits, if available.** Compare authenticated mundane writing and any earlier patterned notes, with blind handwriting assessment and a transcription audit of both photographed pages. The contradictory family/FBI accounts and the FBI's request for another sample make this more discriminating than choosing a generic shorthand lexicon. If no comparison material can be obtained, record that limitation. [FBI, "Cryptanalysts: Help Solve an Open Murder Case" (2011), p. 26, URL above; Tritto, "Code Dead" (2012), URL above.]
2. **Run a constrained structural test on the observed groups**, preserving spacing, punctuation, numbers, brackets and spelling variants. Compare, for example, a shared-stem model for NCBE/WLDNCBE/WLD'S NCBE and the -RSE family against shuffled-order and frequency-matched nulls. Pre-register a small set of predictions and require held-out lines to fit. Pelling's token observations and Scott's abstract motivate this experiment, but neither supplies a verified key. [Pelling, "Nick's 2024 thoughts" (2024), "Ricky McCormick's notes," URL above; Scott (Dunn), "Beyond Cryptography" (2026), abstract, URL above.]
3. **Calibrate the token annealer before interpreting its output.** The repository's 132-token control recovered 0.8% of its own mapping, so vary synthetic length, number of singleton types, recurrent-token fraction, and lexicon size until a matched control passes a pre-set recovery gate. A longer, easier control by itself cannot validate inference at the actual 132-token shape; use a control that reproduces that shape or report the method as underpowered. [NoAutopilot/cipher-lab, "NOTES.md," cheap test 4 (2026), https://github.com/NoAutopilot/cipher-lab/blob/main/ciphers/mccormick-1999/NOTES.md.]
4. Only then test a narrow abbreviation lexicon built from **documented** writing habits or independently sourced 1999 local context. Gregg-specific or street-code expansions without such evidence are **unverified**. Score hypotheses against untouched portions of both notes and against alternate writers/context models; avoid retrofitting the full page.
