## Premise check (CHECK-SHERWOOD, 18 Oct 2026)
(a) What Cossit and Summer were: a British Library catalogue search (`searcharchives.bl.uk`) surfaced a
    separate BL item -- a letter from "Ranna Cossit; Grand Isle, 1 Jan. 1783" among the papers of "Sherwood and
    Dr. George Smyth, commissioners for the exchange of prisoners" -- consistent with Cossit being a named
    Loyalist/clergyman correspondent inside Sherwood's Vermont intelligence network (Grand Isle is on the same
    Lake Champlain islands as Sherwood's North Hero base). "Summer" is not independently identified by name in
    any source checked this sweep; Brymner's calendar treats Summer the same way as Cossit, as a named
    deliverer/author of cipher messages from inside Vermont, not as a place or a code word.
(b) Known Loyalist/British secret-service cipher system: **the Haldimand service is known to have used a
    documented, already-broken book cipher.** `sources/cryptiana/web/haldimand.htm` (Tomokiyo, "Haldimand-
    Clinton Book Cipher (1778-1782)", cached, first posted 26 Apr 2026) documents a book cipher used between
    Haldimand (Quebec) and Clinton (New York) 1778-1782, keyed to the title page of *A List of the General and
    Field Officers* (1778): figure pairs give line-number/letter-number into that page, with a secondary code
    list (word-by-first-letter, 40-50 words per sub-list) layered on top from ~1781. The key was identified by
    NSA's Capt. Edward W. Knepper (Friedman, *Six Lectures on Cryptology*, 1965, pp.41-43) and extended by
    Leighton & Matyas (1985). This is for a *different* pair of volumes (BL Add MS 21807/21808, not our
    target's 21837/21838) and a different correspondent pair (Haldimand<->Clinton, not Sherwood<->
    Mathews/Riedesel) -- grepped the cached page for "Sherwood", "Mathews", "Riedesel", "21837", "21838",
    "Cossit", "Summer": no hit, so this is not a direct match, only a strong structural lead: Mathews was
    Haldimand's own secretary, the date ranges overlap (the book-cipher article's own worked example literally
    decodes to "Vermont assembly is to meet the 1st Octr. I shall send a large detachment...", 13 Sept 1781),
    and Brymner's calendar for this same stretch independently shows other Haldimand-system correspondents
    (Lord Germain, vol.II f.1/f.4) reusing the same book cipher. Whether Sherwood's Vermont-network cipher with
    Cossit and Summer is the *same* book cipher, a derivative of it, or an unrelated field cipher is untested;
    this repo has no target folder yet for Add MS 21807/21808 itself to compare against.
(c) No edition prints the cipher or a reading: confirmed by my own read of Brymner 1888 above. Independently
    full-text-searched *Collections of the Vermont Historical Society* II (archive.org `collectionsofver02vermuoft`,
    1870/1871, be-api full-text search): "Cossit" -- 0 hits; "Sherwood cypher" -- 1 hit, p.568, a narrative
    discussion of Haldimand authorizing "capt. Sherwood to treat with gov. Chittenden", no cipher content, no
    mention of Cossit or Summer. This independently confirms the harvest row's note that VHS II has nothing.

**Sherwood's sketch is written out** (p.84-85; our reading of his hand). It covers four numbered messages:

**Form of the cypher.** Figure pairs `P-W` (separators -, :, = or .), 214 pairs over three pages. P runs 4-472 and W runs 1-38.
A few numbers stand alone (500, 2, 11, 1783), there is one triple (23-4-1), and some words are in letters ("A", "H", "Os°",
"then", "One", "Two", "three", "No four", "Lameness ten liable being left", "low", "Ponderwill[?]"). The pages are
divided into messages that end "One", "Two" (p.346), "three" (p.347) and "No four" (p.345). That order matches Sherwood's
numbering: p.346 opens with No.1 and p.345 is No.4. p.347 carries words in letters written *over* figure groups, an
interlinear period gloss: "Lameness / ten / liable / being / left" over 203-19 96-16 210-19 32-1 209-13, and "February"
under 138-28 in the date line "138-28 = 11 = 1783".
Transcription: ciphertext.tsv, from two blind Opus passes per page (passes/), reconciled with tools/reconcile_passes.py:
p.345 46/50 agree (92.0%), p.346 146/169 (most of the 23 disagreements are line-break joins of one pair), p.347 32/45
(the disagreements are the interlinear words and the date grouping). Disagreements were settled from the full-resolution
image. Tokens left ambiguous are marked `?`; the 3/5 forms are the commonest doubt (131/151-22, 230/250-20).
