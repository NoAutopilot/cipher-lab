**1. H.A. 1836 (borssele-heinsius-1714) -- highest priority, one photograph may close the item.**
Letter no. 959 (Ph.J. van Borssele van der Hooghe to Heinsius, London, 3 April 1714). The edition's own footnote
(Veenendaal, Deel 15/GS227 p.531) states that on the same leaf is "bijgeschreven een brief in onopgelost
cijfer" (an unsolved cipher letter, added on the same leaf), and that a decipherment ("oplossing") of a
different enciphered letter, "mogelijk dezelfde" (possibly the same one), is also present in the dossier.
Photograph: the whole leaf/dossier for letter 959 -- the cipher letter itself, the "oplossing" document, and
the copy of Van Borssele's letter to the States-General the footnote also mentions. Estimated extent: 2-3
leaves. Why: comparing the "oplossing" text against the cipher letter's ciphertext by eye is the only way to
resolve whether this item is already solved (found-solved) or is a genuine sibling-decipherment case; nothing
online can settle it (rule 2, image over transcription -- no transcription exists at all here).

**2. H.A. 946 + H.A. 1034 + H.A. 2317 together (heinsius-hermitage-1704) -- second priority.**
Sauniere de l'Hermitage's cipher passages to Heinsius from London: H.A. 946 (letters no. 166 [26 Feb 1704],
177 [29 Feb 1704], 477 [30 May 1704], all "gedeeltelijk in onopgelost cijferschrift"; also worth photographing
while there, letter no. 251 [25 Mar 1704], whose footnote does not say "onopgelost" and may already be solved,
unresolved by any pass so far) and H.A. 1034 (letter no. 1231, 4 Dec 1705, printed in full with four numeric
codes 14/33/15/50, editor's footnote "de code is onbekend, een sleutel is niet aanwezig"). H.A. 2317, "Sleutel
van een cijferschrift, waarschijnlijk voor correspondentie met Engeland" (a cipher key, c.1705, probably for
correspondence with England, found in the archive's own "2.4.10 Cijferschrift" subsection), is the best-fit
candidate key for this correspondent by geography and date, though the editor's own "waarschijnlijk" (probably)
means it is unconfirmed. Photograph: 946's four letters (166, 177, 251, 477), 1034's one letter (1231), and
2317 in full. Estimated extent: roughly 8-13 leaves (946's four letters plus 1034's one plus 2317's key
document). Why: 946 and 1034 are the same correspondent's two cipher instances 19 months apart -- comparing
their numbers directly (impossible from print, since 946 is only Dutch paraphrase and never shows a numeral)
is the single most useful test in the whole l'Hermitage problem; 2317 is the one candidate key in the archive
that might open either or both.

**3. H.A. 2315, with H.A. 756 (heinsius-dopff-1702) and H.A. 841 (heinsius-vanhaersolte-1703) -- third priority.**
