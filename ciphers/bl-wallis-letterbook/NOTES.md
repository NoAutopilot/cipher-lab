# John Wallis's decipherer's letter-book — BL Add MS 32499 (key-lead resource, not a fresh cipher target)

QUEUE row: N42 (`QUEUE.md`, "Candidates not on DECODE"). Per the brief, this item is to be treated as a
KEY-LEAD resource, not scored as an open cryptanalysis target.

## Source

British Library, Western Manuscripts, **Add MS 32499**. Catalogue text as quoted in QUEUE.md's N42 row:
"Letter-book of John Wallis, D.D., Savilian Professor... decipherer of political correspondence to the
government... containing originals and copies of ciphered letters, with [decipherings]. William III and
Mary II: Intercepted Jacobite correspondence, with decipherings: 1689-1693. Louis XIV of France: Deciphers of
French diplomatic and other letters: 1651-1694." Not digitised.

John Wallis (1616-1703), mathematician and government decipherer from the Civil War through William III's
reign, is one of the two named decipherers (with Blencowe) whose output the TNA France/Empire traffic (SP 78,
SP 87, SP 90, 1650s-1690s) partly depends on.

## Check-solved sweep, 24 September 2026

1. **The item is, by its own catalogue description, already a decipherment, not an unsolved cipher.** Its
   scope note states plainly that it contains "originals and copies of ciphered letters, with decipherings" —
   Wallis's own 17th-century solutions are filed alongside the ciphertext. This is not a search result about
   some other party's later discovery; it is what the manuscript itself is.
2. **Print.** Davys 1737 (*An Essay on the Art of Decyphering*, quoted in `sources/cryptiana/web/davys_e.htm`)
   is built around defending Wallis's status as "the father of deciphering" and prints "the whole text of the
   introduction attached to the collection of deciphered papers that Wallis deposited in a public library in
   1653" — a different, earlier deposit (1653, pre-dating Add MS 32499's 1689-1701 material), not this
   letter-book itself. Web search for Beeley & Scriba's *Correspondence of John Wallis* (OUP, Philip Beeley
   and the late Christoph Scriba) found the edition has reached only volumes 1-4 (1641 to April 1675) of a
   planned eight; it therefore has **not yet reached** the 1689-1701 Jacobite/William III material this
   letter-book covers, and does not print or discuss Add MS 32499's specific contents.
3. **Web, general / community lists.** Tomokiyo's own page on Wallis (`cryptiana.web.fc2.com/code/wallis_e.htm`,
   fetched live 24 Sept 2026; not in the local snapshot) directly cites the shelfmark. Quoted verbatim:
   *"Letters deciphered in 1651-1701 are in British Library, Add MS 32499"* (attributed by Tomokiyo to
   Jacqueline A. Stedall's 2002 *A Discourse Concerning Algebra: English Algebra to 1685*). Tomokiyo's page
   cites the manuscript only as a location reference for Wallis's known corpus — it does not analyse or
   transcribe any letter from it, consistent with treating it as a resource rather than a target.
4. **DECODE.** Cached catalogue grepped for "32499" and "wallis": no record. Expected — DECODE catalogues
   unsolved ciphertexts, and this item already carries its own solutions.
5. **Bourdeau.** Fresh shallow clone grepped for "32499": no hit. Grepped for "wallis": only incidental
   mentions (e.g. `hyde/NOTES.md` cites Wallis's decipherments as historical/editorial context for a different
   target, Hyde/Clarendon correspondence, not Add MS 32499). No target folder for this manuscript.
6. **Aymeloglu.** Fresh shallow clone grepped for "32499" and "wallis": no hit anywhere.
