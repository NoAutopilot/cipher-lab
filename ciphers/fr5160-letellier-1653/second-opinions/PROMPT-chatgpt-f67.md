# Second opinion request: Brienne to Servien (French ambassador at Turin), Paris, 10 October 1659

I am checking whether a seventeenth-century French diplomatic cipher letter has already been deciphered or printed.
Please answer only with sources you can cite exactly: author, title, volume, year, and page or document number. If
you are unsure a source exists, say so. Do not guess.

**Item.** Letter from "de Brienne" (Henri-Auguste de Loménie, comte de Brienne, Secretary of State for Foreign
Affairs, or his son Henri-Louis, who held the reversion) to "M. de Servien", French ambassador at Turin, most likely
Ennemond Servien (ambassador to Savoy 1648-1676), dated "A Paris ce 10 8bre 1659". Original: Bibliothèque nationale
de France, Français 5160, fol. 67r-v (Gallica ark:/12148/btv1b9060495t, views 129-130). Most of the letter is in a
numerical cipher; it opens and closes in clear French.

**Clear text on the leaf (excerpts):** "... en devrez parler à leurs Altesses Royales"; "Desja je vous ay mandé ce qui
s'est ..."; "... il est du service du Roy que vous nous mandiez ce qui en est".

**Our partial reading of the cipher (gist, uncertain in detail):** Brienne asks Servien to find out whether the reported
"mésintelligence" between the young Duke of Savoy (Charles-Emmanuel II) and his mother Madame Royale (Christine de
France) is real, whether she intends to "se retirer du maniement des affaires", what part "leurs principaux ministres"
play, and what the Duke's inclination is for his marriage (a "Mademoiselle" is named, possibly Mademoiselle d'Orléans or
Mademoiselle de Montpensier). Phrases: "de la vérité desquelles il importeroit fort d'estre esclaircy", "quelle peut
estre l'inclination", "presupposé que ce qu'on dit de la mésintelligence d'entre le filz et la mère soit véritable".

**What to look for.**
1. Any printed text, translation or summary of this letter, or of Brienne's dispatches to Servien of October 1659
   (for example in an edition of the correspondence of Christine de France / Madame Royale, of Charles-Emmanuel II,
   of the Brienne secretariat, or in a study of the Servien embassy at Turin).
2. Any archival copy of the letter in clear: the French foreign ministry's Correspondance politique, Sardaigne (or
   Piémont/Turin), volume for 1659 (please give the volume number and folio if you know it), or a register of
   Brienne's dispatches.
3. Any modern study that cites BnF Français 5160 for 1659 (for example Élodie Conti on the Servien embassy, Histoire,
   économie et société 2024/4; Matthieu Gellard in "De Paris à Turin. Christine de France", 2014).
4. Already checked, no need to repeat: Chéruel, Lettres du cardinal Mazarin IX; Recueil des instructions XIV
   (Savoie-Sardaigne et Mantoue I, 1898); Cosnac, Mazarin et Colbert (1892); Claretta, Storia del regno e dei tempi di
   Carlo Emanuele II (1877-78); Saint-Genis, Histoire de Savoie (1869); Brienne's Mémoires (1916).

For each source, give the exact citation and say whether it prints the letter in full, in part, or only mentions it.
