# Cardinal Alessandro Farnese's correspondence on France and Portugal, partly in cipher — BL Add MS 8716

QUEUE row: N46, "Candidates not on DECODE" (N41-N66 block, scoring pass of 24 September 2026). British
Library, Western Manuscripts, Add MS 8716. Catalogue description: "FARNESE CORRESPONDENCE RELATING TO FRANCE
AND PORTUGAL; 1539-1540, 1560-1564. Italian. Copies, partly in cipher." Correspondence of Cardinal Alessandro
Farnese (1520-1589), nephew of Pope Paul III, relating to France under Francis I and Charles V, with
dispatches from Marcello Cervini (future Pope Marcellus II) and Guido Ascanio Sforza among the copyists' work.
280+ folios.

**Disambiguation, important for the next worker.** There are at least two "Farnese cipher" clusters already
known to this project's sources, and neither is this item: (1) Cardinal Alessandro Farnese (1520-1589, this
target's man) has an unrelated ciphered dispatch to the nuncio in Spain, AAV Segr. Stato Spagna 1A, DECODE
R91/R92 — the R92 half (his letter of 15 Apr 1542 to Poggio) was solved by Simon Klee in Sept 2026 and is in
`dbourdeau/cyphersolver`'s `vatican5/` and `SOLVED_CATALOGUE.md`; R91 (Dec 1541, on the Nuremberg diet and
Buda) is still open there. Different holder (Vatican Archive, not BL), different correspondent (Poggio, not a
France/Portugal context), different years (1541-42, not 1539-40/1560-64). (2) A different Farnese, Alessandro
Farnese, *Duke of Parma* (1545-1592, grandson of the above's brother, a different generation), has a
well-documented general cipher with Filippo Sega/Bernardino de Mendoza/Philip II covered in Tomokiyo's
`spanish3.htm`/`viete.htm` (Cryptiana) and Devos/Jensen's published studies — again a different person,
different period (1588-92), different subject (Spain/Netherlands, not France/Portugal). A third, Odoardo
Farnese, Duke of Parma (1612-1646), has a solved 1637 cipher to Sabran, BnF Baluze 156 (DECODE R2753, solved by
George Lasry 2022, per Cryptiana's `GL.htm`) — also a different person and item. This target's Cardinal
Alessandro Farnese, France/Portugal, 1539-40 and 1560-64, BL Add MS 8716, did not appear under any of these
three clusters' search results.

## Editions-first check (24 September 2026)

The brief names the *Nunziature di Francia* / *Nunziature di Portogallo* documentary series, Susta, and Ancel
as the relevant editions. WebSearch confirms these exist and cover the general period and figures — R. Ancel
edited *Nonciatures de France: Nonciatures de Paul IV (1554-1557)* (Paris, 1909-11), which brackets but does
not fully cover this item's 1560-64 span; a WebSearch hit also names Giovanni della Casa's own correspondence
with Alessandro Farnese (1546-47, ciphered, with the nuncio's own decipherments — a different embassy, Della
Casa in Venice, not France or Portugal) as a separate published corpus edited from the same general Farnese
chancery tradition, underscoring that Farnese's ciphered correspondence across several embassies has real
editorial coverage in principle. No search this pass located a *Nunziatura di Portogallo* volume by name, nor
confirmed that either the 1539-40 or 1560-64 portion of BL Add MS 8716 specifically is printed in any of these
series — the correct volume(s) were not identified and no full-text check was run against them.

## Verdict

**open**, stage 2 verified unsolved (conditional: the *Nunziature di Francia*/*Nunziature di Portogallo*
series and Susta's and Ancel's editions are named candidates with real edition risk — Farnese correspondence
is, per the brief, "a well-studied Renaissance-diplomacy corpus" — but none has actually been checked against
this specific item's two date ranges; the next worker should identify which nunziatura volume(s) cover
1539-40 and 1560-64 for France and for Portugal specifically before any copy order). No source in this sweep
identifies, quotes, or describes the content of this correspondence; the three other "Farnese cipher" items
already known to this project are all confirmed different people, items, or both, so this remains a genuine
open candidate rather than a duplicate of solved work.
