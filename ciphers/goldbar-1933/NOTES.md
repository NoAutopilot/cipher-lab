## Cheap test 1: per-letter frequency vs random-sampling control (25 Sept 2026, LANE B3 worker bGLD)

Per `specs/goldbar-1933.json` `cheap_tests_in_order[0]` ("confirm or refute the commenter's 'every letter ~10 times' claim independently... report the design implication") and the lane brief's control-size instruction (1000 draws, vs Bourdeau's own 200,000, run independently on a from-scratch script rather than reusing his `analyse.py`, per rule 8).

**Control-design correction found this pass, logged rather than silently fixed:** the brief and the spec both describe the control as "a random permutation of the same letter multiset." That is a *degenerate* control for a whole-corpus letter-frequency chi-squared statistic -- any permutation of a fixed multiset has, by construction, the identical letter counts and therefore the identical chi-squared value. First run of `freq_test.py` confirmed this empirically: control mean == control min == control max == target statistic, 1000/1000 shuffles identical. The statistic that actually needs a random-sampling null (and the one that answers the spec's real question, "is this flatness the kind of thing chance alone produces") is: 1000 *fresh* N-letter strings, each letter drawn i.i.d. uniformly from the 26-letter alphabet, NOT constrained to the observed multiset. That is exactly Bourdeau's own Monte Carlo design (his NOTES.md: "200,000 random 263-letter strings"), just at 1000 draws instead of 200,000. Fixed and re-run; both versions of the script and the corrected output are in `specs/cheap-tests/goldbar-1933/`.

Script: `specs/cheap-tests/goldbar-1933/freq_test.py` (stdlib only, no numpy/scipy dependency, disk-only, no network). Ran on both readings named in the spec: as-printed (263 letters, McCurley/Schmeh transcription, `ciphertext.txt` as committed here) and with the Bret-Bowen/Bourdeau line-8 correction applied in memory only (262 letters, `UGMNCBXCFLDBEY` -> `UGMNCBXCFLDBY`; `ciphertext.txt` itself is left untouched per rule 2, no manuscript/photograph check was performed by this worker, image fetch is test 3, not run this pass).

Statistic: chi-squared of the 26 letter counts against the uniform expectation (N/26 each, 25 df). Control: 1000 fresh i.i.d.-uniform random N-letter strings (seed 20260925, recorded in the output JSON for reproducibility); same chi-squared statistic computed per draw; target's percentile rank reported against the resulting empirical null.

| reading | N | target chi-squared (25 df) | control mean | control [min, max] | control [p5, p95] | target percentile |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| as-printed | 263 | 1.251 | 24.92 | [8.17, 52.86] | [14.30, 36.84] | 0/1000 (below every draw; 0.0 pct) |
| Bowen/Bourdeau line-8 correction | 262 | 1.176 | 24.76 | [8.52, 55.36] | [14.27, 37.50] | 0/1000 (below every draw; 0.0 pct) |

Both readings: the target's own chi-squared (~1.2) sits below every one of 1000 fresh random draws of the same length (control mean ~24.8-24.9, essentially the textbook expectation of 25 for 25 df; control minimum still 8.2-8.5, nowhere near 1.2). This independently reproduces Bourdeau's finding (his 1.251/1.374/1.176 range across five readings, P ~1e-12 to 1e-13 against 200,000 draws) on a smaller control (1000 draws here vs 200,000 there) and a from-scratch script: **a corpus this flat essentially never arises from chance letter selection of this length; the near-uniform distribution is a designed property of the letter pool, not a coincidence of a natural process.** This is inconsistent with any encryption (substitution, transposition, short-key Vigenere, or long-key/OTP) of natural-language plaintext, all of which would preserve either the plaintext's own letter-frequency skew or the full random-sampling variance around uniform (chi-squared centred near 25, not near 1).

judge_plaintext.py: not run. The spec's `judge` block deliberately omits `language` (schema_note: a language-corpus check would FAIL any real decode by construction here, given the near-uniform design, so it would be actively misleading rather than uninformative) and this test produced no candidate plaintext line to judge.

Verdict for the spec: **closed-negative on "this is a substitution/transposition/short-key-Vigenere/OTP encryption of natural-language plaintext"**, control-backed (rule 3: matched control is a fresh i.i.d.-uniform random draw of the same N, not a fresh-English-text control, because the design question here is "does chance alone produce a corpus this flat", not "does this read as English/Chinese-pinyin prose"). Not a NEAR-candidate: this is not a control-backed *gap* (a technique that came close to the gate) but a control-backed floor -- the target sits outside the control band on the side that argues against any of these encryption designs, not inside/near it. ## Milton Kim's claimed decipherment tested (CHECK-GOLDBAR (account-4), 3 Oct 2026)

Status after this pass: **open**. Kim's decipherment is logged as **claimed, unverified, and not supported by a control**. It is not `found-solved`, because no published key gives a reading that beats a matched control.

**Source.** Milton Kim, Cipher Mysteries, comments on "Those Chinese Gold Bar Ciphers, once again..." (19 Mar 2015 post): comment 13 (26 Oct 2024, #comment-522493), comment 14 (4 Nov 2024, #comment-523710) and comment 15 (27 May 2025, #comment-540506). His repository is github.com/milton6310/cgbCiphers (HEAD 2023f559, 3 May 2025, one shallow clone into the scratchpad, not committed). It has **no licence file**, so nothing from it is copied here (rule 8). Its method is in `docs/How to Solve Chinese Gold Bar Ciphers.pdf` (22 pp.), and that is what was read. This session's permission policy refused reading or running Kim's Python (`libs/enigma.py`, `libs/rotor.py`, adapted from pyEnigma), so it was not run. The Enigma used below was written from scratch from the wiring printed in his PDF.

**Kim's method, as stated in the PDF.** It assumes a factory-wired Swiss-K Enigma: three rotors plus a reflector, no plugboard and no notch changes. For each line he decodes under all 17,576 KEY settings and picks one output "based on plausibility", using a word-segmentation library. RING is normally the line's own last three letters ("L3L"). Otherwise it is three letters taken from another line's chosen reading, written in brackets such as [SRH], [FWT] or [EFG]. Pieces of an output that do not read are treated as a further cipher and decoded again under a new key and ring (IZMUBMHU -> CTGOLEES; WXDYFXJFEXCBN -> ADJGATAMASTWY). The last nine letters of line 4 are read backwards ("GOV SEE LOG"). Two lines are "XOR"ed together (YQH16 with VIO12). Several lines are left undeciphered as "placeholders". The transcription was changed (HLMTAHGBGFNIV -> MLMTAHGBGFNIV) "based on its decryption result". Kim's own caveats: the process is "prone to human error". Deciphering "doesn't guarantee that the finalized one will always be the original message", and "the result presented in this report is merely an example". For ZUQUPNZN he lists three alternative readings: GOD BY ILL, ALL OR RUE and XOR ID IWA.

**(a) Reproduction from our transcription.** Files: `kim-check/enigma_k.py` (own Swiss-K implementation; `--find` sweeps conventions) and `kim-check/kim_table.tsv` (Kim's 13 KEY/RING/reading rows, typed from his PDF pp. 13-18).
- **Line 4 reproduces exactly.** FEWGDRHDDEEUMFFTEEMJXZR at KEY JQE, RING AAA gives OUSTGOVPBANKIZMUBMHUVOG. Our implementation matches when rotor I is the fast rotor, there is no entry-wheel permutation, Kim's KEY is written rotor I-II-III, and the fast rotor's KEY letter is the position at the first letter.
- **The nested piece reproduces.** IZMUBMHU at WSF/MHU gives CTGOLEES, so comment 13's composite OUSTGOVPBANKCTGOLEESVOG is reproduced.
- WXDYFXJFEXCBN at RPY/CBN gives ADJGATAMASTWY exactly.
- **The other ten rows do not reproduce under standard Enigma ring semantics.** Several miss by one or two letters: MQO24 UVE... for USE..., ALT09 DJUDBORIC, MLM13 AGOVTCIUBOUCO, YQV16 CONTACTAWT.... Others are wholly different, and the same reading turns up only at unrelated keys. Kim's simulator therefore handles non-AAA ring settings differently from a historical machine. Without running his code we cannot say whether the gap is his software or ours. It does not change (b), which is a property of searching 17,576 keys, not of one convention.

**(b) Does the method read language better than chance?** Script: `kim-check/sweep.py`, which needs numpy. Raw results are in `kim-check/sweep_result.json`, seed 20261003. Control: the same 17,576-key sweep run on **random letter strings of the same length**, each with a random 3-letter ring. The ciphertext changes, so every statistic can differ between target and control (rule 3).

| statistic | Kim / real line | random-line control |
|---|---|---|
| line 4, RING AAA: keys whose decode contains BANK | 4 | mean 0.74; 2 of 200 controls reach 4 or more (p ~0.01, one a-priori word, one line, no correction for choosing that line or the Enigma model) |
| line 4: keys containing OUST / GOV | 1 / 20 | expected ~0.8 / ~21 at chance |
| line 4: best English 4-gram score over all keys | -1.353 | 108 of 200 random lines score as high or higher (median of the control) |

| Kim's 12 chosen readings: word cover (greedy segmentation) | 0.29 to 0.75 | random best-of-200 cover meets or beats Kim's in 25-40 of 40 lines, depending on the reading |

Examples of best decodes from random strings under the same procedure: TONESSHI, QDMAYHEAD, PTMUSTEPT, VMIVRRTHELAS, RRKFWXORFORNIN, AUXWITIENTAME, LMWSUSEFISHOU.
