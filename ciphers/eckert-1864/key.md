# Key: Cipher No. 1 (Huntington mssEC 41) as used in the 1864 "Ciphers Sent" ledger mssEC 19

Written 19 Sept 2026. Grades follow CLAUDE.md rule 4: H = read from a key source (here the filled-in cipher book
mssEC 41), C = known plaintext (the Official Records print of the same telegram), I = carried over from another
entry of the ledger, M = uncertain. Every row of sections 3-6 is grade H: the meaning is what is written in
mssEC 41 at the page, line and side given in the source column ("p.9 l.1 L (326)" = page 9, printed line 1,
left-hand printed word, Huntington page pointer 326, image `images/mssEC41_p326.jpg`). Section 7 lists the
few words met in the twenty entries that the book does not give.

## 1. What the ledger records

mssEC 19 (Huntington Digital Library object 9302, 415 page images) is the War Department telegraph office's
book of outgoing cipher telegrams from 27 Jan 1864 to Dec 1865. Its 1864 entries are in two ciphers: to the
eastern posts (Baltimore, Harpers Ferry, Fort Monroe) in Jan-Feb 1864 the entries are still in the older
Stager vocabulary (signature "Applause" / "Abortion" = Halleck, "Youth" = troops, "Zodiac" = movement, i.e.
Cipher No. 9 or No. 12), but from Feb 1864 the entries to Grant's headquarters (operator S. H. Beckwith) and
from March 1864 nearly every entry use Cipher No. 1 ("unity", "zebra", "zodiac" = period; "yoke", "youth",
"webster", "walrus" = signature; "Growl", "Grapes", "Hang" = blind words; "Plank", "Pebble" ... = numerals).
Counting marker words over the volunteer transcription, No. 1 outnumbers the older vocabulary from page 21
(March 1864) onward on every ten-page stretch of the volume.

A No. 1 entry as the ledger records it is: a header (the addressee's operator, the place, the date, sometimes
the hour in clear), then the text as the sender's clerk prepared it: a blind word (from the route pages;
"Growl" = 9 columns, "Grapes" = 9 columns, "Hang" = 8 columns ...), a time word (the women's names of the
TIME page), the month in clear and the day in numeral words, the message with names, places, ranks, common
military words and all punctuation replaced by arbitraries, then the word for "signature", the coded signer,
and a few filler words. The text is written in reading order: the route transposition was applied on the
wire and is not recorded, so no route is needed to read the entries (the routes are given in section 6 for
completeness, and would be needed for the received books, mssEC 09-11, if those record the transposed
text). A few names are disguised instead by phonetic splitting ("Tap = pay han = nick", "Lock = wood", "tooth" =
to the, "toby" = to be, "canby" = can be, "opr rate"), which the book's directions allow; these are plain
words, not code, and are left as written.

## 2. The book

mssEC 41 (object 351, 41 page images) is a filled-in copy of Stager's printed template "Cipher for
Telegraphic Correspondence ... 1861 & '62", the same template as mssEC 67 (Cipher No. 9, used in the 1862
pilot). The title page verso is headed "No One Cipher" by hand; the printed explanation is corrected by hand
("Commencement Word" struck and "Line Indicators" written; "lines" struck and "Columns" written on every route
page; the sentence on "three combinations of cipher on each of the first eight pages" crossed out), which is
Tomokiyo's diagnostic for Cipher No. 1 (civilwar1.htm). Page [B] lists the holders: Col. Stager, Maj. Eckert,
Capt. Bruch, Capt. Smith, Capt. Fuller, Beckwith, HQ Army of the Cumberland, HQ Army of the Ohio, Mason. A loose
letter in the volume (pointers 348-349) is Eckert to Capt. R. C. Clowry, St Louis, 10 July 1865, sending
Cipher No. 5, repeated 10 April 1867 by Chas. A. Tinker. Contents: TIME page (48 names, 1 AM to 12.30 AM);
pages 1-8, routes for 5, 6, 7, 8, 9, 10, 7 and 8 columns with nine blind words and twenty line indicators
each; pages 9-24, 26 printed lines a page, a printed code word at each end and the meaning written between
(a bar divides the line when the two words carry two meanings): States, Forts, Rivers, Places, Rebels,
cabinet and generals, then "Miscellaneous Words" in alphabetical order of meaning; numerals on a handwritten
leaf numbered 25. Three lines carry blotted-out printed words under section headings (p.9 l.19, p.15 l.7,
p.18 l.6) and one is blank (p.9 l.18 Apollo/Altar). Three lines were added by hand (p.16 l.27 Knave/Knox =
Butler, p.17 l.27 Mock/mouse = Palmer/Negley, p.20 l.0 Roman/Rape = Expedition) and two changed (p.16 l.9
"Jasper" struck, "Jew" written; p.16 l.22 a struck name, "Wilson" written). A "#" in the margin marks the
verbs to which "ed" or "ing" is added (foot of pages 21 and 24: 'add "Ed" or "ing" to Arbitrary').

Transcription: two independent passes by subagents from the 2000 px images (19 Sept 2026), compared row by
row by the orchestrator. Over 156 + 162 + 182 rows the passes differed on 3 + 37 + 27 rows, almost all
formatting (initials with or without spaces, ":" or "." in the times, "=" or "-" between the inflection
marks); the substantive disagreements and how they were settled by re-reading the crop are listed in
NOTES.md, section 3. The route pages agreed in every cell.

## 6. Blind words, routes and line indicators (grade H, mssEC 41 pages 1-8)

The nine blind words of a page stand for the number of columns written at its head; the route is the order
