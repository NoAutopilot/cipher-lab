Written 20 Sept 2026. The block between the markers is generated by `python3 decode_no2.py --write` from
`ciphertext-no2.txt` and the tables in `key-no2.md`; `python3 decode_no2.py --check` exits 1 if it is stale.
Grades per CLAUDE.md rule 4 apply to the code-word tokens: H = the meaning is written in the cipher book
mssEC 47; C = only the Official Records print of this very telegram gives it; I = inferred from the layout or
another entry; M = uncertain. Plain English words are not graded; they are what the clerk wrote, including the
phonetic disguise "She reeves port" (Shreveport) and the slips "Holdgate", "supercede", "confi dential ly".

## Why these three

NOTES.md section 2 set the Beckwith and Kimber entries aside as "not Cipher No. 1". The brief of 20 Sept 2026
asked whether the Huntington's Cipher No. 2 books are that cipher. Three entries printed in the Official
Records were chosen from the ledger's volunteer transcriptions (search over all 403 transcribed pages for
"Beckwith" and "Kimber" with the punctuation words "tulip", "pike", "yacht", "yawl", "yard"): two to Grant at
Culpeper in April 1864 and one to Canby in June 1864, i.e. both headquarters and both operators. Each was
transcribed by the orchestrator from the 2583 px page image with the volunteer text as second witness; the two
witnesses agree on every code word (the volunteer text reads "Chant" where the page has "Chart" in N2-B).

## Notes on particular readings

- The headers. N2-A begins "Fanny Knapp Genesis" = 11 AM, 16, April (time word, day in numeral words, month
  from the blue leaf [25C]); N2-C begins "Huron June Dawson Topsy" = Washington, June 6, 12.30 PM. N2-B has no
  head words in the text; its header carries "Sumter quarter", which are a blind word (9 columns, route of
  page 8) and a line indicator (10 lines, page 7): the operator's note of the route used on the wire, the only
  trace of the transposition in these entries. Its tail "Genesis Nelson Franklin Henrietta" = April 29, 2.30 PM.
- The signatures: "yawl Lamb" (N2-A), "yawl Embrace" (N2-B), "yawl Battle" (N2-C) = signed H. W. Halleck,
  General in Chief, Maj Gen H W Halleck. Halleck has six words in the book (Battle, Comet, Cupid, Lamb, and
  Embrace/Enlist = General in Chief); "Battle" is also a blind word of page 3, and the arbitraries table takes
  precedence in decode_no2.py (key-no2.md, head).
- Punctuation: Tulip, Yacht and Yardstick = period, Pike = comma, Stanhope = interrogation, all grade H; the
  OR's punctuation agrees at every one of the 27 marks.
- N2-A: "Arnold Dwight Pekin" = 2,000 cavalry (Arnold = 2 on the numerals page, and the same word gives "two
  engineer officers" in N2-C and "two more batteries" in N2-A itself); the OR prints "5,000 cavalry". The
  ledger records what was sent. "the French" = the 10th; "Baker Bethel" = Fourth New Hampshire, "Edwards
  Babel" = Eighth Maine; "trifle" = will be (blue leaf [25C]). The closing "Tulip another" after the signature
  is filler.
- N2-C: "Ordnance" and "pass" are printed code words of the book (After the; Fire) but are plain here ("Ordnance,
  Quartermaster's and Commissary Departments"; "pass his lines"), marked plain in ciphertext-no2.txt as the
  1864 pilot did for "Camels" and "person". "Albert" is the clerk's Abert, "Holdgate" his Holgate. The tail
  "warm day" is the operator's chat.
- Pencilled figures and words over the first line of N2-A and N2-B (2, 6, 4, 10, 8; "read", "little", "egg",
  "been", "plain"; "yesterday", "10 am"; "(sent 12 m from Book ...)", "(90 w)", "(2.35 PM Tinker)") are the
  telegraph office's service notes and a later hand's attempt at the first words; they are not transcribed.
