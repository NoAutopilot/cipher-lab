# Key: the old vocabulary of the Jan-Feb 1864 entries in mssEC 19, read from mssEC 67 (Tomokiyo: Cipher No. 9)

Written 6 Oct 2026 (R7A-ECK64, LANE LANE-RUN7-account-1). Grades follow CLAUDE.md rule 4: H = read from a key source
(here the meanings handwritten in the Huntington copy mssEC 67, object 1750, between the two printed code words of each
line), M = uncertain. Pointers are Huntington page pointers; page p.[n] = pointer 1720 + n (p.[1] = 1721, ciphers/eckert-1862
key.md section 2). Pages 1720 and 1731-1733, 1743 were fetched at 1000 px and 1737-1741 at 1400 px from the IIIF server and
read by the worker, one eye, no subagent; pp.[22] and [24] are the committed ciphers/eckert-1862/images/mssEC67_p1742.jpg
and p1744.jpg. Only the lines this sample needs are tabled, plus the whole TIME page; the rest of mssEC 67 is not transcribed
here. Many printed lines of mssEC 67 carry no handwritten meaning (all of p.[19] Pacific-Pine, the right-hand page of p.[15]
not seen), so a word missing from this table may be a printed word left blank in this copy, not a plain word.

Why this book: NOTES.md section 2 identified the Jan-Feb 1864 entries as the older vocabulary by the marker words Applause/
Abortion (Halleck), Youth (troops), Zodiac (movement), all three as filled in mssEC 67. The four entries read with this table
(reading-no9.md) agree with the Official Records print in every coded word but one (Village = Major against the print's
"Brig. Gen." for K. Garrard, a data conflict logged in reading-no9.md, not settled here), which is the check that the
handwritten meanings of mssEC 67 are the ones in use in the War Department office in Jan-Feb 1864.

decode_no9.py reads every `| code word |` table here with the machinery of decode.py (endings s/es/ed/ing on a stem; the
clerk's "Lock = wood" splits are joined before lookup, so "Lockwood" is the plain name, not Lock = Jackson + wood).
