## Image request: three cipher originals whose digitised WVO scan holds only the decipherment (WC-NASSAU-FIT2, 26 Sept 2026)

**Why this matters:** the 5549 body (Jan van Nassau to Willem van Oranje, 21 Nov 1573, the "verendertte Instruction
oder Ciffer" key adopted after the key change of Nov 1573) is still unread -- WC-NASSAU-FIT and WC-NASSAU-FIT2
have now fit-tested nine Willem-authored siblings against it (5803, 5804, 5805, 5210, 5214, 5812, plus J6/J7's
5204/5205/5207/5208/5209/5213) and none matches (cosine 0.21-0.54, all well under the 0.70 gate). The three
items below are letters the Briefwisseling van Willem van Oranje (WVO) project's own catalogue records as
carrying a period decipherment ("oplossing") -- but the PDF actually served for each one, checked page by page,
turns out to hold only that decipherment or a duplicate copy, not the ciphertext original. Each record's own
source table (quoted below, read from `resources.huygens.knaw.nl/wvo/app/brief?nr=<n>`, 26 Sept 2026) shows
where the true cipher original sits. None of these three has been read against the 5549 body; if any one is in
the same key, it is a direct crib (its own contemporary decipherment gives the plaintext for its ciphertext, the
alignment problem the solver-repository lesson (LESSONS.md, "look for the sibling") already flags as the most
productive route for this kind of case).

1. **WVO 4502** (23 Nov 1573, Willem to Lodewijk van Nassau, from Delft -- two days after the 5549 body).
   Opmerkingen: "Het origineel in cijferschrift, met oplossing en met een duplicaatschrijven" (the original in
   cipher, with a decipherment and with a duplicate). The record's own source table lists three witnesses:
   - **Koninklijk Huisarchief Den Haag, shelfmark A 2, 723 A/I, "origineel"** -- no image in this WVO scan.
   - Groen van Prinsterer, *Archives ou correspondance inédite de la Maison d'Orange-Nassau*, série I, IV,
     250-252 no. CDLIII (printed edition; a clear-text edition, not the cipher).
   - Koninklijk Huisarchief Den Haag, shelfmark A 3, 895/I, "kopie contemporaine" -- this is the one WVO digitised
     (the PDF fetched this pass); both its pages are plain French, confirming it is the decipherment/duplicate
     copy, not the cipher original.
   **Wanted:** an image of KHA A 2, 723 A/I (the "origineel", in cipher).

2. **WVO 5808** (2 Dec 1573, Willem to Lodewijk, Jan and Hendrik van Nassau jointly, from Delft).
   Opmerkingen: "Het origineel betreft een duplicaat, met een postscriptum, gedeeltelijk in cijferschrift, met
   afzonderlijke oplossing. De contemporaine kopie [bevat] alleen het niet in cijferschrift geschreven gedeelte
   van de brief" (the original is a duplicate, with a postscript, partly in cipher, with a separate
   decipherment; the contemporary copy holds only the non-ciphered part of the letter). The record's own source
   table lists:
   - Koninklijk Huisarchief Den Haag, shelfmark **A 3, 895/I, "origineel"** -- this is the WVO-digitised PDF (8
     pages fetched this pass); every sampled page is plain French, matching the opmerkingen's own statement that
     this copy has only the non-ciphered part.
   - Groen van Prinsterer, série I, IV, 281-285 no. CDLVII (printed edition).
   - Koninklijk Huisarchief Den Haag, shelfmark A 3, 895/I, "kopie contemporaine" -- no image link at all in
     this record.
   **Wanted:** the cipher passage and its "afzonderlijke oplossing" (separate decipherment) for this letter,
   evidently a physically separate leaf from what was scanned under A 3, 895/I here -- ask the KHA whether a
   distinct cipher/decipherment leaf exists under this shelfmark or an adjacent one.

3. **WVO 7205** (16 Jan 1574, Willem to Lodewijk, Jan and Hendrik van Nassau jointly, from Vlissingen).
   Opmerkingen: "Het origineel is een duplicaat, grotendeels in cijferschrift. De contemporaine oplossing is
   bijgevoegd op f. 211 r - 212 r" (the original is a duplicate, mostly in cipher; the contemporary decipherment
   is attached at f.211r-212r). The record's own source table:
   - **Algemeen Rijksarchief van België (ARAB), Audiëntie 90, f. 208 r-213 r, "origineel"** -- the WVO-digitised
     PDF (10 pages fetched this pass); a page-by-page check found dense clear-French secretary hand throughout,
     no numeral cipher anywhere, despite the opmerkingen's statement that the original is "mostly in cipher" and
     that a decipherment sits at f.211-212 within this same folio range. Either the ciphered leaf(s) within
     Audiëntie 90 ff.208-213 were not captured by this scan, or the foliation/page order in the PDF does not
     match what the opmerkingen describe.
   **Wanted:** confirmation from the ARAB of what Audiëntie 90 ff.208-213 actually contains leaf by leaf, and an
   image of whichever leaf(s) carry the cipher passage (the opmerkendecipherment at f.211r-212r would be the
   crib if so).

## Lookup/copy ask: Staatsarchiv Marburg (HStAM), the "4f Nld. 165" second copy of the 17 April 1574 letter

**Target:** WVO 5551 (briefnr 5551), Jan (Johann VI) van Nassau to Willem van Oranje, 17 April 1574, from Köln
-- the two ciphered lines at the top of p.3 (`ciphers/jan-van-nassau-1572-75/AUDIT.md`, "V-TX2 second audit").
Rolf Glawischnig's 1973 study *Niederlande, Kalvinismus und Reichsgrafenstand 1559-1584. Nassau-Dillenburg unter
Graf Johann VI* (Marburg, 1973) cites, per a Google Books snippet read by a previous verifier pass, a second
copy of this letter cluster at **Staatsarchiv Marburg, "4f Nld. 165"** (his own footnote shorthand for "Bestand
4 f Niederlande", item/Paket 165).

**What was found this pass (TX-KEYS, 25 Sept 2026):** the fonds is now catalogued online in **Arcinsys Hessen**
(arcinsys.hessen.de), the Hessian state archives' finding-aid and digitisation system. It is reachable without
login (browser tool needed -- the typeahead-driven search forms do not work with plain curl POSTs, and a
site-wide free-text search only finds terms that literally co-occur in the same record). The relevant material
lives under **HStAM > 4 f Staaten N (Staatenabteilung: Nassau-Nürnberg) > 5 Nassau-Dillenburg, Fürsten**, split
across per-subject nodes (5.12 "Krieg; Militär", 5.14 "Politik", and others, 5.1-5.20) -- each item's own
"(Vor-) Provenienzen" field confirms these were reorganised out of an **older fonds literally named "4 f
Niederlande"** (e.g. one item's provenance note reads "Landgraf Wilhelm IV., aus 4 f Niederlande, Paket 1"),
each with its own old "Paket" number that does not survive as a literal substring in the new catalogue entry.
**Item "165" specifically was not located** in the pages sampled this pass (5.12 "Krieg; Militär" page 1 showed
items numbered 228, 780, 778...; 5.14 "Politik" showed 819, 141, 254, 364, 1574, 1579, 1651...; neither an
identifier-search nor a free-text search for "4 f Niederlande" AND "165" returned a hit). Several items in this
same branch do carry preview thumbnails in the online catalogue (confirmed by screenshot), so at least part of
this fonds is digitised -- but this pass could not confirm digitisation, or even locate the current catalogue
entry, for the specific old "165" item Glawischnig cites.

**What is needed:** the archive's own staff can resolve an old fonds/Paket citation like "4f Nld. 165" against
their current Arcinsys signature far faster than more blind browsing here (state archives normally keep a
concordance between a pre-digitisation numbering and the current one). Ask:
1. What HStAM's current Arcinsys signature is for the item cited by Glawischnig (1973) as "4f Nld. 165" (old
   fonds "4 f Niederlande", Paket/item 165).
2. Whether that item is digitised in Arcinsys (many neighbouring items in the same branch already are).
3. If digitised: a link to or a screen-quality image of the leaf(s) datable to 17 April 1574 (Jan van Nassau to
   Willem van Oranje, from Köln), specifically whether the two ciphered lines carry an interlinear or marginal
   period decipherment.
4. If not digitised: the cost/process for a reading-room photograph of that item.
