# Charles Whitworth (Moscow) to Harley and Boyle, 1707-08: the "undeciphered" items of TNA SP 91/5

- **Status:** open. Narrowed on 19 Sept 2026 to one short passage. Of the five Discovery items catalogued
  "undeciphered", four have their plaintext in print or in the same volume; only the gap in SP 91/5/108
  (Moscow, 30 July/10 Aug 1707) is unread. Nothing can be done from published sources; the next step is the
  copy order in `REQUEST.md`.
- **Plaintext language:** English. Sender Charles Whitworth, envoy extraordinary at Moscow; recipients Robert
  Harley (Secretary of State, Northern Department, to Feb 1708) and Henry Boyle (from Feb 1708).
- **Ciphertext:** none transcribed. SP 91/5 is not digitised on Discovery and is online only behind the Gale
  State Papers Online paywall, so no `ciphertext.txt` exists yet. Discovery item list, generated by
  `tools/discovery_items.py`, in `sp91-5-cipher-items.tsv` (53 items of SP 91/5 whose description mentions
  cipher, decipher, duplicate or undeciphered; 47 of them say only "Partly in cipher", which in this piece
  means the cipher was deciphered on receipt, since every such letter checked is printed in full in the
  Sbornik).

## Search before solving (CLAUDE.md rule 1), 19 Sept 2026

1. **Search engine.** WebSearch for "Whitworth" "SP 91/5" cipher and for Cryptiana/Tomokiyo Whitworth Moscow:
   nothing relevant; the only Whitworth ciphers anywhere are Lord Whitworth's Paris despatches of 1803
   (see DECODE below).
2. **Printed correspondence.** Sbornik Imperatorskago Russkago Istoricheskago Obshchestva (Collection of the
   Imperial Russian Historical Society), vol. 39 (St Petersburg 1884), "Diplomatic correspondence of English
   ambassadors and envoys at the Russian court, 1704-1708", and vol. 50 (1886), the same for 1708-1711.
   Both print the English text from the State Papers ("Public Record Office, Russia, No. 8" is the source note
   under each letter) with a Russian translation beneath.
   - Vol. 39 read from page images at the State Public Historical Library, Moscow (elib.shpl.ru, node
     10315, pages 1316195-1316199 and 1316258-1316265 of the viewer; free, no login). Not on archive.org; the
     HathiTrust catalogue answered HTTP 403 from this environment and was not checked.
     - **f. 108 = vol. 39 no. 122, pp. 407-411**, "M. Whitworth to the right honourable m. secretary Harley,
       Moscow, 30 July (10 August) 1707". Printed in full except one gap, p. 410: "since the prospect of a
       sudden peace in the north seems to decrease, ..... (undeciphered) ..... the french minister had
       acquainted the court he had lost all hopes of succeeding." The Russian has "(не разобрано)" at the
       same place. This is the unread text. Its length is not given; from the sentence it is a clause of a
       few words to a few lines about the French minister's mediation between Sweden and Russia.
     - **f. 163/180 = vol. 39 no. 143, pp. 470-477**, "M. Whitworth's private letter to ... Harley, Moscow,
       10/21 March 1707-8". Printed from the decipher, no "(undeciphered)" mark on any of pp. 470-477; the
       editors abridged the opening and close (marked "..."), so the full deciphered text is at f. 180 only.
   - Vol. 50 read from the archive.org OCR text (identifier sbornikimperatorskogorusskogoistorichesk58,
     2.5 MB djvu text; the Russian OCR is usable, the English is garbled but present). Contents page and
     body: **f. 274 = vol. 50 no. 27, p. 88 ff.**, despatch of 22 Sept/3 Oct 1708 on the affront to the
     Russian ambassador (Matveev) in London; **f. 294 = vol. 50 no. 28**, "private letter of Ch. Whitworth
     to secretary Boyle, 22 Sept (3 Oct) 1708", on the same affair, the young noblemen sailing from
     Archangel, and the Chancery of Embassies. Neither carries an undeciphered mark; the only
     "(шифрованное и не разобранное слово)" in vol. 50 is in a 1710-11 letter of Weisbrod, outside SP 91/5.
     So the duplicates ff. 298 and 300 are enciphered copies of letters whose plaintext is in print.
   - Not checked: HMC Portland vol. IV (Harley's own papers, BL Add MSS 70000 series), which may calendar the
     private letters of 1707-08 again. It cannot add unread text, since the SP 91 copies are the originals.

## What is established and what is inferred

- Established (H, from the Sbornik and Discovery): the plaintext of ff. 274, 294 and 180 is in print; f. 108
  has one undeciphered passage and is otherwise printed in clear.
- Inferred (I): that the 1707 letters in SP 91/5 marked "Partly in cipher" carry the office decipher
  interlined or on the facing page, as was the practice; the Sbornik prints them without gaps, which fits.
  Not verified on an image.
- Inferred (I): that Whitworth used one cipher through 1707 and possibly the same one in 1708. Harley was
  replaced by Boyle in February 1708; a new key may have been issued then. The 298/294 pair would settle the
  1708 key, the 1707 neighbours of f. 108 settle the 1707 key.
- Unknown: the length and design of the cipher (numerical nomenclator is the norm for SP 106/7 keys of the
  period, but no image has been seen).

## Route

The key is the route, not cryptanalysis: the passage is a few lines, below any unicity distance, but every
neighbouring letter of the same summer (ff. 106, 121, and the rest of the 1707 "Partly in cipher" run) was
deciphered in London, so the same key is recoverable as known plaintext from any one of them. Order f. 108 and
one or two neighbours; see `REQUEST.md`. Grading of any reading will be H against the recovered key, with the
Sbornik text (vol. 39 p. 410) as the context on both sides of the gap.

Why it is worth little: the gap is one clause in a letter already printed, on the well-documented French
mediation of 1707 (Besenval's), which Whitworth also reports in clear in neighbouring letters. Weight in the
queue should drop from 2 to 1.
