# Armstrong private-correspondence source search — 27 September 2026

**Result: unsolved. No new plaintext, numerical assignment, or verified glyph assignment.** This pass changed instruments: it searched contemporary correspondence and collection descriptions for an additional witness, rather than rerunning the unsuccessful substitution searches. Two specific Livingston items warrant image retrieval. Neither has yet been compared with the target.

## 1. A catalogued Livingston key in the Monroe Papers

The Library of Congress **1963 Index to the James Monroe Papers**, printed **page 11**, PDF page **27** (zero-based 26), lists:

| Entry | Date | Series | Pages | Addenda |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| LIVINGSTON ROBERT R CIPHER CODE | 1803 | 1 | 2 | KEY |

[Index PDF](https://tile.loc.gov/storage-services/service/gdc/gdclccn/62/06/00/06/62060006/62060006.pdf). The page was inspected as an image. The title page confirms **Monroe**, not Madison; an initial working attribution to Madison was wrong and is corrected here. The index counts written docket/cover pages, so “2” does not establish that two pages contain key entries.

The [1904 chronological catalogue](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/d/d3/Papers_of_James_Monroe_%28IA_papersofjamesmon00libr%29.pdf), printed p.29 / PDF p.49, puts a Livingston cipher at the end of its 1803 section. These are probably two descriptions of the **same item**, not independent key witnesses. The modern [Monroe finding aid](https://findingaids.loc.gov/repositories/19/resources/3523) links the 1963 index and explains the collection's transfer from State to LOC in 1903.

**Next retrieval target:** Series 1, year-only 1803 material, likely near the end of 1803 on [reel 3](https://www.loc.gov/item/mss33217003/) (1803-10-09 through 1807-01-16). That placement is an inference from catalogue order, not a resolved image number. No manuscript image was obtained: LOC item/resource/JSON requests returned access errors, and the 1893 catalogue's two available PDF copies exceeded the web reader's size limit; a direct download also failed. Do not interpret those failures as absence of a digitized copy.

**Unestablished:** whether this is WE027, WE028, a partial key, an older cipher, or something else; whether it has any connection to Armstrong's anomalous code. No direct-transfer test is possible until its entries are read. This lead is distinct from the already logged Brant Box 37 worksheet request.

## 2. A private Livingston cipher letter at Brooklyn

The [Center for Brooklyn History finding aid](https://findingaids.library.nyu.edu/cbh/1974_002/all/) identifies **Mid-Atlantic early manuscripts collection, 1974.002, Box 1, Folder 25**. It includes a **1803 Paris letter from Robert R. Livingston to his brother**, about the Louisiana negotiations, described as **“partly in cipher handwriting.”** The catalog does not establish which brother received that 1803 letter. Its uncertainty about Edward occurs in the description of the separate 1801 letter.

This is a specific private-channel specimen, rather than another general claim that Livingston used ciphers. No image or decipherment was retrieved. An image should first be screened against known THE=972 and WE028 entries and the already sampled Livingston/WE027 specimens. Only a materially different pattern would justify building a new pool. A family connection alone does not establish a shared key with Armstrong.

The collection is catalogued as open for research. Its generic instruction to use digital surrogates when available does **not** show that this particular letter has been digitized. No request was sent.

## 3. Warden: a narrower search space, not a key discovery

[William D. Hoyt Jr., “The Warden Papers,” Maryland Historical Magazine 38 (1943), pp.69–85](https://msa.maryland.gov/megafile/msa/speccol/sc5800/sc5881/000001/000000/000149/pdf/msa_sc_5881_1_149.pdf), describes **twenty Armstrong letters, 1804–1810**, on p.69. These include correspondence while Armstrong travelled and instructions about the Paris office. This supplies a finite candidate group. The article does not identify an Armstrong cipher key. No hit for `cipher` or `shorthand` in the downloaded volume's OCR is only an OCR-search result, not evidence that the manuscripts contain no code.

The holdings must be distinguished:

| Repository | Locator | Relevant scope / limit |
|---|---|---|

No key explicitly catalogued. |

The LOC Box 25 lead was already mentioned in the target blog's comments; it is not a new discovery from this pass. The useful addition here is the twenty-letter group identified by Hoyt.

## 4. Context does not yet supply a crib

[Armstrong to Jefferson, 15 February 1808](https://rotunda.upress.virginia.edu/founders/default.xqy?keys=FOEA-print-04-01-02-7420&mode=deref) describes an unnamed bearer with French naval service and earlier dispatch-carrying work. That makes an introduction plausible as a topic. It does not prove that the 20 February cipher repeats the introduction. The bearer was not securely identified in this pass. Later correspondence mentioning Captain Haley is insufficient to assign his name to the cipher.

The earlier repetition conflict in the proposed opening remains unresolved. No words from that proposal were promoted to recovered plaintext. [Madison's 15 May 1808 postscript](https://rotunda.upress.virginia.edu/founders/default.xqy?keys=FOEA-print-02-01-02-3083&mode=deref) remains the reason to investigate another correspondent's key, not proof of a particular correspondent.

Other triage: the [FDR Hudson manuscript finding aid](https://www.fdrlibrary.org/documents/356632/390886/findingaid_roos_hudsonmanuscripts.pdf/5b0e08ba-5f8c-48f4-a801-3835452fec2a), Appendix I item 1, lists Armstrong family microfilm but specifies permission restrictions; no material was obtained. A [digitized NYPL Livingston correspondence result](https://old-digitalcollections.nypl.org/items/e9b176b0-faad-0133-37d5-00505686a51c) belongs to **Henry Livingston Jr.**, not an identified Robert-to-brother cipher specimen. The [1942 Parsons article](https://americanantiquarian.org/proceedings/44807068.pdf) was read for Livingston/King context but did not supply a new code table.

## What would count as progress from these leads

Retrieve the Livingston key image first. Transcribe its readable code/plaintext pairs with locations and uncertainty; check known encoded correspondence as a positive control before applying it to Armstrong. Then retrieve the Brooklyn private letter and compare actual symbols/numbers. A common vocabulary or a few plausible words is insufficient. Any proposed target reading must use a consistent key, account for repeated groups, and extend coherently into passages not used to choose the key.

No solver experiment was run in this pass, so there is no new controlled cryptanalytic negative and no excluded cipher family. Retrieval errors and search-engine misses are not evidence against the existence of further material.
