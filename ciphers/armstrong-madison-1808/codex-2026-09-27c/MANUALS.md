# Primary shorthand inspection, 27 September 2026

**No Armstrong reading established.** The manuals support a correction to
how the earlier Annet comparison was described; they do not identify
Armstrong's system.

## Sources and edition boundary

Read the 1752 and 1770 Annet scans linked by
[Stenophile](https://www.stenophile.com/historical). See
`manual_sources.json` for exact URLs, scan identifiers and SHA-256 hashes.
Inspected each PDF's third page visually and read the extracted instruction
text. OCR, especially in 1770, contains severe bleed-through errors and is
not a trustworthy transcription of the graphic signs.

The 1761 scan used in the old shape inventory could not be fetched in this
pass: Internet Archive endpoints returned HTTP 502. The 1752 and 1770
alphabets differ. Neither has been substituted for a verified transcription
of the 1761 instructions.

## 1752 observations

The alphabet plate assigns both letters and standalone word meanings.
Examples include a vertical d meaning THAT, a dot i meaning I/EYES, a
circle o meaning NOTHING, and a long horizontal w meaning WITH. The
instructions discuss joining letters, abbreviating word parts, varying
length, and marks placed inside, above or below characters. Consequently,
testing only a fixed one-sign/one-letter alphabet does not test the whole
system. The numbered examples are instructional examples; their numbers
are not evidence of an Armstrong codebook.

## 1770 observations

The plate explicitly separates an alphabet from word parts and additional
forms. Representative entries read:

| Printed entry | Standalone meaning / word part | Visual description |
|---|---|---|
| e | the | short horizontal stroke |
| l | will | small cup |
| m | him | wide arch |
| n | in | small arch |
| o | not | small circle |
| w | with | dotted cup |
| th | that | long curved stroke |
| con/coun | prefix | inverted narrow U |
| com/come | word part | inverted broad U |
| ment | ending | arch |

These descriptions are orientation aids, not a machine-readable alphabet.
The instructions also give context-sensitive shortening and omissions.

Armstrong's provisional shape class 16 resembles the dotted cup, giving
WITH as a possible **visual hypothesis** for the first graphic sign.
No systematic reading of the other classes follows from it, and it has
not passed a repeated-passage check. It is not entered in a key or used as
a numeric crib. This resemblance neither proves Annet nor disproves the
competing proposed AN reading.

## Correction to the earlier inventory

The old `HYPOTHESES.md` ARM-S3 passage calls the Annet 1761 specimen a
nonalphabetic arbitrary sign index. That is insufficiently supported as a
description of Annet's system: inspected editions have explicit alphabets,
standalone word signs and compounds. Retain the old shape measurements as
measurements, but do not turn their frequency non-test into an Annet
exclusion. A genuine test needs an edition-specific alphabet, composition
rules, and a matched specimen. The new letter/digraph solver is merely a
bounded surrogate for variable-length signs, not that historical test.

## Historical cross-check

The [15 May 1808 Madison-to-Jefferson letter](https://rotunda.upress.virginia.edu/founders/default.xqy?keys=FOEA-print-02-01-02-3083)
was opened directly. Its postscript says the unexplained Armstrong letter
was probably misaddressed and used a cipher agreed with another
correspondent, absent from the office. The site labels this an early-access
transcription, not a final edited version. This corroborates the private-key
lead; it does not supply a key or prove which surviving letter it describes.
The correct observed Rotunda identifier ends in **3083**, not 3082.
