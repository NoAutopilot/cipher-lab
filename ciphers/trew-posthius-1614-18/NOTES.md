## Source and correspondents

- **13 March 1614** letter, `H62/TREWBR POSTHIUS_ERASMUS[2`, `bav:UBE-TRE-00000BAV80016364`.
- **February 1618** letter, `H62/TREWBR POSTHIUS_ERASMUS[8`, `bav:UBE-TRE-00000BAV80016370`.

Both: Universitaetsbibliothek Erlangen-Nuernberg, Briefsammlung des Nuernberger Arztes Christoph Jacob Trew
(catalogued by Eleonore Schmidt-Herrling, *Die Briefsammlung des Nuernberger Arztes Ch. J. Trew in der
Universitaetsbibliothek Erlangen*, 1940; the manuscript catalogue was published in book form 2007, ISBN
3-89131-528-7, and the collection (~19,000 letters, 2,200 correspondents, 16th-18th c.) was digitised from
December 2017; also searchable, with a free account, at trew-letters.com — see Access below). Both bavarikon
object records quote Schmidt-Herrling's "Kurzaufnahme eines Autographen" verbatim (already logged by scBAV).

**Sender:** Erasmus Posthius (3 Aug 1582, Wuerzburg – 1618), physician in Heidelberg; son of Johannes Posthius
(1537-97), the Neo-Latin poet and physician to the Wuerzburg prince-bishop and, from 1585, electoral physician
at Heidelberg. Erasmus matriculated at Heidelberg 1600 (MA 1604; re-enrolled 1613 and 1618), studied at Siena
(1611) and Padua, and was in London in 1612 (WebSearch, Deutsche Biographie / de.wikipedia.org "Johannes
Posthius" and related pages, 24 Sept 2026). The 1614 letter's "Parens b.m." ("my late father, of blessed
memory") and the 1618 letter's context are consistent with this identification, not with his father the poet
(who died 1597, before either letter). A Bamberg Staatsbibliothek record for Erasmus Posthius's own *Stammbuch*
(album amicorum, 1595-1610, Inc.typ.Ic.II.46; Kalliope id DE-611-HS-3746185) independently confirms the person
but was not opened this pass (out of scope for a check-solved run; flagged for a future palaeography pass, since
an album amicorum entry in Posthius's own hand would be a comparison sample for the key hand — see below).

**Recipient:** Johann Christoph Eisenmenger/Eysenmenger the Elder (6 Sept 1592, Heilbronn – 20 Feb 1663), city
physician of Heilbronn from 1626, son of city physician Jeremias Eisenmenger; studied at Heidelberg from 1608,
doctorate at Basel 1614 (de.wikipedia.org "Johann Christoph Eisenmenger der Aeltere"; Kalliope authority record
eac124549381, "Eysenmenger, Johann Christoph (1592-1663)", Arzt, Heilbronn). The 1614 letter's date (13 March)
is a few months before his Basel doctorate; the letter's own address side and the 1618 letter's body both
address him as "Doctori Medicinae" / greet him as newly graduated — consistent. The 1618 letter's body also
names "D.D. Knoblochius", travel "extra viam... Hailbronnae", and "recta tenderent Francofurtum" (heading for
Frankfurt), i.e. Heilbronn is where Eysenmenger is based, matching his biography.

## What is on the leaves (eyeballed at full native resolution; NOT transcribed or decoded, per brief)

**1614 letter, recto (`images/1614_p1.jpg`, crop `images/1614_p1_key_crop.jpg`):** an ordinary Latin letter
(medical/personal chat — apothecary recipes, a visit from "Cornelius Drebbelius", Röslin's tables, etc.),
signed "Erasmus Posthius D." Below the signature, bottom-left of the leaf: a note "Eandem descriptionem olim
communicavit Parens b.m. domino D. Camerario Norib[ergensi]" (my late father once sent the same description to
Camerarius of Nuremberg), then a 4-line block of ciphertext in ordinary Latin letters (not drawn symbols) —
e.g. line 1 reads as "AWs fir uao [?]pcaho xih" — followed immediately by the clear-text crib "Fridericus
Henricus vocat[ur] recens natus Princeps" (a newborn prince is called Friedrich Heinrich — a real, dateable
event: Friedrich Heinrich, later stadtholder, was born 29 Jan 1584 in Delft, so this crib is NOT about him;
more likely a different, contemporary 1614 birth carrying that name pair — not investigated further, out of
scope). Directly under the signature, to the right, is a two-line keyed alphabet: "**Salutembcdfg**" over
"**hiknopqrwxyz**" (read at full native resolution; the second line's first letter is **h**, not **b** as an
earlier eyeball pass logged it in QUEUE.md — b already appears at the end of line 1). This resolves cleanly as
a textbook keyword-mixed alphabet: the 24-letter Latin alphabet with no j/v (a b c d e f g h i k l m n o p q r
s t u w x y z) reordered as keyword **SALUTEM** (Latin "greetings", i.e. the letter's own opening formula: the
1618 letter is headed "SALVTEM") followed by the remaining 17 letters in plain alphabetical order — b c d f g,
then h i k n o p q r w x y z, exactly the two written lines. **The key is a complete permutation of the full
24-letter alphabet**, so it can express any plaintext in that alphabet; every letter visible in the 4-line
ciphertext block falls inside the 24-letter set. It sits in the same ink, size and looping italic hand as the
signature and the rest of the letter, immediately below "Erasmus Posthius D." — this reads, by eye, as the
**sender's own hand**, appended for the recipient's use, not a later addition. (This is an eyeball judgement,
not a palaeographic comparison against another authenticated Posthius sample; the Bamberg Stammbuch above would
be the control if this is taken further.)

**1614 letter, verso (`images/1614_p2.jpg`):** address side, wax seal, no cipher content visible.

**1618 letter, recto (`images/1618_p1.jpg`):** an ordinary Latin letter, headed "SALVTEM", dated "Februarij
1618 Haidelbergae raptim", signed "Ex tua ... Posthius". **No ciphertext or key in the letter body itself** —
the whole recto is plain Latin prose (news of other correspondence, an "Anatomia" course, greetings to
"Sebastianus Hornmoldus"). This differs from what the scout's brief characterised ("a short enciphered block");
the enciphered material for this letter is confined to the address leaf, described next.

**1618 letter, verso/address side (`images/1618_p2.jpg`, crop `images/1618_p2_key_crop2.jpg`):** address block
("Viro Clarissimo atque Doctissimo Domino Johanni Christophoro Eysenmenger, Medicinae Doctori, amico meo
suavissimo"), wax seal, and, below the fold at the bottom of the leaf, **two distinct key blocks in two
visibly different hands**, plus a short worked specimen that mixes plain German words with cipher:
- **Left block**, in a running cursive that could plausibly be the same informal hand as the rest of the
  correspondence: a plaintext row beginning "**Conradb**" continuing into five hand-drawn invented glyphs (not
  Latin letters), and a second row "**klmp**" continuing into more drawn glyphs then "**uwx**" — i.e. a
  keyword-to-symbol nomenclator ("CONRAD B..." as the keyword) rather than a letter-to-letter cipher like the
  1614 key. A single diagonal line is drawn through most of the glyph portion of both rows — struck through,
  cancelled, or crossed out by whoever wrote or later handled it. Not legible/countable precisely enough at
  this pass to say whether all 24 letters are covered; the glyphs are drawn marks, not transcribable from an
  eyeball pass.
- **Right block**, in a markedly different, careful, almost print-like round hand (letterforms and pen
  clearly unlike both the left block and the letter's own cursive body text): a second, independent
  plaintext-to-plaintext-letter key, "**Conradbefgbi**" over "**Klm.pqstuwxyz**" (also keyword "CONRAD..."-led,
  but a straight letter substitution, no drawn glyphs), followed immediately by several more lines that read as
  a worked specimen mixing plain German and cipher letters (e.g. "...ist ein", "kleiner ...", "Der ...",
  ending "...xuob."). This looks like a practice/demonstration passage for the cipher, not a real secret
  message concealing news, and its hand does not obviously match Posthius's signature hand on either letter.
- **Net read for 1618, without decoding:** this leaf carries cipher *teaching or reference* material (two
  competing key systems plus a specimen), not an actual enciphered communication hidden inside the letter's
  own news. Whether either key is complete, which (if either) is Posthius's own hand versus Eysenmenger's or a
  third party's, and which key (if either) the 1614 letter's cipher shares a design with, are open questions
  for a solver/palaeography pass, not settled here (brief: do not decode).
