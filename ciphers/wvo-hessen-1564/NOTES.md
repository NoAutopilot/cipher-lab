## Correction to the brief's own description

The brief describes 1109 as "Willem van Hessen to Willem van Oranje". **WVO's own record reverses this**:
1109 is **Aan Willem van Hessen** ("Aan" = To) -- i.e. **Willem van Oranje to Wilhelm IV, Landgrave of
Hesse-Kassel**, sent from Brussels, 18 Sept 1564, archived at the *recipient's* archive (HSAM, Bestand 3II,
Nassau-Niederlande, Korr. 1564-1565, f. 22r-24v) -- which is exactly why the original with its cipher
enclosure survives at Marburg rather than at the sender's own Nassau archive. 1107 (22-8-1564 per WVO,
Wilhelm of Hesse to the Prince, from Kassel) is the letter 1109's own Opmerkingen field cross-references
("Vgl. 1107" = cf. 1107) -- an earlier, separate, uncoded letter in the same exchange (see below), not the
letter carrying the cipher.

## Images opened this pass (both PDFs, all 9 pages, by eye, this worker directly -- no subagent)

**1109 (5 pages):**
- p0 (f.22r): docket "1564. Sept. 16." top-left (a date that disagrees by two days with WVO's own 18-9-1564
  and the enclosure's own "ad 1564. Sept. 18." heading below -- an archivist's/filing note, not treated as
  authoritative), foliation "22" top-right. **Plain German cursive**, ordinary diplomatic prose (news of the
  Landgrave's family, the Emperor, general courtesies). No cipher.
- p1: continuation, plain German (news from France, the Cardinal of Lorraine, the Duke of Lorraine's
  marriage plans). No cipher.
- p2 (foliated "24"): the main letter's closing and date, **"...Septembris Anno [1]564"**, place "Brüssel",
  signed "Willem Printz zu Uranien Graff zu Nassau Catzenelnbogen" and again, more personally, "gantz
  dienstwilliger Bruder Wilhelm Printz zu Uranien". Plain German throughout. No cipher.
- **p3 (foliated "23"), headed "ad 1564. Sept. 18." -- the cipher enclosure itself**, matching WVO's
  Opmerkingen exactly (a separate leaf, bound between f.22 and f.24, carrying the "eigenhandig ondertekende
  in cijferschrift geschreven bijlage"). ~16 lines. **Mixed plain-German-word / invented-cipher-sign text**:
  ordinary German function words and short words are legible in clear ("en v ertrauen ni[cht]", "und", "das",
  "man", "die adern zweimahl", "clagen und ta[u]sch zweimahl", "purgiren mussen dermassen das ... worden sei"
  -- this worker's own rough, uncertain eye-read, grade M/I, not a transcription), interspersed every few
  words with invented cipher signs (not a Latin/Greek/Hebrew alphabet, not shorthand) -- no interlinear gloss,
  no marginal reading, no key table anywhere on this leaf. Signed again "Wilhelm printz zu Vranien" at the
  foot, confirming this is Orange's own enclosure, in his own cipher. Rough sign-token count: on the order of
  100-150 individual cipher-sign tokens across the ~16 lines (not counted exactly; a real transcription pass
  would need to segment clear-word runs from cipher-sign runs first). The thematic content this worker's rough
  read suggests ("adern" = veins, "purgiren" = to purge/bleed medically) fits the letter's own Inhoud (news
  refuting a rumour that August of Saxony died of a hunting injury) far better than a name/place nomenclator
  would -- read as a description of medical treatment (bloodletting/purging), plausibly the specific detail
  Orange did not want left in clear.
- p4: outer address/docket leaf. "Praesentatum ... 28 Sept Anno [15]64 ..." (received/presented 28 Sept 1564,
  ten days after the 18 Sept dateline -- ordinary transit time Brussels-Kassel), addressed "Dem hochgebornen
  Fürsten unserm ... freundlichen lieben Herrn Vetter und Schwager Wilhelm Landgraven zu Hessen" -- confirms
  addressee and correspondence circle. Wax seal fragment (triangular device visible). No cipher.

**1107 (4 pages, KHAG origineel):** p0-p2 continuous plain German cursive (matches the Groen print almost
verbatim at the opening, confirmed above); p3 is the outer address/docket leaf with wax seal, plain. **No
cipher anywhere in this copy** -- consistent with 1107 carrying no "bijlage" note in WVO's own Opmerkingen
(only 1109 does) and with Groen printing it in full, seamlessly, as ordinary prose.

## Sign-system comparison with `willem-van-hessen-1567` (brief item 3: sign type and value range only, no application)

1109's cipher enclosure (18 Sept 1564) and `willem-van-hessen-1567`'s briefnr **174** (9 Apr 1567, **the same
direction: Orange to Hesse**, three years later) are worth comparing because they are the same two
correspondents' own hands going the same way. By eye, at the 400dpi crop resolution available
(`images/01109_p3_crop400*.jpg`), 1109's repertoire is the same *kind* of invented-sign system 174's letter
body and key leaf use: angular and geometric shapes (triangle Δ, square □, a barred "Π"/table sign, "X",
retrograde "R"), astrological/zodiac-style marks (a ram's-horn "γ"-like sign resembling Aries, hooked
crosses, "‡"/"†"), and arrow-like strokes -- not a real alphabet, not shorthand, not the curvier
zodiac/alchemical set OX-WV69 describes for **1069**'s interlinear gloss. This is a qualitative, by-eye
impression only (no glyph-by-glyph atlas built this pass, out of this brief's scope) but is a real,
checkable lead: **174 already has an extracted 26-letter nomenclator key** (`willem-van-hessen-1567/siblings/
key_174_nomenclator.tsv`, from the same OX-WVH pass) sitting unused, from the same sender writing the same
direction to the same recipient. No key was applied to 1109 this pass (brief scope: comparison only).

## Verdict

enclosure or a decipherment (see line 2 and the searches below); WVO's own Brongegevens lists no printed
edition for 1109 at all (manuscript only). **Copy status: fully copy-free** -- both 1109's HSAM origineel
(carrying the cipher) and 1107's KHAG origineel are free PDFs, no login, already fetched into this folder.
**Kind: cryptanalysis** (no crib located this pass -- 1107 is a different, uncoded letter in the same
exchange, not a plaintext parallel to 1109's cipher passage; unlike `willem-van-hessen-1567`'s 1127/1069
pairing, there is no known plain copy of 1109's own enclosure).

**Next step: park -- below unicity, no key.** The enclosure is short (~16 lines, an estimated 100-150 cipher-
sign tokens once clear-word runs are excluded) and carries no gloss, marginal reading or key table on the
leaf itself; do NOT anneal blind. The one concrete, cheap lead for a successor, not pursued this pass: test
174's already-extracted nomenclator key (`willem-van-hessen-1567/siblings/key_174_nomenclator.tsv`) against
1109's enclosure by hand on a handful of signs, since both are Orange's own hand, same direction, same
correspondent, only three years apart -- a real key-reuse hypothesis, not blind annealing, and the brief for
that test should require a control (does an equally-sized run of 174's key applied to a *different* short
period ciphertext of unrelated origin produce comparably plausible German, or does 1109 alone light up?)
before treating any hits as more than suggestive (CLAUDE.md rule 3).
