## 1. Every leaf of invnr 2442

invnr 2442 has **11 scans total** (`viewer.response.scans` in the item page's drupal-settings-json). Per this
job's cap ("at most 8"), scans 1-8 (the `order` field) were fetched at full size from `service.archief.nl` and
eye-checked; scans 9-11 were not fetched (see `images/manifest.json`).

**invnr 2442 is not one letter -- it is a bound file of several separately-foliated copied documents**, of which
the Senisteros-Pena letter is only one. The corner folio stamps visible on the fetched leaves are not sequential
with scan order (54, 55, 84, ~40, [none visible], 56, 62, [none visible]), and leaves 3 and 8 are plain
uncoded Spanish prose in a different hand/item with no cipher at all, while leaves 4, 5 and 7 carry heavy cipher
that reads as a **different** correspondence (no plausible textual continuity with leaves 1/2/6). Leaves 1, 2 and
6 (folios 54, 55, 56) are the most plausible run for the actual Senisteros-Pena letter and match the unittitle's
description most closely (cipher blocks embedded in running Spanish prose about ordinary household/patronage
business, a papal nunciature, a canonry vacancy, ships and cargo -- consistent register for two correspondents,
not obviously diplomatic/state business).

Cipher share: leaves 1, 2, 4, 5, 6, 7 (6 of 8 fetched) carry substantial cipher passages -- entire paragraphs of
letter+digit groups, not just names or short phrases. Leaves 3 and 8 have none.

**No interlinear or marginal decipherment, no translation, and no separate key or key-table was found on any of
the 8 leaves fetched.** Every cipher passage sits directly in the same ink, same hand, same running text as the
plaintext prose around it -- no annotation in a second hand, no bracketed gloss, no numbers written above/below
the cipher groups.

Symbol inventory, cheap look only (no decoding attempted, per brief): cipher groups mix lowercase Roman letters
with digits, e.g. "s8crd4r37", "c4l82", "7ccq4s37n", "d8c3r7". Digits recur far more densely, and in more
positions, than a name-only or short-phrase cipher would need -- consistent with (but not confirming) a
**vowels-as-digits** pattern layered onto an otherwise-plain consonant skeleton, which is a known Spanish
diplomatic-cipher convention of this period; some digits (7, 4, 8) recur very frequently in short, word-final
runs, which is at least suggestive of vowel frequency. This is an observation for whoever takes this to
cryptanalysis, not a finding -- no decoding was attempted here.
