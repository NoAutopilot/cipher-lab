# Anonymous 1669 cipher letters to James (Duke of York, later James II) with a partial name-key — BL Add MS 21483

QUEUE row: N43 (`QUEUE.md`, "Candidates not on DECODE").

## Source

British Library, Western Manuscripts, **Add MS 21483**. Catalogue text as quoted in QUEUE.md's N43 row:
"Anonymous letters, in cipher: 1669" (ff.1-8), continuing "...partly in cipher, on political affairs, WITH A
KEY TO SOME OF THE NAMES" — i.e. a partial key to names survives bound in the same volume. The volume adds
nine further ciphered correspondence items 1685-1688 (Exclusion-Crisis-to-Glorious-Revolution political
material, including Melfort, the Abbess of the English Benedictines at Ghent, and a letter involving Eleanor
"Nell" Gwyn), not itemised past the folio list this sweep. Not digitised. In 1669 the recipient held the title
Duke of York; he did not become James II until 1685, so "James II" in the QUEUE row's summary line is
retrospective, not a contemporary title for the earliest letters.

## Edition risk

**Untested rather than cleared.** Clarke's 1816 *Life of James the Second* is exactly the kind of edition that
could print or discuss ciphered correspondence from this period (it draws on Stuart-court papers of the same
general provenance as much of this queue's Jacobite material), but this sweep did not run a full-text search
against it. That is the first thing to close out before treating this as a live cryptanalysis target.
