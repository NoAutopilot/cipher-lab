# Group D: the case that c1 and c2 carry no readable language, and where it stops

DEB-SWARM-D, 29 Sept 2026 (box 03:08-06:08 UTC). Public copies only; the settled three-pass drafts of cryptograms 1
and 2. Nothing here is a reading; rule 10 throughout. Every number below is regenerated by the script named beside
it; the attempt-by-attempt record is `LOG.md`.

## The claim, stated so it can fail

If c1 and c2 were a language written in any system that keeps the order of its units (letters, syllables, a mixed
letter/word code, with homophones up to the real sign curve, an X-like null at 13 pct and 15 pct transcription
noise), the order of the signs would carry information that the same signs shuffled within their lines do not. The
test is a single frozen score: z(adjacent mutual information) + z(bigram types seen twice or more) + z(repeated
3-grams) - z(doubled adjacent signs), per text, plus the held-out transfer of a bigram model from one text to the
other (`dcore.score`, threshold 4.3 on the pair, 3.5 on c2 alone, fixed on calibration controls before any blind or
real text was scored).

## Where the case stops (routes these tests cannot see)

Each of these makes a real French text look like the real c2 on this battery, measured at c2's shape:

| route | c2 score, 20 controls | share <= real |
|---|---|---|
| running-key Vigenere, then homophonic | median -0.7 | 14 of 20 |
| letters shuffled inside each word (ANAGRAM), then homophonic | median 1.3 | 8 of 20 |
| random nulls at 35 pct of the signs (plus X, plus noise) | median 0.4 | 12 of 20 |
| random nulls at 50 / 65 pct | -0.2 / 0.7 | 17, 11 of 20 |

So the strongest honest statement is conditional: **c2 carries no sequential order of the kind any unit-by-unit
writing of a language leaves at this length and noise. If it is language, the system randomises order on purpose: a
non-periodic key (running key), letters scrambled inside words, or random filler making up a third or more of the signs,
in each case on top of X.** c1 alone (125 signs) cannot tell language from null by any test here.

## What argues the other way (reported, not argued away)

- Cryptogram 4's verse ends its couplet lines on matching signs (H5: within 5 of 10, across 0 of 9, p < 0.0001, two
  independent transcriptions) and pictograms open lines far more often than chance (H31, p 0.0002, reproduced on
  Bourdeau's transcription). Both are layout structure: a writer making meaningless verse-shaped writing can
  produce them on purpose, and a language produces them too. They are not order inside a line, which is what this
  battery tests; they do not decide between the two.
- The real text is also unlike the two human "meaningless writing" models tried: a writer with habitual successors
  (NULL-HABIT, pair score median 16, 0 of 40 as low as real) and one avoiding recent signs (NULL-AVOID, doubled z
  about -2.6; real about -0.2). The real sequence is closest to signs drawn independently from a table. Human
  attempts at random sequences usually show one of those two habits; that it shows neither is a point for a
  deliberate randomising process (a cipher's filler, or picking signs by chance from a list), not only for scribble.
- Test R (re-reading): see the section below.

## Test R, the one surprise

Re-reading c2 as the undoing of a columnar transposition of height 34 gave the scan maximum 8.18, above all 12
NULL-IID scans and at p = 0.010 against 200 scans of c2's own within-line shuffles (median 5.11, p95 6.89). One family
among about ten here. Pre-registered replication (LOG.md 03:30 UTC):

| check | result |
|---|---|
| (a) this group's reading, fresh seed, 200-scan own-shuffle null (`r34_rep_fresh.json`) | best again R34, 7.52 vs null median 5.19, p95 7.27: **p 0.03** |
| (a) harness reading, punctuation-class boxes kept (N 658) (`r34_rep_harness.json`) | best R6, 4.31 vs null median 5.27: **p 0.80**, gone |
| (b) the R34 reading's two halves, 1,000 shuffles (`r34_rep_halves.json`) | rows 1-17: 5.55; rows 18-34: 4.23; both above 0 |

By the letter of the pre-registration it is a lead (the fresh-seed route and both halves hold). Read plainly it is a
**weak, fragile lead**: the fresh seed re-samples only the null (the text is the same), the halves were not
independent of the choice of R34, and it vanishes when the 15 punctuation-class boxes are kept -- which a real
transposition of height 34 would survive only if those boxes are not cipher signs. What would settle it: a solver
group runs its matched-control method on the R34 reading of c2 (the reading is `reread.readings()['R34']`), with
the harness's c2a/c2b fold held-out; and the image check of whether BLOB, HOOK-L and DASH-H are signs or marks.
Nothing is claimed from it here.

## One line for the merge

Structural facts: (1) inside its lines, c2 has no more order than its own shuffles (frozen score 0.4-1.0 against 8.8+
for every harness language control at 15 pct noise, 5 of 5 blind); (2) no frequent sign is a word space; (3) periodic
polyalphabetics, word codes, autokey and column or transposed writing are excluded by controls that read them, except
the fragile R34 reading. Constraint that kills a class of keys: any key that reads c2 in line order as letters or
syllables, one unit per sign or with homophones, is reading a text with no measurable order, so it must be fitting
noise unless the system also carries a running key, word-internal scrambling or a third or more random filler. Tool:
`dcore.py` (the frozen order score, usable on any group's intermediate text) and `harness_blind.py`.
