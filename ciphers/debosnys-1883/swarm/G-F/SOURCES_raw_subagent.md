# Published keys of Masonic / fraternal ciphers, 1800-1885 (raw source notes)

Accessed 29 Sept 2026. Grades: VERIFIED = I read the page/scan myself; SECONDARY = read only via a web summary; UNVERIFIED = not seen.
Convention: "cell" = one of the 9 cells of a # (tic-tac-toe) grid; a cell's glyph is the frame pieces around it.
Sign-shape words: ┘ = L-corner with vertical stroke on the right meeting a horizontal stroke at the bottom (lower-right corner); └ = vertical on left + bottom; ┐ = top horizontal + vertical on right; ┌ = top horizontal + vertical on left; ⊔ = U, open at top; ⊓ = inverted U, open at bottom; ⊐ = "]" (right vertical + top and bottom strokes, open left); ⊏ = "[" (open right); ▢ = closed square.

## 1. Royal Arch cipher ("key to the ineffable characters") - Bernard 1829 (VERIFIED from page scan)

**Citations**
- David Bernard, *Light on Masonry* (Utica: W. Williams, 1829), pp. 138 (the key as sign row, in the Royal Arch lecture) and 143 (narrative: key "kept in the ark on four distinct pieces of paper ... marked on a square piece of paper", divided by dotted lines; "The other part of the key X is made on the back of the same piece of paper"; "said that these characters were used by Aaron Burr"). Google scan on IA: https://archive.org/details/lightonmasonrya00berngoog (leaf 150 = p.138, leaf 155 = p.143; page image https://archive.org/download/lightonmasonrya00berngoog/page/leaf150_w1400.jpg). OCR text: .../lightonmasonrya00berngoog_djvu.txt. Date 1829.
- Avery Allyn, *A Ritual of Freemasonry* (Philadelphia, 1831; IA item https://archive.org/details/A_Ritual_Of_Freemasonry_-_A_Allyn, edition/date in IA metadata not stated - unverified which printing), text p.141 ("The key to the ineffable characters or royal arch cipher ... delineated in plate 13, fig. 7, consists of right angles, in various attitudes, with the addition of a dot. Being dissected, it forms 26 distinct characters ... call the first sign a; the second b; and so on, reading from left to right"; footnote: "said to be the cipher used by ... Aaron Burr"). Text read, plate 13 fig 7 image NOT looked at.
- Malcolm C. Duncan, *Masonic Ritual and Monitor* (first ed. 1866; the IA copy https://archive.org/details/Duncans_Masonic_Ritual_And_Monitor_-_M_C_Duncan is an undated later printing, sacred-texts-style transcription, "Key to the Ineffable Characters" p.248-249): same wording as Allyn ("right angles, in various situations, with the addition of a dot ... twenty-six distinct characters"). Sign figure not in the OCR text; not viewed.
- Albert G. Mackey, *Encyclopaedia of Freemasonry* (1874; the IA scan https://archive.org/details/volume1encyclopa00mack is the later Clegg-revised printing, Masonic History Co.), s.v. "Cipher Writing": describes the Agrippa 9-compartment cipher, then "About the middle of the eighteenth century, the French Freemasons adopted a cipher similar in principle ... addition of four compartments, made by the oblique intersection of two lines in the form of a Saint Andrew's Cross"; "never officially adopted ... except in the American Royal Arch"; "Rockwell openly printed it in his Ahiman Rezon of Georgia". Also cites a purported "Oliver Ritual" of 1749 (reliability unverified; the passage as printed may be a 20th-c. Clegg addition - unverified). Also says on old lodge boards the dot is not used, so each sign stands for two letters. Text-only; chart not in OCR.

**Design (VERIFIED, Bernard p.138)**: The key figure is printed as a # (two horizontals crossing two verticals) followed by a separate X. Rule printed: "beginning at top of this diagram at the left hand angle ... The upper left hand angle without a dot is A; the same with a dot is B, &c." So the 13 frame pieces are taken in reading order (9 # cells row by row, then 4 X wedges), and EACH frame piece serves two consecutive letters: bare = first letter, dotted = second letter. 9 cells x 2 = 18 letters (A-R); 4 wedges x 2 = 8 letters (S-Z). Total 26, no I/J merge, so J is its own sign. This differs from the modern "grid, grid dotted, X, X dotted" layout (section 2). Bernard prints the 26 signs in a single row under the letters A..Z (his row is visually ambiguous: the 26-letter caption spacing is uneven, so letter-to-sign pairing below is inferred from the stated rule and confirmed only by counting 26 signs in the row).
Signs are all angular, thin strokes, no curves; dot is a small round dot inside the frame.

Also the 9 cell glyph order (which corner sits where) is consistent with the # geometry but was read from OCR-poor 1829 print; treat cells 7-9 (┐ ⊓ ┌) as read from the crop, high but not perfect confidence.

## 3. Agrippa's Nine-Chambers / Rosicrucian type (period print 1801, 1874)

- Barrett, *The Magus, or Celestial Intelligencer* (London, 1801), Book II ch. on characters (printed p.~61-63; the OCR shows running head "Part I. CEREMONIAL MAGIC" and page mark ~63 - page number UNVERIFIED), IA https://archive.org/details/1801-barrett-the-magus-or-celestial-intelligencer . Text: Hebrew letters in three classes of 9, distributed "into nine chambers ... framed by the intersection of four parallel lines ... right angles (Plate fig. A). Out of which, being dissected into parts, proceed nine particular figures (fig. B) ... by that Neco (point), which if of one point shews the first letter of that chamber; if of two, the second; three, the third." Same source lists three other writings: "Malachim, or Melachim, i.e. of angels, or regal", "passing through the river", and the celestial writing; "you may see [them] in the following Plates". Plate images NOT viewed - so no per-letter shape descriptions for Celestial / Malachim / Passing the River from this source (UNVERIFIED).
- Mackey, Encyclopaedia (above), gives Agrippa's scheme in words: cells hold letters 1,10,19 / 2,11,20 / ... / 9,18,27; 1, 2 or no dots (Mackey's own OCR sample: no dot, one dot, two dots).
- Rosicrucian layout per Wikipedia: ABC DEF GHI / JKL MNO PQR / STU VWX YZ, cell shape = the nine frame pieces above, 1-3 dots (left/centre/right in some renderings).

## 4. Knights Templar (Maltese-cross) cipher (SECONDARY, 20th-c. print)

- McKeown, "Purported Templars cipher", https://freemasonry.bcy.ca/texts/templars_cipher.html (200): reproduces from A. Bothwell-Gosse, *The Knights Templars* (Golden Rule Lodge No. 21, Transactions I; London: The Co-Mason, 13 Blomfield Rd., pp.89-92, undated, 20th century) with L.J. Dickinson. Letters: Series I unmarked A B C D E F G H I K L M; Series II dotted O P Q R V T U S X Y W Z; N is the central letter (24 letters + N = 25; J omitted). Groups of eight: 1st group (A-D, O-R) from "baseless triangles forming the arms of the Cross"; 2nd (E-H, V-S) "triangles composing the Cross of the order"; 3rd (I-M, X-Z) "lozenge-shaped figures". Text also says three letters appear in "Paris MSS (13th c.)" and on Templar seals. Per-letter shapes are in a picture I did not see. Other web sources (dcode.fr/templars-cipher, asecuritysite.com/cipher/templar) not fetched. Wikipedia's Templar diagram is Wikimedia file "Knights-templar-letters-horizontal.svg" (based on Grand Lodge of BC & Yukon description); not viewed.
- The search snippet attributes an earlier reference to Grégoire, *Histoire des sectes religieuses* (1828) - UNVERIFIED, not read. I found no 19th-c. monitor (Richardson, Duncan, Allyn, Mackey) that prints the Templar alphabet in OCR text.
Design (from the text): signs are cross-arm triangles/wedges and lozenges, dot distinguishes the two series; N is a lone sign.

Design (secondary only): multi-character composite figures (boxes containing several elementary characters), not one sign per letter; a horizontal top line is a frequent component (42% of boxed groups); some whole-word figures. A per-sign shape table is UNAVAILABLE.
