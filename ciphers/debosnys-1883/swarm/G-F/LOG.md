## Summary for the merge (transferable, no keys)

1. **Pictograms are not Copiale-style word symbols.** Copiale's word symbols are few and reused (11 types, subject
   words: master, lodge, society); Debosnys's pictures are many and mostly single (23 types in 60 tokens; Copiale
   never exceeds 8 in 60). Their line-initial excess (ratio 3.7) is double Copiale's (1.8), and a Copiale-sized
   effect would be invisible at Debosnys's N anyway (3 of 49 windows). A group reading pictures as recurring
   subject logograms should not expect a Copiale pattern; "drawn once, placed at a line start" fits a decorative or
   initial-capital role better than a lexical one (Copiale's own decorative capitals: ratio 17, never line-final).
2. **Plain X is not a Copiale-type space.** At X's density the space test sees Copiale's spaces in about 9 of 14
   size-matched windows; X shows no sign of it (adjacency, gap regularity, one-sign gaps all at shuffle level).
   So if X is not text (H39), it is a null or filler dropped at random, not a word divider. A tool: `space_test.py`
   runs on any class of ids.
3. **Masonic alphabets are not the source of the sign set.** Pigpen shapes cover under 5 pct of tokens; the Copiale
   overlap is the shared symbol stock (planet/alchemical signs, Greek letters), and Copiale's values read nothing.
   The Masonic lead from Bauer is a resemblance of genre, not of alphabet. The one untested Masonic design is the
   Folger composite figure, whose text is not online.
