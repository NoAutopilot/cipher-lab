# Copy request: NRS GD406/1/2197

What to ask for, in the order that finishes the job soonest. Take the exact contact route from the NRS website
(nrscotland.gov.uk, "Ordering copies" or the Historical Search Room enquiry form) rather than from here, since
addresses change. Bourdeau's note cites the copying service; the NRS site is the authority.

## The order

1. **GD406/1/2197**, "Keys for ciphers used in the correspondence of the Duke of Hamilton", c. 1645, 5 items.
   Catalogue access: Open, on site. Ask for digital images of all five items, both sides of every sheet.
   Say why: to decipher four printed letters of Charles II to the 2nd Duke (Camden Society 1880 nos. 174-177),
   which the cataloguer's description says these keys may read.
2. **Only if no key in item 1 fits:** the microfilm frames of Hamilton Red Book vol. ii nos. 156-159, which are
   GD406/1/10573, 10574, 10575, 10576 (the letters of 6, 14, 31 August and 27 September 1650). The originals are
   not produced to readers; the microfilm is available in the Historical Search Room, so ask whether frames can be
   copied. These would settle the five places where the two printed witnesses disagree.

## Things to ask about in the same message

- Whether reproduction of GD406 items needs the depositor's consent. The Hamilton muniments are deposited, not
  owned, by NRS, and some family collections require the owner's permission for copies or for publication.
  Ask for copies for private research first; permission to publish a reading can be a second request.
- The cost and the turnaround. Copying services usually quote per image.
- Whether GD406/1/2496 (Patrick Maxwell, Paris, June 1650, "asking for the duke's cipher") can be produced. It is
  catalogued closed as unfit for production, so probably not, but it shows a cipher was being issued in 1650.

## What the cataloguer's "c 1645" means

A guess for undated sheets. The 2nd Duke succeeded his brother in March 1649 after the execution, and the letters
here are from the King in exile in Scotland in 1650, so the relevant key may be dated later than 1645 or not at
all. Do not let the date put you off ordering all five.

## Fallback if NRS cannot supply

Aymeloglu's shortlist points at John Wallis's papers in the Bodleian, which contain royalist letters he
deciphered for Parliament in 1650. If Wallis broke this cipher, his worksheet would be the key. That is a second
copy request, to the Bodleian, once the NRS answer is in.
