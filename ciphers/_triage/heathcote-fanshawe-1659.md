## What was found (the two items, precisely)

Both are in the Hyde-to-Fanshaw group HMC 1899's own preface locates at "pp. 7-15" of the report's own internal
numbering (preface p.iii-iv; this maps to the externally-cited "pp.155-156" via the report's position within
the bound annual HMC volume -- not independently confirmed page-for-page, see gaps):
1. **[1659], June [4-]14, Brussels** -- Hyde to Fanshaw, "the long letter concerning offices" (Fanshaw's Latin
   Secretary and Master of Requests appointments). Ends: "Holograph. 1[?] [The parts in italics are in cipher,
   undeciphered]".
2. **[1660 N.S. / 1659 O.S.], January [14-]24, Brussels** -- Hyde to Fanshaw, a postscript about books sent.
   Ends: "Holograph. {The words in italics are in cipher, undeciphered}. 1 p."
(A third, 1649, Charles II-to-Fanshaw cipher passage in the same volume is marked "deciphered in the margin,
but not apparently very exactly" -- a different item, different direction, not part of LEAD-AO2.)

**HMC 1899's own preface (p.iii-iv) on item 1**: "The long letter concerning offices is much of it in cipher...
There are many pages of decipher amongst his [Fanshaw's] papers, evidently sent to him from England in answer
to a confession to Arlington (in a letter in the Spanish Correspondence at the Public Record Office) that he
thought he must have taken out the wrong key... It seems doubtful whether he ever read the letter here printed
at all, for an attempt (in his own handwriting) to unravel the first sentence, in which the words, 'for the
wrong he has done you,' take the remarkable form of 'from they wara onga ha here divide gaine,' cannot have
helped him much to grasp the contents, although it has proved a very useful clue in discovering the key of the
cipher." This is the 1899 editors' own claim to have reconstructed (at least partially) the cipher key from
Fanshaw's garbled attempt -- not a printed plaintext, and not confirmed to cover the whole passage, but a named,
dated clue and a named second location (PRO Spanish Correspondence, Fanshaw's confession letter to Arlington)
that has not yet been chased.

## Key in reach? (grade M lead, never a key)

Tomokiyo's `charlesii.htm` (on disk, `sources/cryptiana/web/charlesii.htm`) independently documents, from *Life
of Dr. John Barwick* (unabridged, Latin and English editions), a **published cipher key Hyde used in exactly
this period**: a 1-63 single-letter + 70-692 syllable/word tabular cipher, which "was enclosed in Hyde's letter
of 12 June 1659" to Barwick -- eight days after our item 1's dateline ([1659], June [4-]14, Brussels) -- with
the full table printed in the Latin *Vita Johannis Barwick* (next to p.316) and described (with typos corrected)
in the unabridged English edition p.408. The same page also lists undeciphered cipher *superscriptions* on
Hyde's letters to Barwick dated Brussels, 29 Sept 1659, 17 Oct 1659, **14 Jan 1660**, and 16 Jan 1660 -- the 14
Jan 1660 date is an exact match (old style) for our item 2's dateline ("[1660], January [14-]24, Brussels"),
though the addressee there is Barwick, not Fanshaw, so this is at most a same-day/same-place parallel, not the
same letter. Neither cipher table has been tried against our two items' transcriptions in this session (no
transcription of the cipher groups exists yet -- HMC 1899 prints only the English gloss "[undeciphered]", not
the cipher figures themselves; getting the actual cipher groups needs the manuscript, via whichever holder has
it). Graded **M, lead only**: a named, dated, published key that fits the period and the sender exactly, not
yet matched to any ciphertext because no ciphertext has been transcribed from either holder.
