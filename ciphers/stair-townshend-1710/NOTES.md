# John Dalrymple, 2nd Earl of Stair, to Viscount Townshend, 27 Feb 1710 (Univ. of Kansas, Spencer Research Library, MS P556)

- **Status:** open.
- **Plaintext language:** English, probably. Both correspondents are British: Stair a Scottish peer and
  British diplomat; Townshend, at this date, ambassador extraordinary to the States General (later Secretary
  of State). Pages 1-2 (clear) were not quoted verbatim by the KU record, so this is inferred from context,
  not confirmed; the subject matter (Danzig, Sweden, Poland, Russia in the Great Northern War) is the kind
  British envoys occasionally discussed partly in French, so confirm from the actual clear pages once a scan
  is obtained.
- **Ciphertext:** none transcribed yet. No digital image is online (checked KU Digital Collections,
  digital.lib.ku.edu, for "P556"/Dalrymple/Stair/Townshend 1710: no match). A scan must be requested; see
  `REQUEST.md`.

Source: https://archives.lib.ku.edu/repositories/3/resources/4655 (collection-level resource; a folder-level

KU's scope note (paraphrased, not a single verbatim block) says the letter discusses the deputy from Danzig,
the Dutch envoy, pretended indifference to Danzig's fate "for fear of Polish jealousy," Count Fleming's
attitude, Poland's reaction to Swedish neutrality, possible Russian involvement in Denmark, Polish army/Council
politics, an envoy named "Fitzdome" and Russia, and the Polish king's inclination against France. This situates
the letter in the endgame of the Great Northern War (Danzig, Poland, Sweden, Russia), the same theatre as
`ciphers/whitworth-1707/`.

Reproductions (source: https://spencer.lib.ku.edu/using-the-library/request-reproductions, checked 20 Sept
2026): free self-scanning in the Reading Room (not publication quality); staff-made copies $0.25/image (PDF
reference), $2.00/image (low-res JPEG), $5.00/image (high-res TIFF), about 3 business days, delivered by email
or a cloud link removed after 30 days. A 3-page item would run $0.75-$15 depending on format.

## Ideas

Page 3 is "largely in code," and the scope note's clear-text content (Danzig, Fleming, "Fitzdome", the Polish
king, Russia) gives topical cribs once the ciphertext is in hand. A clerk's-hand cipher passage inside an
otherwise-clear diplomatic report, written by a named, dateable British envoy, is closer to the "key beside the
letter" or "sibling in a family edition" pattern (`LESSONS.md` §"Look for the sibling") than to a cold
ciphertext-only attack — check Graham 1875 first.

## Print check, 20 Sept 2026

Free check requested by `REQUEST.md` before ordering Kansas scans: is the 27 Feb 1710 letter (or its cipher
page) already printed anywhere. **Result: not found printed, in clear or in cipher, in any source searched.
The Kansas order is still needed.**

**Method.** Internet Archive full-text search (`be-api.us.archive.org/fts/v1/search`) plus direct download and
local `grep` of each book's `_djvu.txt` (per CLAUDE.md "scripts read, models judge" — the model did not read any
volume in full, only grep hits and their surrounding lines). TNA Discovery JSON API
(`discovery.nationalarchives.gov.uk/API/search/records`) for catalogue-level cipher/decipher notes. NRS's own
catalogue (`catalogue.nrscotland.gov.uk`) returned HTTP 403 to curl and was not reachable this session.

1. **J. M. Graham, *Annals and Correspondence... Stair* (1875).** Both volumes on Internet Archive
   (`annalscorrespond01grahiala`, `annalscorrespond02grahiala`, IA-hosted OCR, University of California copy).
   Grepped both full texts case-insensitively for `townshend`, `danzig|dantzic|dantzick`, `fitzdome|fitz.dome`,
   `fleming`, `cipher|cypher|decipher`, and every year `1700`-`1720`. Vol. 1 covers the 2nd Earl's career
   including his winter-1709/spring-1710 mission as envoy-extraordinary to the King of Poland at Warsaw
   (pp. 240-243): it prints one incoming letter (Secretary Boyle to Stair, 31 Mar 1710) and one from Townshend
   to Stair (15 Apr 1710, on the Gertruydenberg conference), both quoting from "Stair MS. Papers, vol. i.", but
   **no outgoing Stair-to-Townshend letter of Feb-Mar 1710 is quoted or summarised**, and none of Danzig,
   Fitzdome, Fleming, or "Polish jealousy" appear anywhere in either volume. Vol. 2 covers 1715 onward (no
   1700s-1710s chapter at all — the earliest February date in it is 1719). Conclusion: Graham 1875 does not
   print this letter, in clear or cipher; the general topical context it gives (Stair envoy at Warsaw,
   Feb-Apr 1710, corresponding with Townshend on the Northern War) matches the KU catalogue description and
   supports the letter's dating and setting, nothing more.

2. **HMC 11th Report, Appendix IV — *Manuscripts of the Marquess Townshend* (1887).** IA identifier
   `manuscriptsofmar00greauoft`, full text downloaded and grepped. This calendar of Townshend's own embassy
   papers turns out to hold an extensive, previously-unrecorded (in this repo) series of 1710 Stair-Townshend
   correspondence from Warsaw and, later, the Flanders camp — entries for **29 Mar 1710** ("a good letter from
   Lord Stair at Warsaw... referring to the difficulties created by... Charles XII", per the report's own
   preface) and **30 Jul / 4 Aug 1710** (Camp at Vilers Brulin, discussing Poland, the North, "General Count
   Fleming prime minister of the King of Poland", Riga, the Czar, Sweden) are printed in full. Both are
   genuine siblings in subject matter to the KU letter, but **neither is the 27 Feb 1710 letter**: the
   calendar's chronological list of the Townshend papers has an entry for Feb 13 and the next for Feb 28, with
   nothing between (checked line-by-line for the whole of Jan-Apr 1710); no entry anywhere is dated 27 Feb;
   "Danzig"/"Fitzdome"/"Polish jealousy" do not occur in the volume at all (the volume's only "Fleming" and
   "cipher" hits are unrelated: a 1609 Fleming and a 1620s Bolingbroke cipher). Conclusion: the Marquess
   Townshend's own retained copy of his 1710 correspondence, as calendared in 1887, does not include this
   letter — consistent with the KU item being a Stair-family (sender's) copy that never reached, or was not
   kept with, Townshend's own papers.

3. **Marlborough despatches (ed. Murray, "Letters and Dispatches of John Churchill... 1702-1712").** The
   1968 reprint on IA (`lettersdispatche0004marl`, `lettersdispatche0005marl`, covering the right years) is
   lending-restricted; `_djvu.txt` returned HTTP 401. The public-domain 1845-edition Google-Books scans found
   under this title (`lettersanddispa00marlgoog`, `lettersanddispa02marlgoog`) turned out, on inspection, to
   cover 1700-1706/1712-13 only, not 1710, and contain zero hits for "Stair" — wrong volumes, not useful. A
   third copy of the volume that does cover 1709-1710 (`in.ernet.dli.2015.512031`, Digital Library of India
   scan) downloaded but its OCR is unusable (garbled, mixed with Devanagari-script noise); zero hits for
   Stair/Townshend/Danzig in it, but that is not a reliable negative given the OCR quality — **not properly
   checked**, flagged for a future worker with access to a better scan or HathiTrust's Bibliographic/EF API
   for this specific volume.

4. **Hardwicke, *Miscellaneous State Papers* (1501-1726).** Not searched directly this pass. Graham's own text
   (vol. 1, line ~16507 of its `_djvu.txt`) cites a *different*, 1720 Stair letter as printed in the Hardwicke
   collection ("Stair's letter to Mr Craggs of Feb 14, 1720"), which is not relevant to the 1710 letter and was

5. **TNA Discovery API**, `sps.searchQuery` combinations `Stair` (dated 1710), `Stair Townshend` (dated
   1709-1711), `decipher` (dated 1710), `GD135 cipher`. No record for a decipher/cipher note on any
   Stair-Townshend item of 1710. Confirmed leads already in this file (SP 87/13, SP 87/5/62 - an unrelated
   intercepted Marlborough-to-Sunderland letter, 14 Apr 1710) but nothing tying a cipher/decipher description
   to Stair's Feb 1710 correspondence. One partly-relevant hit not previously noted: **SP 34/12/2**, "W[illia]m
   Blencowe to George Tilson, concerning his efforts to decipher letters sent to...", dated 1709/10 [Mar 2] —

6. **Incidental finding.** "Fitzdome" is not an invented or garbled name: IA full-text search
   (`be-api.us.archive.org/fts/v1/search?q=Fitzdome`) turns up *The London Gazette*, issue 4811 (17-20 Feb
   1710), reporting news "from Monsieur Fitzdome, his Polish Majesty's Envoy to the Czar of Muscovy" — a real,
   contemporaneous public reference to the same person named in the KU scope note, dated the same week as the
   letter. This corroborates the KU cataloguer's transcription of the name and the letter's dating and
   subject, but is not a printing of the letter itself.
