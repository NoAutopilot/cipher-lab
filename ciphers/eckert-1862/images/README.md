Each item record carries the statement: "For information on use of Digital Library materials, please see Library
Rights and Permissions: https://www.huntington.org/library-rights-permissions". That page (read 19 Sept 2026 via
the Wayback Machine, the live page sits behind a browser check) says: "The Huntington Library does not require
that researchers request permission to quote from or publish material that is in the public domain or for which
The Huntington does not own the copyright, nor does it charge for such activities. The Huntington Library does ask
that researchers identify and cite specific item information when publishing images or quoting from materials
held by the Library." The originals are US government records of 1862 and are in the public domain. Cite as:
Thomas T. Eckert Papers, mssEC 15 / mssEC 67 / mssEC 41 / mssEC 40, The Huntington Library, San Marino,
California. The page transcriptions in `../ciphertext.txt` were made here from these images; the volunteer
transcriptions of Decoding the Civil War (2016-2017), which the Huntington publishes on the item pages, were
used only as a third witness in the reconciliation and are credited there.
