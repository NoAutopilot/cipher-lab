Nulles: `1  89  35  28  0`   (H; the 8 is the looped glyph)

Lines as read (grade H except where noted):
1. "fault notter que a 1 qui sert de nulle il ny fault point mectre de point audessus" -- note that a 1 serving as a null must NOT carry a dot above it.
2. "Aussi fault indiferament metre les 1 par toutes les caractaires combien qu'il soit mis au milieu de deux qui signifieront une lre come vous 78314528 07 Car en effacent le 1 et l'0 vous trouverez aisem[ent] les mot vous" (aisem[ent] abbreviated, M) -- nulls 1 are put indifferently among all characters, even in the middle of two figures that signify one letter; example "vous" = 7831452807; striking the 1 and the 0 gives the word.
3. "Au contraire il ne fault point entrelasser les 89 35 et 28 ains les mectre en telle sorte" -- the nulls 89, 35 and 28 are NOT interleaved inside a letter's figures; they go between letters.
4. Example: "Vous 52 89 67 86 28 53" (written 5289 67 86 2853) = 52 u, [89], 67 o, 86 u, [28], 53 s.

Parsing rule implied (grade M, my reading of the text): the stream is cut into two-figure letter groups; the single figures 1 and 0 are nulls that may fall anywhere, including inside a group (78 3[1]4 52 8[0]7 -> 78 34 52 87 = u o u s, all four values in the alphabet row); the two-figure nulls 89, 35, 28 occur only between groups. A decode must therefore delete every 1 and 0 first (not just those between groups), then read pairs, then drop 89/35/28 pairs. Consequence for the screen: 1 and 0 carry no letters in this key.
