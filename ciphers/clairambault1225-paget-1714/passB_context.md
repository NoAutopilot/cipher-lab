# Pass B — context notes (independent blind transcription)

Transcribed from the 13 images only, in the order given, without consulting any other transcription.

## Letter 1 (f60R–f65L, signed "Paget", "A Genes le 8e Avril 1714")

Paget acknowledges Monseigneur's letter of the 19th of the previous month. An abbé (cipher-coded, apparently
"l'abbé Lomeliny/Lomellini") writes from Venice that he will be there until the end of next month and asks
whether Paget has news touching a cipher-coded name/place ("Gille de Cabarque"?). Paget then describes
negotiations he had with "l'abbé" concerning some group ("les Genois"? unclear, cipher-coded) who wanted a
share of something, who suspected he was only negotiating for one portion, and who demanded a meeting; they
told him they knew of his negotiation with "l'abbé" and wanted the treaty made for everyone; he answered it
was up to them to make their proposal. He remarks that everyone concourt avec empressement à la vente d'une
île (an island's sale, cipher-coded). He then reports at length, in plain French with no cipher, on European
politics: uncertainty in Genoa about whether the Franco-Imperial peace will exclude the King of Sicily
(Victor Amadeus of Savoy) from a clause letting the Emperor make war on him to recover Milanese places ceded
to him, versus a rival rumor that the Emperor will not trouble him in his present possessions. He then relates
at length, still in plain French, the death in Rome of a rich Genoese banker, the "marquis Pallavicin" (Carlo
Pallavicino's natural/bastard son, later legitimated with 100,000 Genoese "croisats" by his father after a
famous scene where the father called him "bastard" and made amends), who died suddenly intestate, leaving a
succession dispute among several claimants (a legitimate nephew/cousin "Estienne Pallavicin," "Carlo Lomelino,"
"Alexandrin Grimaldy" the Republic's envoy at Rome who sealed the house) and a competing claim from the
Apostolic Chamber on grounds of bastardy. He reports the (unrelated) news that quarantine restrictions on
travelers from the German side had almost been relaxed but were kept because of a fresh outbreak of plague in
some German towns, and expresses satisfaction at hearing the France–Empire peace has been signed, hoping it
will end his own "triste estat." He asks Monseigneur to send his "expeditions" so he can go fill the consulate
of the French nation in Sardinia that has been promised him, recalling a memoir on the duties of a consul he
sent in April 1712. He closes reporting that his zeal led him to share with the new vice-consul (a Sr. Auber[t],
in post 5 months) some cipher-coded advice on how to proceed secretly in this country; names this vice-consul
as "Laugier de Toulon," praised as very capable in maritime affairs, of use to the consul unless the vice-
consul's own advantages elsewhere prove insufficient to keep him. Signs "Paget," dated "A Genes le 8e Avril
1714."

## Letter 2 (f65R–f66R, signed "Paget", "a Genes le 28 aoust 1714")

Paget acknowledges three letters from Monseigneur of the 13th of the month, two of which Mr. Aubert has already
answered; he reserves for himself the point Monseigneur wished to know concerning (heavily cipher-coded) "la
Princesse de Parme et ses 2 oncles" — evidently Elisabeth Farnese, discussed as a candidate already regarded
("on reconnoit déja") as future Queen of Spain, "a toutes les belles qualités qu'on peut désirer." The rest of
the letter (f66L–f66R) is a long, almost continuously cipher-coded physical and character portrait of this
princess: she will complete her 22nd year on 25 October next, born the same month in [1692]; well-made,
blonde, of Rome[?], regular features, of average/good height, pretty, of a modest wit and air; no particular
inclinations noted, but complete submission and blind compliance toward "Madame la Duchesse sa Mere," who has
always kept her under close watch; a particular attachment giving her a good education; Vostre Excellence is
reminded that after the death of "cette mere['s husband?]," Mr. le Duc de Parme her husband [i.e. the princess's
father's line] married in second marriage the Prince François Farnese, [now] Duc de Parme regnant, brother of
the deceased, of whom she [the princess] had no [children by him]; and [something] lost the hope of ... The
letter continues (f66R) still heavily in cipher: though this last Duke is only 38, there is also the Prince
Antoine de Parme, another brother of the Duc de Parme, only 35, unmarried and with no inclination for marriage,
so that the young princess seems bound to expect all the wealth of her House, being the sole/unique... beloved
and adored of the two Princes who are charmed to see her marry the King of Spain, all the more as he is issue
of France, for whom they have always shown great partiality; the Duchesse (her mother), on the contrary, is
said to be entirely of German inclination — one pretends the young princess leans rather toward the party of
her uncles on that point than toward her mother's. Signs "Je suis avec très profond respect / Monseigneur de
Vostre Excelence / Le très humble très obeissant serviteur / Paget," dated "a Genes le 28 aoust 1714."

## Oddities / uncertain readings noted while transcribing

- **f61R and f62R both open with the identical two lines** "mais une mort subite l'a surpris n'ayant / donner
  ordre a sa succession, de maniere" under nearly identical crossed pagination stamps ("243" on both). f61R's
  first two lines then jump to an unrelated topic (a second pagination stamp "241" appears mid-page) while
  f62R's continue logically into the Pallavicini narrative that follows f62L. I transcribed both images exactly
  as given (so passB.tsv has this pair of lines twice, once on f61R and once on f62R) rather than silently
  dropping the apparent duplicate — flagging it here per the "never silently repair" rule. My best guess is
  that f61R's crop and f62R's crop overlap at a page boundary, or the volume genuinely bound a stray duplicate
  leaf; I have not tried to resolve which.
- **Old pagination stamps are hard to read** throughout (crossed-out numbers with a looping flourish beside
  them, e.g. "239"/"93" on f60R, "243"/"93" and "241"/"93" on f61R, "245"/"96" on f63R, "281"/"59" on f66R).
  I transcribed my best guess for each with conf=M; several (f61L, f66L) I could not read at all and omitted
  rather than invent a value.
- A short line of text near the bottom of both f62R and f63R that I first read as "emancation a un g..." is,
  on comparison with f64R, almost certainly ghost bleed-through of f64R's real line "...entiere emulation a
  ceux qui les recoivent" (the same phrase showing faintly through the paper on two earlier rectos). I did
  NOT transcribe it as content on f62R/f63R once I recognized this — it is omitted from passB.tsv on those two
  lines.
- **f66L's cipher-number lines run to the right edge of the crop** and several groups are cut off /
  unreadable at the very edge (e.g. the line "38.41. estant 175.34.146.165.158.38.46.95.157..."). The facing
  image f66R has a column of short number/word fragments down its far-left margin that appear to be the same
  text repeated (crop overlap with f66L rather than new content), so I did not transcribe that left-margin
  column as separate content on f66R to avoid double-counting; I used it only informally to sanity-check a few
  of f66L's readings. This means some of f66L's cipher groups may be one or two digits short of the true
  reading — flagged with conf=M throughout that image.
- f66L/f66R are extremely dense with interlinear insertions (small glosses above the cipher groups identifying
  what they stand for, e.g. "la Princesse de Parme et ses 2 oncles", "le Prince Antoine de Parme", "Made la
  Mere") — I attached each to the main line it sits above per the task's convention, but given the density I
  may have mis-attached one or two insertions to the wrong neighboring line, or mis-split where an insertion's
  wording runs on to a second line.
- Many individual cipher digits on f66L/f66R are genuinely hard to distinguish (1 vs 4, 3 vs 8, 0 vs 6) at this
  resolution and in this small a hand; essentially all cipher tokens on these two images are graded conf=M.
  By contrast the cipher groups on f60R, f61L, f61R, f65L and f65R (letter 1's negotiation passages, and
  letter 2's opening) are written larger and more clearly and most are graded conf=H internally, though I still
  marked a handful of individual ambiguous digits M (e.g. "56" vs "145" on f61L_22).
- A handful of ordinary words I was not fully sure of, all marked conf=M in the TSV: "fair" (f61L_04, probably
  "fait"), "necessaires" (f61L_03, abbreviated with a superscript in the manuscript), "paroles" (f62R_13,
  written oddly), "Auber" (f65L_04, cut off — probably "Aubert", confirmed spelled "Aubert" on f65R_05),
  "reguliers" (f66L_06, cut at page edge), "Envers" (f66L_11, uncertain reading of a short word after the
  cipher group).
- I did not transcribe two faint tiny catchword/pen-trial fragments at the bottom right of f63L and f63R
  (a stray "nt" in the margin) — too fragmentary to be confident of a reading.
