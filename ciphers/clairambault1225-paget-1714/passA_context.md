# Pass A context notes — Clairambault 1225, Paget letters, 1714

Independent blind transcription, images only (f60R–f66R). No other transcription was consulted.

## Letter 1 (f60R–f65L, signed "Paget", "A Gennes le 8e Avril 1714")

Paget acknowledges Vostre Excellence's letter of the 19th of last month. He reports that "Mr [l'abbé
Lomeliny]" (a name written as a contemporary interlinear gloss over the cipher group "145.31.67...")
writes again from Venise, staying there until end of next month, and asks whether Paget has news
touching a person glossed "Sr de Labarque". Paget then reports on contacts in Genoa who believe he is
negotiating only for "a Genes" a cession/procuration, involving people glossed "pour traitter avec eux"
and "de Sicile"/"Sile"; he had conferences with "Labbé" [145.31.67, later re-coded 56.31.67 and 145.65.67]
and reports the other party's demands about a share ("une portion") of something, an interview
("entrevue"), and their belief he was negotiating only for a portion involving Sicily. He asks the
addressee's guidance. He then relays local Genoa gossip: general talk about the just-concluded
France–Empire peace (the parties disagree whether the King of Sicily is covered by it, whether the
Emperor reserved the right to make war on him over the Milanese, etc.), the impatience in Genoa to learn
the peace's actual articles, and a long anecdote (f62L–f63R, no cipher at all) about the recent death in
Rome of a rich Genoese banker, the marquis Pallavicin(o) (natural/bastard son of Carlo Pallavicino), his
illegitimate birth, his 100,000-écu ("croisats") gift from his father, his legitimate half-brother who
moved to Rome and married into the Rospigliosi family and died without heirs, and the resulting
succession dispute between the Rospigliosi, the Apostolic Chamber (which claims the estate as that of a
bastard), and other claimants (Carlo Lomelino, Alessandro Grimaldi). Paget then (f64L) reports renewed
plague rumors in Germany and relief at the France–Empire peace, and (f64R) asks Vostre Excellence to send
his "expeditions" so he can go fill the consulate of the nation in Sardaigne, referring to a memoir on
consular duties he had sent in April 1712. Letter 1 closes (f65L) with Paget explaining he passed on to
the new vice-consul (M. Laugier de Toulon, who succeeded "le Sr Aubert" five months earlier) intelligence
about steps needed to penetrate certain matters (cipher, glossed "penetrer ceux" / "y passe en secret"),
and closes with the standard formula and signature "Paget", dated "a Genes le 8e Avril 1714".

## Letter 2 (f65R–f66R, signed "Paget", "a Genes le 28 aoust 1714")

Begins with the "Monseigneur" heading on f65R (after a gap on the page). Paget acknowledges three
letters of the 13th, two of which "Mr Aubert" is answering; he reserves for himself the answer
concerning (heavily enciphered, with the interlinear gloss "la Princesse de Parme et ses 2 oncles")
what appears to be a formal physical/character portrait of a young princess of Parma who is about to
complete her 22nd year on 25 October (the coming birthday), born (per an interlinear insertion) "en mil
six cens quatre vingt douze" (1692) — this matches Elisabeth Farnese (b. 25 Aug 1692), the niece of the
reigning Duke of Parma who in fact married Philip V of Spain in 1714 and is repeatedly glossed here as
intended "pour Reyne d'Espagne". The portrait (f66L, almost solid cipher) describes her face, features,
complexion, height ("la taille belle", glossed "Moyenne"), an "air modeste", a total lack of personal
inclinations but entire submission to her mother the reigning Duchess, an exclusive attachment to her,
and — after the recent death of the mother's [second?] husband the Duke of Parma reigning ("M. le Duc
de Parme son Mari épousa en 2e Nopces le Prince François Farnese") — an account of the succession
within the Farnese family: a younger half-brother/"frere du deffunct" (glossed) who is apparently the
heir presumptive, on whom the princess and her mother's hopes for the family's wealth and the "unique"
remaining Farnese line are said to rest, since the princess herself has had no children by her father's
side, etc. f66R continues in the same register with an interlinear-glossed account of "le Prince
Antoine de Parme", "frere du Duc de Parme qui n'a que 35 ans" and is himself unmarried and without
inclination to marry, and of the two Farnese princes who are said to be charmed to see her marry "le
Roy d'Espagne", "d'autant plus qu'il en est issu de France", for which they have always shown a great
attachment/interest; a passage contrasts "Made la Duchesse" (the mother, "toujours été de genie
Allemand" — German by temperament/extraction) with a rumor ("l'on pretend") that the young princess
leans more to her uncles' party than to her mother's. The letter closes with the standard "Je suis avec
tres profond respect / Monseigneur De Vostre Excellence / Le tres humble tres obeissant serviteur /
Paget", dated "a Genes le 28. aoust 1714."

Given the birth year (1692) and the marriage-to-the-King-of-Spain framing, this second letter is almost
certainly reporting court gossip/intelligence about Elisabeth Farnese in the run-up to her October 1714
proxy marriage to Philip V — a genuinely interesting identification for cribbing, though pass A did not
attempt to decode any cipher group and this is offered only as context.

## Things worth flagging for reconciliation / the orchestrator

1. **f66L's right edge is cut off in the supplied crop.** Several lines on f66L run cipher groups right
   up to the image's physical right margin and are truncated there (e.g. line 1 originally looked like
   it ended "...244.21", line 4 "...95.157", etc.). By good fortune, `f66R.jpg`'s own left margin
   happens to catch a sliver of these same truncated lines (evidently the two page-scans overlap
   slightly at the gutter), so I was able to recover most of the missing endings from f66R's left-edge
   bleed and folded them into the f66L rows below (e.g. line 1 corrected to end "...244.211.220"; line 3
   gets ".32.125" appended; line 4 ends "...157.39.692"; etc.). This recovery is noted per-row with
   conf M on the recovered digits. A second pass should double-check these against the same left-edge
   sliver on f66R.jpg directly (crop roughly x=0-300 of f66R.jpg) rather than trusting my re-reading.
2. **f66L line 5/6 word (kind clear, "Rousse").** After "...blonde, Le Visage" (confirmed from the
   f66R-edge recovery) there is a short capitalized word before "les traits fins et" that I read as
   "Rousse" at conf M; I could not resolve it confidently (it clashes with "blonde" two words earlier,
   so it may not really be a colour word at all — could plausibly be misread and be something else
   entirely, e.g. a short name or "Assez"). Worth a fresh look at the image around f66L y≈705-770,
   x≈0-250.
3. **Repeated/duplicate fragment f61R vs f62R.** f61R opens with a pagenum "743" and two lines ("mais
   une mort subite l'a surpris en n'ayant... / donner ordre a sa succession, de maniere") that exactly
   duplicate the opening of f62R (also stamped "743", full text about the Pallavicini banker's death).
   This is almost certainly just scan/crop overlap between the two page-half images, not a real
   duplicate leaf in the manuscript — but I transcribed both occurrences literally as instructed, so a
   reconciler should not be surprised to see f61R_02/03 match f62R_02/03 nearly verbatim.
4. **Old pagination sequence.** The crossed-out old page-numbers read (with M confidence throughout,
   the ink is faint/crossed and the new replacement numbers beside them are cursive and hard to pin
   down exactly): 739 (f60R), 741 (f61R), 743 (f62R), 745 (f63R), 747 (f64R), 799 or possibly 749 (f65R,
   very unsure — worth rechecking), 781 (f66R, also unsure and appears out of the expected +2 sequence,
   so one of the last two readings is probably wrong). Verso ("L") images show only illegible
   bleed-through in that corner, not a real stamp of their own, so I did not transcribe a pagenum row
   for f61L, f62L, f63L, f64L, f65L, f66L.
5. **Cancelled/struck-through words.** A few short words are struck through and illegible by design
   (e.g. before "quatre vingt douze" on f61R/f62R; before "deux Princes" on f66R). I did not transcribe
   these cancelled words as tokens since their content cannot be read; noted here instead.
6. **One fully illegible cipher group.** f61L line 6, between "175." and "276.", one 2–3 digit cipher
   group is inked/scratched out solid and unrecoverable. Transcribed as token "ILLEGIBLE" (kind=cipher,
   conf M) to preserve the token count/position for reconciliation.
7. **Interlinear glosses reading like a key.** Several interlinear insertions in Letter 1 look like a
   contemporary (or near-contemporary) hand's decode annotations rather than the letter-writer's own
   corrections — e.g. "Mr l'abbé Lomeliny" written directly above "Mr 145.31.67...", "Sr de Labarque"
   above "147.46.146.87. / 232.66.45.204.", "Sile"/"de Sile" above two different cipher groups. These
   look like exactly the kind of crib a solver would want; flagging prominently since this is presumably
   why the target was chosen.
8. **Name echo.** "Carlo Lomelino" appears as a suitor of the Pallavicini inheritance on f63R — note the
   near-identical name to "l'abbé Lomeliny" glossed on f60R/f61L; likely the same Genoese family
   (Lomellini), probably unrelated to the inheritance story but worth cross-checking.
9. Several short function-word glyphs throughout (a tironian-looking "&"/"et" ligature) were
   consistently transcribed as "et" at conf M; the same 5-stroke glyph recurs dozens of times and I
   treated all instances the same way.
10. Old-orthography spellings were kept as written (receu, avois, estoit, sçavoir, desja/deja, vostre,
    etc.); I did not modernize anything.

Not decoded: no attempt was made anywhere to interpret what any cipher group stands for beyond quoting
the interlinear glosses actually written in the manuscript.
