## Verdict
`found-solved`. The paper states: "the \"Manuscrit français 3620\" (fr. 3620) especially contains 73 letters from the King to the Duke from May 1590 to February 1593. However, only the letter written on 1592, September 12 (folios 70-71) is encrypted", and describes a two-page letter whose first page is almost fully encrypted (mostly contiguous digits) and whose second page is in clear. They locate the cipher table in another BnF manuscript (the paper names fr.3995 as the Nevers table volume, fol.141 shown in its Appendix 3) and two copies of the clear first-page text without the postscript: BnF fr.3615 fol.89 (identified only as a "12 September" letter) and fr.4003 fols.10-11, plus the postscript alone at fr.4003 fol.172. Who did not know: the BnF finding aid flags only "avec chiffre" and gives no decipherment; the BnF finding aid's dechiffrement column is empty for this item (record cc500703, snapshot sources/bnf-findingaids/2026-10-07/cc500703.html). What it leaves to hand on: a published decipherment, a key location (fr.3995 fol.141) and clear copies at fr.3615 fol.89 and fr.4003 ff.10-11, so the item is calibration or contribution material, not a reading target.
