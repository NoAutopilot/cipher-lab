# National Coalition of Independent Scholars (NCIS): membership now, research grant later

## 1. The programme

- **What you apply for now:** NCIS membership. It is the gate to NCIS's $599 Research Grants and gives three
  things this project needs at once: an affiliation to name on credit forms that ask for one, letters of
  introduction to archives, and a discounted JPASS (JSTOR's individual subscription).
- **Pages (read 24 Sept 2026):** https://ncis.org/join-ncis/, https://ncis.org/apply/,
  https://ncis.org/levels-qualifications/, https://ncis.org/benefits/, https://ncis.org/research-grants/
- **Deadline:** membership is rolling. Research Grant deadlines are 1 Feb, 1 Jun and 1 Oct each year.
- **Amount and cost:** Research Grant $599. Membership dues (apply page): Full $60 (1 yr), $110 (2 yr), $155
  (3 yr); Associate $50 / $90 / $125; Entry $45 / $80 / $110. "Membership dues must be paid before your
  application is reviewed ... The payment will be refunded if your application is not accepted."
- **Eligibility for the grant, quoted:** "You must be a full member of NCIS (or president of an affiliated partner
  group) in good standing for a minimum of 15 consecutive months prior to the relevant grant application
  deadline." Joining in October 2026 makes 1 Feb 2028 the earliest grant deadline you could meet.
- **Benefits, quoted from the benefits page:** "Use NCIS as their affiliation." "Request a NCIS letter of
  introduction to access collections and other resources." "Obtain a discounted JPASS, JSTOR's individual
  subscription" (search results give $99 for 12 months; confirm on joining).
- **Membership levels, quoted:** Full: "Independent scholars who have demonstrated their involvement and
  commitment in any field through peer-reviewed publications, juried artistic endeavors, or similar activities."
  Associate: for those with work accepted but not yet published, or near-complete doctoral work. Entry: bachelor's
  degree holders who show "high-quality research endorsed by a verified scholar", with "preprints, journal
  submissions, or artistic output" and "a written recommendation and contact information" from a scholar with an
  advanced degree in the field.
- **Fit:** strong on purpose, uncertain on level. Which level you qualify for depends on your own record, which
  this session cannot see. Only Full members can apply for the grant, so if you join at Entry or Associate, the
  grant is further off, but the affiliation and letters of introduction still apply (confirm per level when you
  apply).

## 2. The application form

The form sits at https://ncis.org/apply/ and asks for payment first; its fields were not visible to this session.
From the pages: an application form in English ("Foreign titles, etc. must be in the original language and
translated to English"), payment of at least one year's dues, and evidence that "reflect[s] an appropriate level of
research activity as an independent scholar", verifiable by NCIS. Expect: contact details, level applied for,
education, a statement of research, publications or equivalent output, and for Entry a recommender.

**Step for you or a local agent with you watching:** open the form, copy its field list and limits into this
section, then fill it from section 3.

## 3. Drafted copy

### 3a. Statement of research (about 200 words; trim to the form's limit)

> I am an independent researcher working on historical cryptography. Since September 2026 I have run cipher-lab,
> an open project that locates, transcribes and reads undeciphered cipher letters of the 15th to 19th centuries in
> European and American archives, using AI agent sessions under a written verification protocol. Every decoded
> token is graded by its source, every negative result has a matched control, and a separate verifier searches
> printed editions, catalogues and scholarship before any reading is described as without prior decipherment.
> Fifteen cipher passages from seven groups of letters (1530 to 1864, from the BnF, the Huntington Library and the
> Nassau and Saxon correspondence of William of Orange) have been classed "no prior decipherment located after a
> logged search", each after two audits; thirteen candidate items were shown to be already solved; a catalogue
> correction has gone to the BnF, and keys and readings have been offered to the Huntington Library. All transcriptions, keys, readings, tools and audit logs are
> public at https://github.com/NoAutopilot/cipher-lab. Membership would give the project an affiliation for
> archive and database access, letters of introduction to the archives whose copies I order, and JSTOR access for
> the verifier's scholarship searches.

### 3b. Research output to list (use what the form's publications field allows)

- cipher-lab repository (2026), https://github.com/NoAutopilot/cipher-lab: transcriptions, keys, readings and
  audit files for each item; 33 shared tools with offline tests.
- Audit files, for example `ciphers/fr2980-gramont/AUDIT.md` (Gramont 1530) and `ciphers/eckert-1864/AUDIT.md`
  (Union telegrams 1864).
- Contributions handed on, as logged in `CONTRIBUTIONS.md`: sign table for Bowes 1583 to S. Tomokiyo (Cryptiana);
  catalogue correction for BnF Dupuy 468 f.28; Eckert keys and readings to the Huntington Library.
- [your other publications, preprints or talks]

### 3c. Recommender (Entry level only)

Someone with an advanced degree in a related field who knows the work. Candidates are the people the project
already corresponds with through the outreach drafts (for example S. Tomokiyo), once they have seen the results.
Send them `project-one-pager.md`.
