I run a small non-commercial project that reads cipher passages in historical letters. I send every message myself; the reading of the images, the searches of the printed editions and the checks are done by AI agents (Claude models) working in an open repository, where every step is logged.

My question is about George W. Erving, the American consul and chargé who wrote to James Monroe from London, Lisbon and Madrid in 1804-1807, sometimes in the State Department's numbered code. Our notes say that Erving's letterbook copies of his letters are among the Society's holdings, possibly in the Winthrop Family Papers, but I have not been able to confirm that from the catalogue. Could you tell me whether the Society holds an Erving letterbook covering 1804-1807, and under which collection?

If it does, I would like reference images, within the Society's free allowance of 25, of the copies of three letters to Monroe: London, 23 September 1804; a letter of about November-December 1804 (undated in the 1904 Library of Congress calendar of the Monroe papers); and 24 February 1807. We have looked for the letters Monroe received on the Library of Congress microfilm of his papers (the expected ranges of reels 3 and 4, read frame by frame for the 1807 letter and sampled for the 1804 ones) and have not found them, so a retained copy is the most direct remaining route. A copy in clear or in the code would both help.

What we have done that concerns this correspondence: we transcribed the coded lines of Erving's letter to Monroe of 23 May 1806 (Library of Congress, Monroe Papers, reel 3) and read them with the published code of the period. The same passage had already been deciphered and printed by Irving Brant in James Madison: Secretary of State (1953), and our reading agrees with his word for word. The transcription, the key and the audit are here: https://github.com/NoAutopilot/cipher-lab/tree/main/ciphers/erving-monroe-1806

Images would be used for research only. Any reading would be posted in the same open repository with the Society's credit line, as you prefer it.
