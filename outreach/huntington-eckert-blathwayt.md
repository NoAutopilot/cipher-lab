Dear Reference Services,

I run a small project that reads cipher passages in digitised letters, with each reading checked by a separate search
for any prior decipherment. Two groups of items in your collections are involved, and I would like to hand you the
results and ask whether they are of use.

- Eckert papers, mssEC 41 (the 1864 cipher book) and mssEC 19: two telegrams to General Butler of 21 and 22 April
  1864, Fox to Butler and Meigs to Butler, read with the book's key. Their substance is in print from the clear copies
  (Official Records and Butler's correspondence); what we add is the cipher form, the key as a machine-readable table,
  and twenty 1864 ledger entries read with it. No prior decipherment of the two telegrams was located in the Official
  Records, Butler's and Lincoln's editions, the Decoding the Civil War project pages or the catalogues.
- Blathwayt papers addenda, mssBLA 186 and 191(a), Madrid and Port St Mary intelligence of 1728-29, read with a key
  rebuilt from seven items in the same run that carry contemporary decipherments (395 groups). The clear part of BLA 186
  is printed by Rose (Marchmont Papers, 1831, ii 414-15) with the cipher shown only as "(Cypher.)"; the cipher gives an
  agent's request for orders and Pareti's report on Ripperda's escape. No prior decipherment located in the Huntington
  and HMC catalogues, Rose, or full-text search.

Every reading is regenerated by a script from our transcription and the key, and every token is graded (H from a key
source, C from known plaintext, M uncertain). Folders with the keys, readings and the full search log:
https://github.com/noautopilot/cipher-lab/tree/main/ciphers/eckert-1864 and
https://github.com/noautopilot/cipher-lab/tree/main/ciphers/huntington-blathwayt-madrid-1728 (AUDIT.md in each). If the
Library or the project already holds a decipherment of any of these, we would be glad to know and will correct our notes.

If it would help, we can supply the mssEC 41 key, the ledger readings and the Blathwayt key in whatever format your
catalogue or the Decoding the Civil War project takes, and try any cipher books, telegrams or letters in your
collections you would like read the same way.
