Dear Klingenstein Library reference staff,

I direct a small open project on unsolved historical ciphers and send its letters myself; AI agents (Claude models) do the reading, the searches and the checking, and every step is logged in the public repository linked below. We are trying to read a letter John Armstrong (1758-1843), U.S. minister in Paris, sent to James Madison on 20 February 1808. It is in a numeral cipher mixed with shorthand-like signs that the State Department could not read; Madison thought it was a cipher "concerted with another correspondent". In 1805 Armstrong is on record as keeping a private cipher with James Monroe, so he did keep ciphers of his own outside the Department's.

The Society holds the largest body of Armstrong's own papers that we know of (about 200 letters 1777-1843, a microfilm of privately owned letters 1798-1833, a letterbook of 1804 and photostats of other collections, as the U.S. House's history office lists them). No finding aid for them was reachable from where we work.

Could you tell me whether the Armstrong papers contain any cipher key or numbered code table, any letter or draft of 1805-1808 written partly in numbers, a retained copy or draft of the 20 February 1808 letter, or a letterbook later than 1804? If something of the kind is there, I would be grateful for a quote for scans of those items only (a quote, please; I will confirm before ordering).

A key or a second letter in the same cipher is the one thing that could make the 1808 letter readable. What we have tried so far is at https://github.com/NoAutopilot/cipher-lab/tree/main/ciphers/armstrong-madison-1808
