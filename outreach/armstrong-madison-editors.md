Dear Papers of James Madison team,

We are a small volunteer group working, with S. Tomokiyo's encouragement, on John Armstrong's cipher letter to James
Madison of 20 February 1808 (Founders Online 99-01-02-2728; NARA RG 59, M34 roll 14, frames 0030-0033). We have not
read it. We are writing to ask one question, and to pass on what we have ruled out in case it is useful to you.

The question: do you know of any other letter written in this cipher, or carrying the same shorthand-like marks among
the numbers -- in Armstrong's papers, or in the papers of the correspondent Madison had in mind when he wrote to
Jefferson on 15 May 1808 that the cipher "must be one concerted with another correspondent"? With 369 number groups,
more than a third of them occurring only once, the letter cannot be read from its own text alone; a second letter in
the same code is the single thing most likely to open it.

What we have checked, each with the control and the numbers on file in the repository linked below:
- A fresh two-pass transcription from the manuscript images (0.957 agreement with the Founders transcription; the
  differences are listed).
- The office code Armstrong used with Madison (THE 972) and Monroe's code (WE028) do not fit it, against permuted and
  shuffled controls.
- Six other coded Armstrong letters of Dec 1807 to Mar 1808 (the "confidentially sent" batch docketed on roll 14,
  frame 0645) and one of 4 May 1806 all screen as THE 972, not as this code.
- Robert R. Livingston's five letters to Madison of 1807-1809 and every page of the Armstrong-Jefferson letters of
  1807-1809 in the Library of Congress are in clear.
- The marks among the numbers match none of ten period English shorthand systems we could compare them with.

If you know of such a letter, a key, a later decipherment, or who the "other correspondent" was, we would be very
grateful for a pointer. Everything we have, including the negatives, is open at
https://github.com/NoAutopilot/cipher-lab/tree/main/ciphers/armstrong-madison-1808 .
