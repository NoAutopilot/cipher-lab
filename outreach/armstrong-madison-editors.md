Dear Papers of James Madison editors,

I run a small open project on unsolved historical ciphers. I direct it and send its letters myself; AI agents (Claude models) do the reading, the edition searches and the checking, every step logged in the repository linked below. With S. Tomokiyo's encouragement we have been working on John Armstrong's cipher letter to Madison of 20 February 1808 (Founders Online 99-01-02-2728; NARA RG 59, M34 roll 14, frames 0030-0033). We have not managed to read it, and I have one question for you.

Do you know of any other letter in this cipher, or carrying the same shorthand-like marks among the numbers? Either in Armstrong's own papers, or in those of the correspondent Madison had in mind when he told Jefferson on 15 May 1808 that the cipher "must be one concerted with another correspondent". The letter has 369 number groups and over a third of them occur only once, so it cannot be read from its own text. A second letter in the same code is the one thing most likely to open it.

So that you know what we have already tried: we made a fresh transcription from the manuscript images; the office codes Armstrong and Monroe used with Madison do not fit it; six other coded Armstrong letters of 1806-1808 (among them the four we could locate of the six listed on the docket at roll 14, frame 0645) look, on a one-line sample each, like his ordinary office code rather than this one; five Livingston-to-Madison letters of 1807-1809 and the Armstrong-Jefferson letters of 1807-1809 at the Library of Congress are in clear; and a comparison of the marks with ten period English shorthand systems identified none of them, though that is a comparison, not a conclusive exclusion.

If you know of such a letter, a key, a later decipherment, or who the "other correspondent" was, a pointer would help us a great deal. Everything we have, including the dead ends, is at https://github.com/NoAutopilot/cipher-lab/tree/main/ciphers/armstrong-madison-1808
