Dear Papers of James Madison team,

One person directs this project and sends this message; the reading, the searches of the editions and the audits
are done by AI agents (Claude models) working in an open repository, every step logged. We work, with S. Tomokiyo's
encouragement, on John Armstrong's cipher letter to James Madison of 20 February 1808 (Founders Online
99-01-02-2728; NARA RG 59, M34 roll 14, frames 0030-0033). We have not read it, and write to ask one question and
pass on what we have checked.

The question: do you know of any other letter in this cipher, or carrying the same shorthand-like marks among the
numbers -- in Armstrong's papers, or in the papers of the correspondent Madison meant when he wrote to Jefferson on
15 May 1808 that the cipher "must be one concerted with another correspondent"? With 369 number groups, over a
third occurring only once, the letter cannot be read from its own text; a second letter in the same code is the
single thing most likely to open it.

What we have checked, with the numbers on file in the repository linked below: a fresh transcription from the
manuscript images; that the office codes Armstrong and Monroe used with Madison do not fit it; that six other coded
Armstrong letters of 1806-1808 (among them the four we could locate of the six listed on the docket at roll 14,
frame 0645) screen, on a one-line sample each, as his ordinary office code, not as this one; that five
Livingston-to-Madison letters of 1807-1809 and the Armstrong-Jefferson letters of 1807-1809 in the Library of
Congress are in clear; and that a comparison of the marks among the numbers with ten period English shorthand
systems identified none of them (a comparison, not a conclusive exclusion).

If you know of such a letter, a key, a later decipherment, or who the "other correspondent" was, we would be very
grateful for a pointer. Everything we have, including the negatives, is open at
https://github.com/NoAutopilot/cipher-lab/tree/main/ciphers/armstrong-madison-1808 .
