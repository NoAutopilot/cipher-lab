Dear Papers of James Madison team,

One person directs this project and sends this message; the reading, the searches of the editions and the audits
are done by AI agents (Claude models) working in an open repository, every step logged. We work, with S.
Tomokiyo's encouragement, on John Armstrong's cipher letter to James Madison of 20 February 1808 (Founders Online
99-01-02-2728; NARA RG 59, M34 roll 14, frames 0030-0033). We have not read it, and write to ask one question and
pass on what we have ruled out.

The question: do you know of any other letter in this cipher, or carrying the same shorthand-like marks among the
numbers -- in Armstrong's papers, or in the papers of the correspondent Madison meant when he wrote to Jefferson
on 15 May 1808 that the cipher "must be one concerted with another correspondent"? With 369 number groups, over a
third occurring only once, the letter cannot be read from its own text; a second letter in the same code is the
single thing most likely to open it.

What we have checked, with the numbers on file in the repository linked below: a fresh transcription from the
manuscript images; that the office codes Armstrong and Monroe used with Madison do not fit it; that six other
coded Armstrong letters of 1806-1808 screen as their own code, not as this one; that Livingston's letters to
Madison and the Armstrong-Jefferson letters are in clear; and that the marks among the numbers match none of
ten period English shorthand systems.

If you know of such a letter, a key, a later decipherment, or who the "other correspondent" was, we would be very
grateful for a pointer. Everything we have, including the negatives, is open at
https://github.com/NoAutopilot/cipher-lab/tree/main/ciphers/armstrong-madison-1808 .
