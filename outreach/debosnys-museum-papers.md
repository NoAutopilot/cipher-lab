# Draft email: Adirondack History Museum, step 2 (the full list of the Debosnys papers)

## Text

Dear Adirondack History Museum,

Thank you for your reply of 28 September and for the scans of Henry Debosnys's writings. We are using them for reference and research only, as you asked, and nothing from them will be shared or published without your permission. I direct this project and send its letters myself; AI agents (Claude models) do the reading, the searches and the checking, and every step is logged in the open repository linked below.

I would like to ask whether the museum could tell me what Debosnys material it holds in full, in the form of a list or finding aid if one exists. Three things make me think there may be more than we have seen:

1. The published cryptogram pages carry numbers: "No. 9", "No. 10", and a note "No. 11 (next page, la suite)". That suggests a numbered series, and I would like to know whether any other numbered pages survive.
2. Cheri Farnsworth's book Adirondack Enigma (2010) credits several items to the Collection of Brewster Memorial Library / Essex County Historical Society, including his foolscap autobiographical notes (one titled "French War 1870", with notes in the margins), a sketch of a family estate in England, and a jailhouse "Autographs" book.
3. A 1933 note in the Ticonderoga Sentinel (18 May 1933) mentions a "Debosnys document", which may say how some of the papers reached the collection.

If a list exists, a copy would help us more than anything else at this stage: it would tell us which pages to ask about rather than asking for everything. For any cipher pages that are not among the scans you have already sent, I would also ask whether they could be scanned under the same restricted terms, and whether there is a fee.

Thank you for your time and your help. What we have so far is at https://github.com/NoAutopilot/cipher-lab/tree/main/ciphers/debosnys-1883
