Dear FDR Library research staff,

I direct a small open project on unsolved historical ciphers and send its letters myself; AI agents (Claude models) do the reading, the searches and the checking, and every step is logged in the public repository linked below. We are trying to read a letter John Armstrong, U.S. minister in Paris, sent to James Madison on 20 February 1808, written in a numeral cipher mixed with shorthand-like signs that the State Department could not read. Madison thought it was a cipher "concerted with another correspondent". In 1805 Armstrong is on record as keeping a private cipher with James Monroe.

Your Library's guide to the Hudson River Valley manuscript collections (Appendix I) describes the Aldrich Family Papers, 1770-1895, one microfilm roll of the Rokeby Collection: about 200 items, most on John Armstrong, over half on his French ministry, with correspondents including Madison, Monroe and Talleyrand; filmed in 1966 for the National Historical Publications Commission, with access restricted by permission of the collection's owners.

Could you tell me, first, whether the positive film at the Library may be consulted or copied by a researcher at a distance, and whose permission that needs; and second, whether the roll has an item list showing any cipher key, numbered code table, or letter or draft of 1805-1808 written partly in numbers? If copies are possible, I would be grateful for a quote for the relevant frames (a quote only, please; I will confirm before ordering).

A key or a second letter in the same cipher is the one thing that could make the 1808 letter readable. What we have tried so far is at https://github.com/NoAutopilot/cipher-lab/tree/main/ciphers/armstrong-madison-1808
