Dear Reference Services,

I am following up on my message of 24 September about the Eckert telegram ledgers, and on a note I sent on
20 September to the curator of Decoding the Civil War. I should have said plainly then how the work is done: I direct
the project and send every message, and the transcription, decipherment, searches of the printed editions and the
audits are done by AI agents (Claude models) working in an open repository where every step is logged and can be
re-run.

My message of 24 September mentioned twenty entries of the 1864 ledger. This is where our reading stands now:

- mssEC 19 (1864 sent ledger): 48 entries read through -- 20 with Cipher No. 1 (mssEC 41), 11 with Cipher No. 2
  (mssEC 47), and 17 January-March 1864 entries with the filled-in book mssEC 67 (13 of those 17 so far from the
  volunteers' transcription only, not yet checked against the page image). All but about 25 of the some 740 code
  words in them are read straight from the books; the rest are clerks' slips and words not in the books, read from
  the context or the printed copy and marked as such. 38 of the 48 are printed in clear in the Official Records, and
  they agree apart from clerks' slips, rounded times and three differences we have logged rather than corrected
  (a troop figure, a rank and one word).
- mssEC 15 (1862 sent ledger): 10 entries read against their clear copies in the Official Records, from which the
  1862 key was rebuilt.
- mssEC 18 (1864-65 sent ledger): from the volunteers' transcription, 45 entries whose every code word is in Cipher
  No. 1 or No. 2, and more in part.

One observation may interest you. In mssEC 18, 18 entries read with neither cipher book, although each lines up with a
telegram of the same date printed in the Official Records. Among them, 14 code words that are in none of the cipher books we have used each stand
opposite the same word of the printed text in two different telegrams, all dated December 1864 to July 1865. This is
a descriptive count, not yet a tested result, but it points to a later code book or key list in use from about the
end of 1864. Does the Library hold such a book among the Eckert papers, perhaps one that is not yet digitised?

The question in my note of 20 September still stands: was a dataset linking the coded telegrams to their cipher
book, key and plaintext ever completed by the project or since? If not, we would be glad to build one for the whole run and give it to you in
whatever format suits the catalogue. The keys, readings and search logs are here:
https://github.com/noautopilot/cipher-lab/tree/main/ciphers/eckert-1864 and
https://github.com/noautopilot/cipher-lab/tree/main/ciphers/eckert-1862
