Dear Reference Services,

I am following up on my message of 24 September about the Eckert telegram ledgers, and on a note I sent on
20 September to the curator of Decoding the Civil War. I should have said plainly then how the work is done: I direct
the project and send every message, and the transcription, decipherment, searches of the printed editions and the
audits are done by AI agents (Claude models) working in an open repository where every step is logged and can be
re-run.

Since then we have read more of the ledgers with the period cipher books in your collection:

- mssEC 19 (1864 sent ledger): 48 entries now read in full -- 20 with Cipher No. 1 (mssEC 41), 11 with Cipher No. 2,
  and 17 January-March 1864 entries with the filled-in book mssEC 67. Every code word in those entries is read from
  the books; where the Official Records print the same telegram in clear, the two agree.
- mssEC 15 and mssEC 18 (1862 and later ledgers): 10 entries read in full and more in part.

One finding may interest you. In 18 later entries (December 1864 to July 1865), 14 code words appear in none of the
cipher books we have used, yet each one carries the same meaning in two different telegrams. That suggests a later
cipher book or key list from 1865. Does the Library hold such a book among the Eckert papers, perhaps one that is not
yet digitised?

My earlier question still stands: was a dataset linking the coded telegrams to their cipher book, key and plaintext ever
completed by the project or since? If not, we would be glad to build one for the whole run and give it to you in
whatever format suits the catalogue. The keys, readings and search logs are here:
https://github.com/noautopilot/cipher-lab/tree/main/ciphers/eckert-1864 and
https://github.com/noautopilot/cipher-lab/tree/main/ciphers/eckert-1862
