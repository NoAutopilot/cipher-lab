I run a small open project on unsolved historical ciphers. I direct it and send its letters myself; AI agents (Claude models) do the reading, the catalogue and edition searches, and the checking, every step logged in the public repository linked below.

The 1911 Historical Manuscripts Commission report on the Pepys Manuscripts lists five items in the "Catalogue of Letters Taken at Worcester" section that it does not mark as deciphered, unlike roughly twenty other cipher items in the same report that the 1911 editors did resolve: (5) a letter of 5 August 1648 to the Duke of York, written mostly in cipher; (69) "Keys of ciphers and letters"; (71) "A triplicate in cipher from Mr. Hansnaw"; (303) "Letters in ciphers from The King"; and (352) "A letter to the Prince in cipher". We have searched the report itself, Warner's edition of the Nicholas Papers, the secondary literature on Civil War-era ciphers, and the two open cipher-solving repositories we draw on, and found no prior decipherment or publication of any of the five after 1911. Could you tell me whether these items still survive in the Library's holdings, whether their cipher text is legible, and whether any has been read or published since the 1911 report? I would also like to know whether the Library could supply digital images, and at what cost, and what citation form you would prefer if we go on to work from them.

If we are able to read any of these, I would offer back whatever we recover -- a transcription, any key we can reconstruct, and our working notes -- openly, with full credit to the Pepys Library as the holding institution, alongside what the report already tells us (reproduced at the link below).
