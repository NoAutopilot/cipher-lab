Dear Mario,

Thank you. As before, the work is done by AI agents (Claude models) in our open repository; I direct it and send these messages.

To your question: recognition only, a credit line in the Digital Library record of each item you use, for example "Decipherment: Cipher Lab, 2026, https://github.com/NoAutopilot/cipher-lab". Nothing further: no fee, no exclusivity. Our readings, keys and notes are already public under an open licence (MIT) that asks only for attribution.

Where we stand: 68 telegrams whose text we did not find in the Official Records or the other editions searched, 52 in mssEC 19, 3 in mssEC 18 and 13 in the Fort Monroe ledger (mssEC 25), read wholly or in part with your own cipher books mssEC 41, 47 and 67 (the list gives each one's depth). Much of each telegram is plain in the ledger and so already in your volunteers' transcription; what we add is the meaning of the code words, often names, places, figures and times. In 35, which the list marks, our audits judged that this adds less: most of the body is already clear, or a printed reply, order or outcome gives the topic. For the Blathwayt papers: partial readings of the cipher passages of mssBLA 186 (20 of 24 groups; Rose printed an English extract of its clear text, without the cipher, in the Marchmont Papers, 1831, ii 414-15) and 191(a) (about three-quarters of its 141 groups), with a key rebuilt from seven other items' contemporary decipherments. Copies we have not seen may carry the text: the telegrams' file copies at the U.S. National Archives and in the senders' and recipients' papers, and the copy of 191(a) sent to Newcastle.

On correctness: a script regenerates each reading from the transcribed cipher text (read from your page images, in one case from your volunteers' transcription only) and the book or key; every code word or group is graded (from the book or key, from a printed or period decipherment, cryptanalytic, inferred, uncertain, unread); and every item has had two separate searches for a prior decipherment or printed text. The list: https://github.com/NoAutopilot/cipher-lab/blob/main/outreach/huntington-decipherments-list-2026-10-08.tsv; the search logs: https://github.com/NoAutopilot/cipher-lab/blob/main/ciphers/eckert-1864/AUDIT.md and https://github.com/NoAutopilot/cipher-lab/blob/main/ciphers/huntington-blathwayt-madrid-1728/AUDIT.md; Rose's volume: https://archive.org/details/selectionfrompap02roseiala. I can also send a spreadsheet giving, per telegram, the page, date, cipher text, reading, uncertain words and searches.

Your catalogue counts about 2,500 telegrams in these three ledgers alone, and the other Eckert ledgers hold more, so I would suggest stages, starting with these three ledgers' entries to late December 1864, when the main books we have were in use; for those in print we can give the Official Records reference.
