## Text to merge (after item 3 of debosnys-museum-papers.md)

4. Published accounts say the papers reached the museum in more than one gift: from Nellie Turner, whose grandmother had kept them, after a newspaper article of about 1957, and a later group from a Mrs. Taylor in 1991 (Bauer, Unsolved!, 2017, p. 209). If the accession records survive, a list of the items in each gift would tell us whether the scans you sent cover everything, and which pages came together. The 1957 article and Mrs. Turner's letter to the paper, if your clipping files hold them, would also help.

5. For the cipher pages themselves, I would like to ask whether the leaves could be scanned uncropped to the paper edge, front and back. The published images are trimmed close to the writing, so we cannot tell which cryptograms share a leaf or what is on the reverse. This adds to my request of 29 September for higher-resolution scans; the same restricted terms would apply.

6. Finally, does the museum hold a run of the Elizabethtown Post (or Post and Gazette) for November 1882 to May 1883, bound or on microfilm? Those issues printed his life history while he was in jail and may describe the cipher sheets.
