Dear Manuscripts and Archives Division reference staff,

I direct a small open project on unsolved historical ciphers and send its letters myself; AI agents (Claude models) do the reading, the searches of the printed editions and the checking, and every step is logged in the public repository linked below. We are working on a letter John Armstrong, U.S. minister in Paris, sent to James Madison on 20 February 1808, written in a numeral cipher that the State Department could not read. Madison wrote to Jefferson on 15 May 1808 that "the undecyphered letter from A. was probably misaddressed" and was in a cipher "concerted with another correspondent"; an editor of the Papers of James Madison reads that remark as referring to the 20 February letter.

We found a precedent in the printed Madison Papers. On 2 April 1805 Armstrong told Madison that a copy of his letter of 18 March 1805 to James Monroe, then at Madrid, had gone to Washington in "the cypher established between him [Monroe] and me" instead of the Department's code (Founders Online, Madison Papers, Secretary of State Series 9, Armstrong to Madison 18 March and 2 April 1805). Monroe's incoming letters from his Madrid mission are partly at the New York Public Library (the editors cite, for example, Erving to Monroe, 2 March 1805, to the NYPL James Monroe papers), and the Library of Congress's own Monroe finding aid places Monroe's letterbook for November 1804 to May 1805 at the NYPL. We could not establish where the originals of Armstrong's March-April 1805 letters to Monroe are; the Library of Congress Monroe papers we checked do not seem to have them.

I would like to ask two things about the James Monroe papers (MssCol 2035). First, whether the collection holds Armstrong's letters to Monroe of 12 and 18 March and 1 and 5 April 1805, any other Armstrong letter to Monroe partly in cipher (1804-1808), and anything that looks like a cipher key or table shared by the two men, together with Monroe's letterbook for November 1804 to May 1805. Second, if they are there, what it would cost to have scans made of those items (a quote only, please; I will confirm before any order).

A key, or a second letter in the same cipher, is the one thing that could make the 1808 letter readable. Everything we have done so far, including what we ruled out, is at https://github.com/NoAutopilot/cipher-lab/tree/main/ciphers/armstrong-madison-1808
