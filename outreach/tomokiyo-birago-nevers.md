Dear Mr Tomokiyo,

A short follow-up in our thread. As before, I direct this project and send its messages; the transcription, the tests and the searches were done by AI agents (Claude models) working in the open repository linked below, with every step logged.

Thank you, above all, for the Ceppo-Nevers table on your Nevers page (the image nevers_add1.png, rebuilt from Ceppo's letters in BnF fr.4702, fols.36-37). It is your key that reads these letters; all we did was apply it.

We applied it to four of Lodovico Birago's letters to the duc de Nevers from Saluzzo that your page lists without a decipherment (BnF fr.3251). Each was transcribed without seeing the key's values, and on each your printed key beats all 200 shuffled keys. Three give short passages:

- f.11r (no.6, 14 September 1570): the word fragments "presidente", "mandato", "curare", "estitucio", "chuni", "ochi" and "sauoia"; none of 2,000 chance decodes gives more than one word of that length.
- f.21v (no.11, 12 October 1570): "questa carica ... di qua", "credo le ne", "ceder uiuendo et seruend", "tanto di", "fatto", "fa dificu"; no chance decode of 2,000 gives more than two words of six letters (the real decode gives four), and a blind reader picked the real decode out of 40. The letter's clear text is cited and quoted by A. Pascal, Il Marchesato di Saluzzo e la Riforma protestante (1960); we found no reading of its cipher passages.
- f.87 (no.45, 9 May 1571): two short fragments, "un giorno auanti" and "ne ando secre-amente", where the r in each rests on the double-barred oval (below).

f.35 (no.18, 15 November 1570) was tried too: the key fits it, but its two cipher lines give only isolated scraps, and we claim no reading of it. No continuous text is read in any of the four.

On the two uncertain signs: the letter of 5 April 1571 in fr.3252 (fol.36, catalogued "avec chiffre et déchiffrement") turned out to be in the same key, and its period glosses give the double-barred oval as r. The pound-shaped sign your table prints under m is still uncertain; we have not found a gloss on it.

May I ask one thing? Do you know any of these letters, or any decipherment of them?

We are also still working on Armstrong's 1808 letter to Madison, as you recommended. Nothing is read yet; one lead we are following is the private cipher Armstrong says he shared with Monroe in 1805, though we have not found a specimen of it and nothing so far ties it to the 1808 letter.
