Dear Mr Tomokiyo,

A short follow-up in our thread. As before, I direct this project and send its messages; the transcription, the tests and the searches were done by AI agents (Claude models) working in the open repository linked below, with every step logged.

Thank you first for the Ceppo-Nevers table on your Nevers page (the image nevers_add1.png, rebuilt from BnF fr.4702 fol.37). It is your key that reads this letter; all we did was apply it.

We applied it to Lodovico Birago's letter to the duc de Nevers, Saluzzo, 14 September 1570 (BnF fr.3251 f.11r, no.6), which your page lists without a decipherment. On a transcription made without seeing the key's values, your printed key alone gives the word fragments "presidente", "mandato", "curare", "estitucio", "chuni", "ochi" and "sauoia". As controls, the printed key ranks first against 200 shuffled keys, and none of 2,000 chance decodes (shuffled keys, or your key on a shuffled sign order) gives more than one word of that length. No continuous text is read, two passages are not read at all, and two signs remain uncertain: the pound-shaped sign your table prints under m, which the context seems to want as l, and a double-barred oval that has no cell in the table. We found no prior reading of this letter; the search is logged in the audit linked below.

May I ask two things? Do you know this letter, or any decipherment of it? And is the letter of 5 April 1571 in fr.3252 (fol.36, no.24, which the BnF catalogue describes as "avec chiffre et déchiffrement") one you have looked at? It falls inside your Ceppo-Nevers window, and if its decipherment is legible it may settle those two signs.

We are also still working on Armstrong's 1808 letter to Madison, as you recommended. Nothing is read yet; one lead we are following is the private cipher Armstrong says he shared with Monroe in 1805, though we have not found a specimen of it and nothing so far ties it to the 1808 letter.
