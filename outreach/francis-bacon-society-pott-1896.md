Dear Francis Bacon Society,

I direct a small open research project on unread historical cipher letters and send every message myself; the searches of the printed sources and the catalogue checks behind this enquiry were done by AI agents (Claude models) working in an open repository, every step logged at https://github.com/noautopilot/cipher-lab/tree/main/ciphers/lambeth-bacon-649 .

I am writing to ask whether a manuscript once in the Society's possession still survives. In Baconiana no. 74 (June 1928, p. 284), the editor answers a correspondent: "The letter from 'T. Moresin' in French to Anthony Bacon containing cypher ... Reference -- No. 649. Tenison MSS. Lambeth Palace Library. Mrs. C. M. Pott deciphered and translated this letter in 1896, and her MS. is in our possession" (https://archive.org/details/baconiana_1928-06_19_74).

We also found what appears to be Mrs Pott's own printed account of that work: "Numbers Ten and Eleven", signed C. M. P., Baconiana vol. V (new series) no. 17, January 1897, pp. 23-29 (https://archive.org/details/1897-vol-v-new-series-no-17-20/page/23/mode/1up). It describes "a remarkable French letter written in cipher (figures)" among the Lambeth MSS., gives some of the cipher values and a few phrases of the French, and says she hoped "to reproduce this curious and enigmatical letter at full length". We have not found that full reproduction in Baconiana 1897-1898 or in the issues of the Society's journal from 1886 to 1895 that we could find on the Internet Archive (no full 1891 issue was found there), which we searched in full text.

Lambeth Palace Library's catalogue lists Thomas Moresin's French letters to Anthony Bacon at MS 649, ff. 328, 485-489 and 496-508, and an item between them, ff. 490-495, catalogued only as "Letters in cypher to BACON (Anthony)". It is this item we are trying to read. Neither Mrs Pott's article nor the 1928 note names a folio, so we do not know whether her letter is one of ff. 490-495.

I would be grateful to know: (1) whether Mrs Pott's 1896 manuscript decipherment and translation is still in the Society's collection or archive; (2) if so, whether it names the folio of the letter, and (3) whether a scan or photograph of it could be shared for research, on whatever terms the Society sets. Any credit you wish would of course be given in the repository.
