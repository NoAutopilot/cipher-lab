Dear Mr Tomokiyo,

Thank you for your reply of 5 October and for the two leads. (As before, I send the letters; AI agents do the work, in our open repository.)

We tested both that day on f.119 and fr.3252 f.100r: neither fits, although the same tests recognise synthetic letters made in each system. If the 1571 key ever turns up, I will tell you.

Much of our work rests on yours: your keys did the hard part, and we applied them.

- Gramont, Rome, 20 May 1530 (fr.2980 f.29r, f.30r-v), with the key you and George Lasry published: f.29r read in part, f.30 in fragments.
- Danzay, 27 January 1557 (fr.20140 ff.35-36), with your 2026 reconstruction: partially deciphered (about 77%).
- Birago, 1572, with your 1572 key: fr.3251 nos.86 and 90 partially deciphered (about 83% and 76%), no.71 in fragments.
- Chavigny to d'Avaux, 25 August 1640 (Baluze 170 f.229r-v), with your D'Avaux table: partially deciphered (about 29%).

Fragments of other letters rest on your keys too, and your Civil War page corrected one of our claims: a Fort Monroe telegram whose only coded content, a cipher route, you had already printed.

Armstrong, the letter you suggested, has not yielded. We tried the office code, WE028 from your transcription, cribs, your sibling-vocabulary route and ten shorthand systems, each against a matched control; nothing reads, and at 369 groups our solvers fail even on synthetic letters. The best lead is a private Armstrong-Monroe cipher: the Monroe Catalogue Online lists three of Armstrong's 1805 letters to Monroe, at the New York Public Library, as "partially in code and deciphered", and in a postscript of 10 July 1807 (Library of Congress) Armstrong cannot read Monroe's last letter "from some change in your cypher or in mine". Whether the 1808 letter uses it is unknown. I have written to the NYPL (5 October) and to the Papers of James Monroe (8 October) about the 1805 letters; the Madison editors tell me they know of no other letter in this code. We are keeping at it: https://github.com/NoAutopilot/cipher-lab/tree/main/ciphers/armstrong-madison-1808

Thank you again. No reply is needed.
