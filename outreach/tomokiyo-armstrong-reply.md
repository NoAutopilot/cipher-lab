Dear Mr Tomokiyo,

Thank you for the Raince post, and for the credit. The Robertet letter of 24 October on f.24 is a useful addition; we have
recorded your post in the repository's citations file.

We have taken up Armstrong's letter of 20 February 1808 as a standing effort, along the lines you suggested. The first
day's findings, for what they are worth:

- No key or decode appears to survive: the Papers of James Madison editors record that Madison never acknowledged
  receipt and that the letter was never decoded, and the Library of Congress abstract of the Armstrong letters skips
  February 1808. The AFIO claimed solution and its rejection are on file.
- The sibling tables we could assemble (Monroe's WE028, 1,600 entries; the THE 972 code from Bourdeau's page and your
  partial transcription) all run block-wise alphabetical. None of them shows two features of Armstrong's 369 groups:
  a strong skew toward units digits 0 and 1, and an empty band between 900 and 1099. So the vocabulary of the
  published siblings may not transfer directly; we are testing whether it transfers under a structure-preserving
  control before drawing any conclusion.
- An English judge corpus built from Founders Online prose of 1800-1815 now sits behind every test, since the
  general English corpus was too unreliable for this register.

Nothing is read yet. If you know of any letter in the same code, or of a published table for the one-part code WE027,
that would be the most valuable thing we could receive. We will send you the numbers, positive or negative, with the
controls, when the first families are logged.
