Dear [editor's name as signed in the reply],

Thank you for the clear answer and for the pointer to Skeen's bibliographical essay; both settled a question for us, and I am grateful that you took the time.

In case it is useful to you, here is one thing we found after your reply came. The University of Mary Washington's Monroe Catalogue Online lists three letters Armstrong wrote to Monroe from Paris, 22 January, 5 April and 4 May 1805, held in the New York Public Library's Monroe Papers and described there as "partially in code and deciphered". We also read, in the Library of Congress's online images of its Monroe Papers (Series 1, reel 4, frame 0302), a postscript of 10 July 1807 to Armstrong's letter to Monroe of 7 July, in which Armstrong writes that "from some change in your cypher or in mine" Monroe's last letter was unintelligible to him. Together these suggest that Armstrong and Monroe had a private cipher of their own, which would make Monroe one candidate for the "other correspondent" Madison had in mind in 1808. We have not read the 1805 letters, and we do not know whether the 1808 letter is in that cipher.

I am asking the Monroe Papers editors for copies of the digital images of two of those letters so that we can test this, and we will tell you the result, whichever way it comes out. I am asking nothing of you.
