## Issue 1

**Title:** Catalogue 328 (BnF fr.2980 nos. 21-22, Gramont, Rome, 20 May 1530): partial readings with the Gramont 1530 key

Hello, and thank you for the catalogue. Your CATALOGUE.md line 76 lists these two letters as readable with the Gramont
1530 key you hold (gramont1529). We applied that key to both. What we found, in the wording of our audit:

- f.29r, no.21, to Villandry: "No prior decipherment located of Gabriel de Gramont's cipher letter to Jean Breton de
  Villandry, Rome, 20 May 1530 (BnF fr.2980 f.29r, no.21). Tomokiyo identified it as readable, and Bourdeau catalogued it
  (no.328). We read its 568-sign cipher passage in part with the Gramont 1530 key published by Tomokiyo and Lasry
  (2023): 533 signs at grade H, 30 uncertain, 5 unkeyed. The search log is in AUDIT.md."
- f.30r-v, no.22, to Francis I: "No prior decipherment located of Gabriel de Gramont's all-cipher letter to Francis I,
  Rome, 20 May 1530 (BnF fr.2980 f.30r-v, no.22). Tomokiyo identified it as readable, and Bourdeau catalogued it
  (no.328). We read it in part with the Gramont 1530 key published by Tomokiyo and Lasry (2023): of 1,973 signs, 1,500
  are at grade H and 158 at grade S with a matched control. Its closest print is the sibling letter of April 1530
  (Archivio storico italiano, Appendice I, pp.473-481), which is a different letter. The search log is in AUDIT.md."

Prior print this rests on: the key (Tomokiyo, "Gramont's Cipher (1530)"; Lasry's 2023 confirmation,
https://cryptiana.web.fc2.com/code/GL.htm). The letters' existence and date are in the *Catalogue des actes de
François Ier* IX, p.61 (https://archive.org/details/collectiondesord09acad).

- Images: https://gallica.bnf.fr/ark:/12148/btv1b9059991d/f31.item (f.29r) and /f32.item (f.30r)
- Transcription, key, reading (regenerated by script), per-token grades and search log:
  https://github.com/noautopilot/cipher-lab/tree/main/ciphers/fr2980-gramont
  and https://github.com/noautopilot/cipher-lab/blob/main/ciphers/fr2980-gramont/AUDIT.md

Corrections welcome, especially on the M and U signs.

## Issue 2

**Title:** Thurloe: Stamford's letter from Calais of 13 March 1655 (Birch III pp.187-189) read with the Stamford key

Hello. Your `thurloe/` folder uses Stamford's key (stamford.jpg) against other intercepts. Stamford's own letter of 13
March 1655, which Birch printed with its cipher numerals and no decipherment, reads with the key given by Birch's
decipherments of his letters of 20 and 30 March. In the wording of our audit:

"Birch (1742) prints William Stamford's letter from Calais of 13 March 1655 with its cipher numerals and no
decipherment. We read its cipher with the key given by Birch's printed decipherments of Stamford's letters of 20 and
30 March 1655. Thurloe's office deciphered this system in 1655, and Tomokiyo has since reconstructed and published it
(Cryptiana, 'William Stamford (1655)'). Our table agrees with his on every value that occurs in this letter. By token:
H 64, C 338, M 16, U 6 of 424, and eleven lines of p.188 are still partly incoherent. No prior decipherment of this
letter located in Birch and its indexes, British History Online, CSPD 1655, the Clarendon calendar vol. 3, the
Nicholas Papers, Abbott, the Clarke Papers, Gardiner, Underdown, the Bodleian's catalogues, Tomokiyo's pages, the solver
repositories, DECODE, Google Books or Internet Archive full text (searched 24 Sept 2026, AUDIT.md). The original leaf,
Bodleian MS Rawl. A. 24, has not been seen."

Prior print this rests on: Birch, *A Collection of the State Papers of John Thurloe* vol. 3 (1742), pp.187-189 (the
letter) and pp.274-280 (the decipherments that give the key), https://archive.org/details/collectionofstat03thur/page/187

- Transcription, key, reading (regenerated by script), per-token grades and search log:
  https://github.com/noautopilot/cipher-lab/tree/main/ciphers/thurloe-printed
  and https://github.com/noautopilot/cipher-lab/blob/main/ciphers/thurloe-printed/AUDIT.md (section "N4 set, P4")

The primary source is Birch's printed page; the manuscript leaf has not been imaged for us.
