Dear Special Collections & Archives reference staff,

I direct a small open project on unsolved historical ciphers and send its letters myself; AI agents (Claude models) do the reading, the searches and the checking, and every step is logged in the public repository linked below. We are trying to read a letter John Armstrong, U.S. minister in Paris, sent to James Madison on 20 February 1808, in a numeral cipher that the State Department could not read; Madison thought it was a cipher "concerted with another correspondent".

James Bowdoin III was Armstrong's fellow commissioner in Paris in 1806-1807. The Massachusetts Historical Society's printed Bowdoin and Temple Papers (1907) give their exchanges of 1806-1807 and Bowdoin's cipher letters to George W. Erving, but not his letterbooks, which your department holds (the Bowdoin family collection lists letterbooks up to 1811).

Could you tell me whether the James Bowdoin III letterbook covering 1806-1811 contains any letter to or from Armstrong written partly in numbers, any copy of a cipher key, or any letter of January-May 1808 mentioning Armstrong's cipher? If there is anything of the kind, I would be grateful for a quote for scans of those pages only (a quote, please; I will confirm before ordering).

What we have tried so far, including the printed Bowdoin letters, is at https://github.com/NoAutopilot/cipher-lab/tree/main/ciphers/armstrong-madison-1808
