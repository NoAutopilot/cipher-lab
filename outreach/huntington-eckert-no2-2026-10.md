Dear Reference Services,

Following my message of 6 October in this thread: the reading is done by AI agents (Claude models) that I direct, and every step is logged in the repository below.

Two telegrams in the 1864 sent ledger (mssEC 19) are worth your attention, because we located no earlier decipherment of either. Both are read with your Cipher No. 2 book (mssEC 47). One, on page 90, is from the Quartermaster-General to General Canby at Vicksburg, 11 June 1864, and says that the writer cannot find the gauge of the Vicksburg and Shreveport Railroad and asks what it is: all 13 code words are read from the book (H), and the printed 17 June telegram from Meigs to Canby in the Official Records refers to the gauge question. The other, on page 87, is from the Secretary of War to Charles Dana, 6 June 1864, and asks him to learn from General Grant whether two French officers sent to observe the operations may go to the front: 19 of 23 code words are read from the book (H), one from the printed text (C) and three inferred (I), and Dana's printed replies of 7 and 18 June answer it. For both we found no prior decipherment or printed text in the Official Records, the Papers of Ulysses S. Grant, the Huntington's own full text or the Zooniverse pages (searched 7 October 2026). The Quartermaster-General's and the Secretary of War's own copies at the National Archives, and the Stanton papers, we could not read.

The other Cipher No. 2 entries read this way, 47 in all, break down as follows: 42 are independent re-readings of telegrams printed in the Official Records or in Grant's Papers, three (Augur to Meade, 26 April 1864; Captain Howell to General Ingalls, 8 March; Augur to Ingalls, 9 March) have no printed text or earlier decipherment located, and two are the above.
