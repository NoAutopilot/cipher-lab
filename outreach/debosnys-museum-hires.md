Dear Adirondack History Museum,

Thank you for your reply of 28 September and for the scans of Henry Debosnys's clear-text writings; we are using them for reference and research only, as you asked, and nothing from them will be shared or published without your permission. As before, I direct this project and send its letters myself; AI agents (Claude models) do the reading, the searches and the checking, every step logged in the open repository linked below.

I have one further question, about the originals of the four cryptograms themselves. The only copies we can work from are the small reproductions published online, where each cipher sign is about 15 to 30 pixels across. At that size six groups of similar-looking signs (pairs, and one set of three) cannot be told apart reliably. We measured this on a test page with known answers built from those same images: our readers misread 15.6 to 17.7 percent of the signs, and the methods we have that would actually recover a key need an error rate under about 5 to 10 percent. We have also tried a sign classifier on the same images, and it did no better than the readers; better images of the originals are the most direct way to change that.

Does the museum hold the original cryptogram pages (six pages in the published copies), including the reverse of the fourth cryptogram's leaf? If so, could they be scanned at 600 dpi or higher, as uncompressed TIFF or maximum-quality JPEG, under the same restricted terms as the scans you have already sent (reference and research only, no sharing or publishing without your permission)? Is there a fee for this, and how would you like it paid? If it helps to keep the job small, I would be glad to say which leaves matter most.

Thank you for your time and your help. What we have so far is at https://github.com/NoAutopilot/cipher-lab/tree/main/ciphers/debosnys-1883
