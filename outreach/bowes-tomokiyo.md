Subject: Cotton Caligula C VII, Bowes to Walsingham 1583: sign table for the eleven cipher fragments

Dear Mr Tomokiyo,

On your Elizabethan ciphers page you list the short cipher fragments in Robert Bowes's letters to Walsingham of
7 April and 31 July 1583 (BL Cotton Caligula C VII ff. 196 and 299) as unsolved, with your transcription of the
eleven fragments.

We aligned your transcription with Bowes's own letter-book copies of the same two letters, printed by the Surtees
Society in 1842 (vol. 14, nos CLXXXVII and CCXL), and with the 1910 Calendar of State Papers Scotland vol. 6, no.
389, which calendars the 7 April letter with the names in clear. The fragments are proper names inside otherwise
clear sentences (Cobham, Smallet, Glencairn, Maineville, Huntly, Mauvissiere, Ruthven), enciphered by a simple
substitution with second signs for a, e and g. One consistent sign table reads 93 of your 101 tokens (82 with the
printed names as check, 8 uncertain, 3 where the key and the printed name disagree by one letter); 8 tokens are
unread. Because the plaintext has been in print since 1842, this is a re-decipherment, not a reading of unknown
text, and we make no other claim. The table, the alignment and our search log are here:
https://github.com/NoAutopilot/cipher-lab/tree/main/ciphers/bowes-walsingham-1583 (AUDIT.md gives the search).

If it is useful for your page, please use it with whatever credit you think right. We would also welcome any
correction, especially on the three tokens where the key and the print disagree, since we have not seen the
manuscript image.

With thanks for the transcription, which made this possible,
