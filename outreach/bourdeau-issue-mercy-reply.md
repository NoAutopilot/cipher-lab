One thing I should have said when I opened this issue: I direct this project and post its messages myself; the transcription, the key recovery, the image checks and the audits are done by AI agents (Claude models) working in the public repository linked below, with every step logged.

We checked each of your corrections against our own crops of the native Gallica images of f.22r-v, and we disagree with none of your values. Per token, briefly:

- r24:4, 65 → 6 5 (s r, "tres regimientos"): confirmed on the image; there is a visible gap, with a crease, between the 6 and the 5.
- v01:15, 19 → 14 (c, "y al camare[ro]"): confirmed; it is this hand's open, crossed 4, not its closed 9. The same holds at r14:7, r16:3 and r16:6, which our blind re-reads of 25 Sept had turned back into 19; on our crops they are 14, as you say (r15 has a 19 and a 14 side by side). So "Cleues" (r14) and "con Copurad" (r16) read again, at our uncertain grade.
- r17:10 52 → 5 2, r17:20 and r20:15 48 → 4 8, r16:21 72 → 7 2: agreed. On our crops each is one tight group, so the image does not decide the split by itself; the key's range (2-34) and the words do, and the same hand writes "que" as 4 8 with a gap at r24. Graded uncertain (M), as you mark them.
- r18:5 and r24:13, 26 → 2 6 (o s): agreed on sense. Since 26 is also a valid code (i), these two rest on the words alone, M.
- Your two re-cuts in r10, 25 → 2 5 ("informe") and 2 8 → one 28 ("della y"): applied at M and credited to you. On our crop the 25 is an ordinary tight group and, as your edits note says, the 2 and the 8 stand apart, so we keep two tokens in the transcription and record the l as a repair by sense.

Five of the eight (the four codes 48, 52, 65 and 72) were tokens above the key's own range, which a mechanical check would have caught on the day the key settled; we have recorded that lesson.

Our reading, counts and audit are updated: https://github.com/noautopilot/cipher-lab/tree/main/ciphers/espagnol142-mercy-1648 and https://github.com/noautopilot/cipher-lab/blob/main/ciphers/espagnol142-mercy-1648/AUDIT.md (per-token details in NOTES.md, sections "Bourdeau corrections folded in (1 Oct 2026)" and "Code 15 (v04)"). Bourdeau (1 Oct 2026) checked by us on the image (488 of 529 code tokens at grade S, 41 at M, none from a key or known plaintext)". S is our grade for a cryptanalytic value backed by a control, M for uncertain. The open places are the ones you list: eight tokens in v04 and the letters lost at the right edge of f.22v. We have not seen the Brussels Secrétairerie d'État et de Guerre registers (t. LXIV-LXV, and reg. 240 fol. 201, the June 1648 dispatch to Philip IV calendared in Lonchay, Cuvelier and Lefèvre, *Correspondance de la Cour d'Espagne* IV, 1933, no. 183, p. 71, which reports the Brandenburg errand and the 3,000 infantry but not the instruction itself) or Simancas Estado, where a clear copy of this instruction may survive: Lonchay (1896, p. 445 n. 2) shows the April instruction to Mercy went to the king enclosed with a dispatch.

Your two identifications are recorded in our notes and audit as yours: the box sign as the comte de Saint-Ibal, and "copurad lon burgstorf su cmarero mayor" as Konrad von Burgsdorff, the Elector's Oberkammerherr. We read the f.21r passage ourselves ("Despues de oydo a la Duquessa con Santibal, Dessearia si fuesse possible que este viniese con Vos incognito") and agree; we have not yet re-read f.20r. We file both as identifications rather than key values, so our key still gives the box sign no letter. Our own crib search of 28 Sept had Burgsdorff as its best-fitting name, but it assumed 72 and 52 were syllable codes; your splits are what make every letter read.

On code 15, which you asked about: we ran three separate checks (the image, the key's layout, and word fit), and nothing decides it. Where it stands:

1. v04:9 is a genuine 15: an S-shaped 5 with a small gap inside the group. It is not a 14, because this hand crosses its 4s, and our three reads of 27 Sept, before your page, also had one 15. So, like you, we do not take the route to "direction" through a 14.
2. The glyph at v04:11 looks to us like 31, not 32: it has the tall l-shaped 1 of the 31s at v04:6 and v04:17, and none of the flat-based 2s on that line. But our two transcription passes, our 27 Sept check and your transcription all read 32, and our three looks at it were all made after reading your "direction" note. It is a proposal until two blind reads confirm it.
3. Our key reads as a table: the even codes 10-32 are a to n in order (no j or k), 2-8 are o to u, the odd codes 17-33 are vowels, and 13 is y. Of the letters this Spanish needs, only x and z have no code, and 11 never occurs. One of the Brussels Secrétairerie tables on DECODE (record 965, p. 7), which is not this letter's key, also has o to u at 2-8 and then x, y, z at 9-11: a design point for z beside y, not proof of the slot (our 9 is q).

On that basis our best value for 15 is z, at grade M. It turns v04:14-19, plus one token lost in the gutter and the start of the next line, into "y si iuz[g]a sera mas conueniente", using your extra i as the j of "juzga". Against it: v04:9 would then read "direztion", a spelling we could not find attested; neither of our two letter models (each tested with a known-answer control) nor a short-window language judge separates z, c and n at either place; and the glyph at v04:19 is only half visible (it could be 13, 15, 17 or 19), so choosing 15 there rests partly on the very word it is meant to support. So 15 = z is a proposal at M, c and n are weaker, and nothing reaches S. Our key file keeps the anneal's n for 15 until this is settled.

What would settle it: two blind reads of v04:11 (and v04:9) on an unlabelled crop sheet; a photograph of f.22v that reaches further into the gutter, which would also check the letters you restored at that edge; or another letter in this key with a readable 15. On the edge itself, your notes call it trimmed; we had taken it as the gutter, the bound side of a verso, which would leave those letters recoverable from another photograph. If you saw a trim line, we would be glad to know.

We will read the clear instructions on ff. 20r and 21r in full (so far we have read only the f.21r passage above). Besides the cast, they may show how this clerk writes the c, ç and z sounds in clear, which bears on code 15.
