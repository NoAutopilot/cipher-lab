On code 15, which you asked about: we ran three separate checks (the image, the key's layout, and word fit), and nothing decides it. Where it stands:

1. v04:9 is a genuine 15: an S-shaped 5 with a small gap inside the group. It is not a 14, because this hand crosses its 4s; our three reads of 27 Sept, before your page, had one 15, and so did two blind reads on 2 Oct (below). So, like you, we do not take the route to "direction" through a 14.
2. v04:11 is 32, as you and our transcription have it. After reading your "direction" note we had three looks at it that leaned to 31; to test that, we cut v04:6-19 and six control groups from other lines of f.22v into a shuffled sheet, labelled only by letters, and had two separate AI readers read it blind. Both read v04:11 as 32 (each with 31 as the second choice), v04:9 as 15, and all fourteen v04 groups as our transcription has them; v04:19 read 15 on both, at low confidence. So the 31 we had leaned to is not supported, and "direction" has no support on the image.
3. Our key reads as a table: the even codes 10-32 are a to n in order (no j or k), 2-8 are o to u, the odd codes 17-33 are vowels, and 13 is y. Of the letters this Spanish needs, only x and z have no code other than the disputed 15, and 11 never occurs. One of the Brussels Secrétairerie tables on DECODE (record 965, p. 7), which is not this letter's key, also has o to u at 2-8 and then x, y, z at 9-11: a design point for z beside y, not proof of the slot (our 9 is q).

On that basis our best value for 15 is z, at grade M. It turns v04:14-19, plus one token lost in the gutter and the start of the next line, into "y si iuz[g]a sera mas conueniente", using your extra i as the j of "juzga". Against it: at v04:9 z gives no word we could find (even with 31 at v04:11, which the blind reads did not support, it gives "direztion", a spelling we could not find attested); neither of our two letter models (each tested with a known-answer control) nor a short-window language judge separates z, c and n at either place; and the glyph at v04:19 is only half visible (both blind readers gave 15 as their best read, at low confidence, with 13, 14, 17 and others as alternatives), so choosing 15 there rests partly on the very word it is meant to support. So z for 15 is a proposal at M, c and n are weaker, and nothing reaches S. Since 4 Oct our key file gives 15 as z, at that uncertain grade, in place of the n our first key-search gave it; this changes only the two v04 tokens, and no count above.

What would settle it: a photograph of f.22v that reaches further into the gutter, which would also check the letters you restored at that edge; or another letter in this key with a readable 15. On the edge itself, your notes call it trimmed; we had taken it as the gutter, the bound side of a verso, which would leave those letters recoverable from another photograph. If you saw a trim line, we would be glad to know.
