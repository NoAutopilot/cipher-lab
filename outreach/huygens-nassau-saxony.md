Dear editors of the Correspondence of William of Orange,

I run a small project that reads cipher passages in published letter images, with each reading checked by a separate
search for any prior decipherment. Six letters in your William of Orange database carry cipher that the database records
as unread. We have read them in part, with keys taken from contemporary decipherments of sibling letters in the same
database, and located no prior decipherment in the principal editions after a logged search. None changes the story the
editions tell; each adds a detail or shows the secret channel.

- WVO 4610, 4611, 4616, Lodewijk van Nassau to Orange, 3 June and 2 July 1573 and 12 April 1574 (two days before
  Mookerheyde). Key from the decipherments of 4613 and 4615. The cipher gives Louis's side of exchanges whose replies
  Groen prints (Archives, 1st ser., IV): a proposal to convene the Electors and French contacts; recruiting, with an
  offer of twenty companies for Friesland; and, in 1574, march and lodging.
- WVO 53, Orange to Elector August, 24 Oct 1561. Read without any key source (a cryptanalytic result, 238 of 364 signs
  at grade S): news of the Prince of Spain's marriage and the Netherlands, Vendôme and Navarre, and Orange's own
  judgement that it would come to nothing. The rumours are printed from other senders.
- WVO 57, August to Orange, 18 Nov 1561, read with the key of Orange's 1562 cipher: the Emperor's approach about electing
  Maximilian King of the Romans, told to Orange in secret within days of the Palatine letters Kluckhohn prints.
- WVO 126, Orange to August, 16 Sept 1564, read with a key from WVO 98: the Queen of Spain's miscarriage, printed from
  Saint-Sulpice; the cipher adds only the channel.

Readings, keys, per-token grades and the full search log for each:
https://github.com/noautopilot/cipher-lab/tree/main/ciphers/lodewijk-van-nassau-1573-74 and
https://github.com/noautopilot/cipher-lab/tree/main/ciphers/august-van-saksen-1561-64 (AUDIT.md in each). Images are
your own record pages (brief?nr=4610, 4611, 4616, 53, 57, 126). If your editors know a decipherment of any of these, in
the KHA or Dresden minutes or elsewhere, we would be glad to cite it.

If useful, we can hand over a table of the 73 WVO letters we found with cipher passages, marked which an edition prints
in clear and which stand unread, and the keys as machine-readable tables, in whatever format suits the database. And if
there are unread passages you would most like read, name them and we will try those first.
