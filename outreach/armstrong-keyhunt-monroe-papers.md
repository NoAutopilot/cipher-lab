Dear Papers of James Monroe,

I direct a small open project on unsolved historical ciphers and send its letters myself; AI agents (Claude models) do the reading, the searches and the checking, and every step is logged in the public repository linked below. We are working on a letter John Armstrong, U.S. minister in Paris, sent to James Madison on 20 February 1808, in a numeral cipher the State Department could not read; Madison called it a cipher "concerted with another correspondent".

Your Monroe Catalogue Online helped us a great deal. Searching it (with the guest sign-in printed on your catalogue page, one session, on 7 October 2026) we found that the New York Public Library's Monroe Papers hold three letters from Armstrong to Monroe in Paris that the catalogue describes as "partially in code and deciphered": 22 January 1805 (catalogue docid 83485), 5 April 1805 (docid 83535) and 4 May 1805 (docid 83558). We also read, at the Library of Congress (Monroe Papers, reel 4, frame 0302), a postscript of 10 July 1807 in which Armstrong tells Monroe that "from some change in your cypher or in mine" Monroe's last letter was unintelligible to him. We take from this that the two men used a private cipher, and that Monroe's deciphered copies may show how it worked. We have not read the 1805 letters and do not know whether the 1808 letter is in the same cipher; that is the test we would like to make.

I would be very grateful for one thing: research-use copies of the digital images of Armstrong to Monroe, 5 April 1805 (docid 83535) and 4 May 1805 (docid 83558), the two records your catalogue marks "Digital image". If an image of the 22 January 1805 letter (docid 83485) also exists, I would be glad to have that too; if none does, a one-line answer saying so would help. If images can be shared only with the permission of the New York Public Library, I would be grateful to be told whom to ask. We would credit your catalogue and the NYPL in anything we write, and tell you what the images show either way.

What we already hold, so that I am not asking for it: the catalogue records themselves (our own export), the Library of Congress frame above, the National Archives despatch copies (RG 59, M34 rolls 13 and 14) and the Founders Online texts of the 1805 Madison Papers letters. Everything we have done, including what we ruled out, is at https://github.com/NoAutopilot/cipher-lab/tree/main/ciphers/armstrong-madison-1808
