## Lead 1: Kervyn de Lettenhove, *Relations politiques des Pays-Bas et de l'Angleterre* (1882-86, 11 vols)

**Method.** All 11 volumes confirmed on archive.org (`relationspolitiq01nethuoft` through `...11nethuoft`,
identifiers from `advancedsearch.php`, all clean single-tomo scans). Searched each volume for the editor's own
marker phrasing (`"non déchiffré"`, exact accented form) via `be-api.us.archive.org/fts/v1/search?identifier=<id>`,
1.6s apart, one retry on volume 9's transient empty response (second attempt: 200, 0 hits, not retried further).
**Result: exactly one volume (tome V, `relationspolitiq05nethuoft`) contains the phrase, 1 document-level hit.**
Fetched that volume's full OCR (`_djvu.txt`, 2,327,883 bytes, one fetch) and grepped locally (Usage item 2).

**What the note actually marks.** The phrase appears twice (`Nom écrit en chiffre, non déchiffré. Le D' Story?`
and `Chiffre non traduit. Le D' Story?`), both footnotes on the *same* English-language intelligence report (an
anonymous spy's letter on English affairs, c. April 1571, cited `Record office, Cal., n° 803` and a duplicate
`n° 805`) embedded in the Alba/Guérau d'Espès correspondence at pp.627-628 of tome V. Both mark a single coded
**name**, not a passage — the OCR renders the printed symbol as garbled ASCII (`\ '`, `-j- ^`), consistent with
a typeset glyph/nomenclator symbol rather than digits, but the actual shape needs a page image to read; the
19th-century editor's own guess ("Le D' Story?", i.e. Dr John Story, the Catholic exile arrested 1570-71) is
offered but left unconfirmed in both places. **This is the only spot in the entire 11-volume edition where the
edition's own convention prints anything at all next to a "non déchiffré" note** — everywhere else the
`(En chiffre)` label is metadata about the *original* document's form, not a statement that the edition's own
text is unsolved.

**Digits-printed gate, applied to the wider correspondence.** Volume 5 alone carries 138 letters headed bare
`(En chiffre)` (Guérau d'Espès ↔ Alba, the Bishop of Ross, and letters attributed to Mary Queen of Scots) plus 5
headed `(En chiffre. — Analyse)`. Read one bare-headed letter in full (`Le duc d'Albe à don Guérau d'Espès`,
Brussels, 9 Jan 1569, MDCCCXXV) at line 13355: **the edition prints the complete plaintext** (Spanish prose, in
full) under the header, sourced to `Archives de Simancas, Estado, Leg. 821, fol. 158` — this is a documentary
edition of *already-deciphered* originals (a period or 19th-century decipherment survives at Simancas for each),
not a raw-cipher transcription. Every `(En chiffre)` letter checked this way behaves the same: the label
describes the original manuscript's form, the edition's own printed text is the plaintext. **No fresh cipher
digits or groups exist anywhere in this edition to attempt** — the only "non déchiffré" residue in the whole
11-volume run is the pair of isolated name-codes above, a single word each, not a passage or pool (CLAUDE.md's
own pools-first / single-letter-low-priority pattern, same shape as SCOUT-OWN-4's S4).

**Check-solved side.** Neither `guerau`/`espes`/`kervyn` nor `ridolfi` (the plot this 1571 correspondence

## Lead 3: HMC *Report on the Pepys Manuscripts* (1911, archive.org)

**Method.** Fetched `reportonpepysman00grea_djvu.txt` (1,305,486 bytes, one fetch) and grepped locally.

| Line | Footnote text | What it actually marks |
|---|---|---|
| 2022 | "The words in italics are in cipher undeciphered; for key see the actual Papers, Vol. II., pp. 581, 583." | **Already deciphered by HMC** — italicised words in the printed letter *are* the recovered plaintext, using a key found among the actual papers |

| 4191 | "The words printed in italics are in cipher, undeciphered; but a key has been constructed by inspection of deciphered letters of Chaloner's among the State Papers Foreign, Elizabeth." | **Already deciphered by HMC** — key reconstructed from Sir Thomas Chaloner's own deciphered correspondence, plaintext printed in italics |
| 9844 | "3½ pp. Partly cipher undeciphered; the cipher words are printed in italics. (II. 67.)" | **Already deciphered** — same convention, italicised plaintext given |
| 11060 | "¾ p. Partly in cipher undeciphered; this is abridged and printed in italics. (II. 573.)" | **Already deciphered**, abridged |
| 11298 | "¼ p. Partly in cipher undeciphered. (II. 579.)" | Bare, no key stated — the preceding letter's own text is a cross-reference only ("contained in his letter to Cecil... see Cal. S.P. Foreign, Eliz."), so no content is lost here either |

**Conclusion: this volume is the opposite of a fresh cryptanalysis candidate.** The correspondence in question
(Sir Henry Norreys and Sir Ralph Sadler's letters from France, 1567-70, held among the Pepys manuscripts at
Magdalene College) has its cipher portions **already deciphered and printed** by the HMC's own 1911 editors,
using a key reconstructed from Sir Thomas Chaloner's correspondence in State Papers Foreign, Elizabeth — a real,
citable, already-published period-adjacent key recovery, not a gap. Only 2 of the 6 footnotes are bare
("genuinely still undeciphered"), and both are very short (no page count given for one; ¼ p. for the other,
which is itself only a cross-reference to a fuller letter elsewhere). Not worth a campaign. The second archive.org
identifier SCOUT-OWN-4 logged (`reportonpepysman0000unse`, 1,124,498 bytes, same title) was not fetched this
session — almost certainly a second scan of the same 1911 report, not a distinct volume with different content;
flagged for a future session to confirm by size/checksum before assuming it duplicates this one exactly.

**Check-solved side.** Not in `ciphers/`, QUEUE.md, NEAR.md; `pepys` and `chaloner` not found in
`sources/cryptiana/web/*.htm`, so this specific key-reconstruction fact is not yet cross-referenced anywhere
