## 1. GOLD-4D's 3.3x overrun: a subagent job-size rule, and the wall-clock box cannot interrupt one long call

GOLD-4D (Sonnet, $7 cap, 60-minute box, LEDGER.md:759) was stopped by the orchestrator at 19:03, 38 minutes in, at
$23.19 -- 3.3x cap. Its brief (`.claude/briefs/runs/2026-09-25-lane-gold-debosnys-passB2.md`) already followed
`2026-09-24-lane-r4-common.md`'s existing subagent rule ("at most ONE Sonnet subagent") and stayed inside the
60-minute box on the clock -- the overrun was not a count problem or a box problem, it was a **single call's own
size**: one subagent transcribed all four Debosnys cryptograms (~1,300 signs) against a 160-sign reference atlas in
one turn (85M cache-read tokens per the ledger row), a cost that scales as signs x inventory size, not by elapsed
minutes. This is the same failure shape the wall-clock-box rule was built to catch for a *worker's own* self-timing
(RETRO-2026-09-24b/25h: workers reading their own cost mid-run), but the box only checks time **between** tool
calls -- a worker cannot look at the clock while a single tool call (here, one Task/subagent invocation) is still
running, so a call sized to outlive the box blows past it before the worker regains control to check anything.
GOLD-4D's own box was 60 minutes and it was killed by the orchestrator's external `get_session` read at minute 38,
not by its own box logic, which never got a turn to fire. The lane already worked around this once, correctly,
in the immediate successor job GOLD-4E (`.claude/briefs/runs/2026-09-25-lane-gold-debosnys-c1passB.md`: one
cryptogram, ~132 signs, no subagent, worker does the transcription directly, commits every two lines) -- but that
fix lives in one lane-specific job brief, not in the shared rule every lane's subagent-based visual-transcription
job reads (CLAUDE.md's own meta-rule: a fix landing in one lane's file and not the shared one is exactly what
RETRO-2026-09-25h found twice the same day). Generalize it into CLAUDE.md's Fan-out limits (item 6), the one place
every worker brief already points to for subagent counts:

```diff
--- a/CLAUDE.md
+++ b/CLAUDE.md
@@ Usage
 6. **Fan-out limits.** At most four subagents at once per worker; two transcription passes, not three, unless
    the two disagree on more than a tenth of the rows.
+   A subagent's transcription job is priced by signs matched x reference-sheet size, not by elapsed minutes or
+   page count: GOLD-4D (25 Sept 2026) gave one Sonnet subagent all four Debosnys cryptograms (about 1,300 signs)
+   against a 160-sign inventory in a single call and was stopped at 3.3x its $7 cap, 38 minutes into a 60-minute
+   box, because the wall-clock box is checked between tool calls and cannot interrupt one call that is still
+   running -- a call sized to outlive the box defeats it regardless of the box's length. Scope a subagent's
+   visual-transcription call to one page or one cryptogram against the reference sheet, never the whole
+   inventory in one call, and have the orchestrator read `get_session` on that worker at a fixed short interval
+   (15 minutes, GOLD's own fix) rather than trusting the box alone when a job includes a large-inventory match.
```

## 2. Lane orchestrators overrun their context line because, unlike cost, nothing reads it for them

LANE B2 handed off at 425k context against its brief's 300k line (1.42x); LANE R6 sits at 505k against a 500k line
(1.01x, essentially on the line) at this window's close. Cost has a mechanical external check the orchestrator
cannot fool itself on: COMMON item 1 ("Cost is read by the orchestrator, never self-reported... the orchestrator
reads `get_session` usage.cost_usd at every check-in") and the parent's own duty 1 reads `rate_limit_info`
independently. Context has no equivalent: `get_session` (grepped across `tools/*.py` and every brief) returns cost,
never a context or token count, so every "425k context" / "505k context" / "493k context" figure in STATUS.md this
window is the orchestrator's own sense of its conversation length -- exactly the self-perception measurement that
two retrospectives (RETRO-2026-09-24b, RETRO-2026-09-25h) already showed is unreliable for cost, before COMMON item
1 fixed it with an external read. Nothing in `parent.md`'s "Lanes" check-in duty (item 2) asks for a context figure
the way item 1 asks for `rate_limit_info`; a lane orchestrator's own brief states the line only as a terminal
stopping condition ("hand off at $25 or 300k context") with no instruction to report it *before* reaching it. B2's
own context figure was never seen by the parent before B2's 19:03 handoff line announced it 41% over; R6's was
seen once, in passing, inside a lane-cost line at 18:21 (415k) -- not asked for as its own check-in item. Since
there is no `get_session`-equivalent for context, the mechanical fix is a proxy the parent (or the orchestrator
itself) can read without introspection -- a context estimate reported at *every* check-in, not only at hand-off,
so an accelerating lane is caught one check-in early rather than announced already over the line:

```diff
--- a/.claude/briefs/parent.md
+++ b/.claude/briefs/parent.md
@@ ## Duties at every check-in
 2. **Lanes.** For each lane orchestrator: status, last ROOM line, last commit, pending check-in. Idle with no pending
    check-in, silent for 60 minutes while running, or failed: brief a successor from the lane's brief file and its
    last handoff or ROOM lines, telling it to adopt the live workers. A lane that wrote its handoff stays closed.
+   Ask each live lane orchestrator for its own context estimate at every check-in (there is no `get_session`
+   equivalent for context the way there is for cost, so this is a self-report -- but a self-report read every
+   30-45 minutes catches an accelerating lane before its line, not only at its own hand-off announcement). A lane
+   past 80 percent of its brief's context line writes its handoff on this check-in, not the next one (25 Sept
+   2026: LANE B2 handed off at 425k against a 300k line, 41 percent over, with no context figure seen by the
+   parent before that hand-off line itself; LANE R6 handed off at 505k against 500k, on the line, the same
+   window -- the difference is whether the line was watched before it was crossed).
```

## 4. es17c's 23.5% vs pt18's 4.0% false-negative rate is a per-fold variance problem, not (mainly) an era or size gap

Read `ciphers/espagnol142-mercy-1648/NOTES.md`'s MJ section in full rather than only the aggregate figure. es17c
(2.10M letters, 3 Internet Archive volumes of *Memorial histórico español*, 1643-47, the same decade and a
letters-not-fiction genre) is already era- and genre-matched by the same method that fixed pt18 -- and MJ says so
directly: "register-matching did not flip the FAIL to a PASS... M2's register-mismatch hypothesis is not confirmed
by this corpus." The real signal is in the leave-one-file-out breakdown MJ reported and flagged as noteworthy but
did not turn into a rule: `memorialhistri17` 39.5% FN, `memorialhistri18` 21.0%, `memorialhistri19` 10.0% -- a
4x spread across the corpus's only three folds, which means the blended 23.5% is dominated by whichever volume
happens to differ most internally, not a stable property of "this corpus." pt18's reported 4.0% is a single
blended number with no per-file breakdown on file (checked `ciphers/antt-linhares-chave/NOTES.md` and
`tools/data/pt18/README.md`; neither carries a per-fold table), so it cannot be confirmed as more internally
uniform -- it may just not have been asked the same question yet. The corpus-size lesson already in CLAUDE.md rule
3 (era, then transcription convention, then era-of-corpus) needs a fourth case: leave-one-file-out on a
small-file-count corpus is itself unstable, and a blended false-negative rate should be reported with its spread,
not alone, or a narrow aggregate gap (es17c real_p05 -0.867 vs. the clear words' own -0.891, a 0.024-point gap)
reads as more decisive than a 10-39.5% per-fold spread supports.

```diff
--- a/CLAUDE.md
+++ b/CLAUDE.md
@@ rule 3 (after the pt18/V6-PTCORP paragraph)
+   A corpus's held-out false-negative rate is only as trustworthy as its fold count. Lesson of 25 Sept 2026
+   (MJ, espagnol142-mercy-1648): `tools/data/es17c` matched the target's era and genre (1643-47 Spanish
+   court-newsletter *Cartas*, not Cervantes) exactly the way pt18 matched Linhares, and the FAIL did not flip --
+   register-matching alone does not always rescue a judge the way it did once. Its leave-one-file-out
+   false-negative rate (23.5%, N=519) is a blend of only three folds that disagree by 4x (10.0%, 21.0%, 39.5%
+   per volume); a blended rate from so few folds says more about which volume was held out than about the
+   corpus as a whole. Report the per-fold spread alongside the blended rate, and treat a corpus with under
+   ~5 source files and a wide per-fold spread as a FAIL/PASS of unknown reliability rather than trusting the
+   single number, the same way a control below its own gate cannot license a target reading (rule 3's
+   headline paragraph).
```
