Four of five (all but Cañada) were caught only at the reconciler or verifier stage, after a capture brief, a

case: nobody looked for (a) an interlinear or marginal gloss on the leaf itself, (b) a duplicate or
contemporary-decipherment copy elsewhere in the same collection/fonds, or (c) the clear text already sitting
in the cited printed edition, before commissioning transcription/alignment passes.

## Subject 3: `tools/room.py --digest`, to stop ROOM.md bulk reads filling the parent's context

This window's slice alone (07:45-09:53 UTC) is 205 lines; CLAUDE.md already tells a worker to read only "the
last 30 lines" before acting, but an orchestrator doing a check-in across five live lanes has no such limit
and currently re-reads the growing file in full each time (the same problem RETRO-2026-09-24c's measurement
caveat ran into: "the ledger lags live activity... dozens more workers claimed and `done`" between reads).
CLAUDE.md's own room rules give the filter for free: "A `flag` line is an interjection: the orchestrator reads
every flag before the next assignment"; a `claim`/`done` pair is what a lane orchestrator needs when collecting
workers; nothing else in ROOM.md needs a full re-read at every check-in.

**Proposed spec for a `--digest` option on `tools/room.py`** (spec only; not implemented here, per brief):

```
tools/room.py --digest [--since "YYYY-MM-DD HH:MM"] [--key flag,claim,done] [--lane PREFIX]

Reads ROOM.md once. For each line (format "TIMESTAMP | actor | signal: text"), keeps it only if:
  - --since is given and the line's timestamp is >= it (default: no time filter), AND
  - the text after "actor | " starts with one of --key's words (default: flag only), AND
  - --lane is given and "actor" contains it, case-insensitive (default: no lane filter).
Prints matched lines verbatim (so they stay pastable back into a report) plus one summary line:
"N of M lines matched (kept flags: <count>, claims: <count>, dones: <count>)".
Exit 0 whether or not anything matched (digesting is not a health check; --start remains the stub/shrink
guard). No line is ever dropped from ROOM.md itself -- this only changes what a caller chooses to print.
```

Example use this window: an orchestrator doing a 09:53 check-in across G2/V2/R2/W/N2 would run
`tools/room.py --digest --since "2026-09-24 09:00" --key flag` and get the handful of `flag` lines (the Groen
print bundled in the WVO PDF, the 5109/Japikse alignment pair, the BLA188 gutter-boundary question) instead of
the 90-odd lines posted since 09:00, most of which are claim/done pairs the lane orchestrators already
collected into their own STATUS.md handoffs.

## Subject 4: verifier throughput today (lesson, not a change)

A reading waited under 25 minutes for its verifier twice, checkable in ROOM.md: LANE R2 worker G1 posted the
Günther von Schwarzburg reading "for LANE V2" at 09:24 UTC; Verifier V4 claimed it at 09:48 (24 minutes) and
finished at 09:50. LANE G2 worker M posted the fr5160 f.67 reading "for LANE V2" at 09:27; Verifier V5 claimed
it at 09:48 (21 minutes). What made this work, per STATUS.md and the 09:53 orchestrator note: verification
split into two lanes by language rather than one queue — LANE V/V2 owns the French BnF and Dutch/Huygens
targets, LANE W owns the English targets (Blathwayt, Thurloe, Eckert) — so a reading lands directly in the
queue of the verifier lane already primed for its sources (Delpher/DBNL/Huygens editions for V2; HathiTrust/
Google Books/HTRC for W) instead of competing in one shared backlog against readings needing unrelated source
families. Both lanes also kept an idle Opus verifier standing rather than spinning one up per reading, so the
claim latency is dispatch time, not spin-up time. Worth keeping as the default shape when a third language
group (e.g. German, for the Saxony/Schwarzburg run) grows large enough to compete for the same two verifiers.
