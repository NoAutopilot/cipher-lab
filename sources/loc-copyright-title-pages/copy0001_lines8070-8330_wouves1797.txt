9th November 1797 -*]ELUCIDATIONS.
This table contains 62. alphabetical columns, and every numerical column
numbered from 1. to 99. answers for two of the alphabetical ones.
The space left betwixt the columns and the initial letters is designed to received
the hundreds which every one may choose to affix to each column: those
hundred might be taken out of any undetermined number, but without vainly
multiplying the figures it shall soon be made evident that, for the safety of the
secret, it is fully sufficient to carry the number of those same hundreds to that
of the columns, that is to say, from 100. up to 6200.
The figures placed above the initial letters serve to indicate the quantum of
each column; so whenever any use is to be made of this table to render into
numerical figures any object whatever, it is proceeded to by previously inscribing
at the head of every alphabetical column any one of the abovementioned
hundreds, which may be taken at random, or just as they present themselves to
any body's mind.  So, to give an instance of it, 5300. may happen to be placed
at the head of the first column, as 4900. at the head of the second, 6200. at the
head of the third, and so forth:  Supposing then that any word, or any division
of a word to be converted into figures, is under the No. 22 of the third column,
then it is to be expressed by 6222. and so of all the rest, having absolutely no
other need for the expression of any object whatever, but of the ten customary
cyphers.
Any body spreading this table just as it is wont to do for the use of a geographical 
map, may, by the means of the initial letters, and of the numbers
which correspond to the alphabetical columns, point out with a single glance,
any word or portion of any word required, and have it immediately converted
into figures.
When on the contrary, any object is to be deciphered, to avoid in this particular
any research, either vague or undeterminate, the progressive list of the
hundreds purposely placed at the end of the table, ought to be consulted, and
successively referring every number to the hundred, to the column, as well as
to the quantum that corresponds to it, the indicated object may be, of course,
written above the same number.  Along side of that progressive list of hundreds,
a margin or blank has been left, so that any one may affix to it the quantum of
the column that corresponds to each of the hundreds.
N. B.  When any person desires that the same table may serve for any number
of correspondents, a progressive list of the hundreds, such as that, or of the same
kind as that of the table, is to be made upon any loose piece of paper, leaving close
to it (for every correspondent) a particular margin; where may be transcribed the
quantum of the columns which corresponds to the hundreds, chosen or designed for
every one of the said correspondents; then taking a slip of paper of the same breadth
as the space left betwixt the initial letters and the figures of the columns, the several 
hundreds are inscribed upon it: Spreading afterwards, that same slip on the same
space, every time it is wanted, use is made of it as explained above.

Of the Division or Partition of Words.
People in general may (at the first aspect) be a little struck with the apparent
difficulty, offered by that same division, or partition; but a few trials or experiments,
will commonly convince of a quite contrary [an] effect in its execution;
and though, by this method, but three alphabetical letters can, at one, be expressed,
which some times may require five divisions and more, for the expression
of a single word, yet it is an experienced fact, that the number of figures
taken collectively, is hardly to that of the letters, and 5 to 3. and that with a little
use or habit, any page in 8 vo. of pica, can easily be rendered into cyphers, within an
hour and a quarter, and even in a less space of time in all the languages aboundinghour and a quarter, and even in a less space of time in all the languages abounding

with vowels, and making use but of few mute letters.  Upon the whole, it maybe
here not improperly observed that, for any one who is not by situation actuated
either by the pleasures, or by the wants of intimacy, every steganographical method
whatever, must inevitably appear somewhat tedious.
The writing of the figures is here according to the usual manner from the
left to the right, with this peculiar attention, to put a stop at the end of every
number or numbers representing any word, and that stop independently of all
the other points, commas, &c. agreeable to the form or custom used in every
language; but when any word is to be divided into several parts or numbers,
every one of them is to be distinguished by a hyphen: So supposing a word to
be divided into three parts, it should stand thus, 5347-3928-6222.
This table having been calculated to answer many different languages, to
avoid in this respect, either confusion or misapplication, it has been thought fit
to place no kind of accent upon any letter, the object of which, as to the English
language, has no difficulty, and is liable to no sort of equivocation or error.
N. B.  As among the languages following the same alphabet, the Spanish language
has besides, a peculiar kind of letter, commonly called the tilt n̄.  It has been 
thought proper to have it here inserted.
Safety of the Secret.
Since this table is composed of 62. columns, it is evident that, on all occasions,
there is at least 62 against 1. to lay that no word or portion of a word is
to be hit upon, or guessed at; but even admitting such a lucky hit, [as] if such [it] one
can, at most, carry along with it for the discovery or for the finding out of any
other, but a kind of indication, as vague, and even more fallacious that the total
ignorance of the first, the object of secrecy might, by that alone, be looked
upon as sufficiently attained and fulfilled; notwithstanding, in the supposition
of any not finding themselves completely satisfied with it, amongst the infinite
variety of the means which may be employed to that purpose, with the only 
caution of agreeing with their correspondents, to add to the numbers expressed,
or to subtract from them any quantity whatever, they may always be sure to
carry, upon this point, the impenetrability of the secret above all the possible
reach of any sort of combination or conjecture.  It is obvious that, in
all cases, the corresponding parties ought to be provided with the same
table as well as with the same hundreds uniformly places at the head of
every column, but hundreds which every one may, at all times, variously
dispose for the respective use of each of his correspondents.
From the tenor of these explanations, it may then be concluded, that, henceforth,
there shall be no man in any sort of business, no merchant whatever
who, when letters are brought to him for the signing, may not, by the only addition
of a few figures, directly impart his most precise orders, or his most
feeling concerns, and that in a manner utterly unintelligible, but to the very
correspondent, or correspondents he has judged advisable to trust.  By the
same means the secret of every individual may likewise be almost entirely
secured to him, at least against the notorious and manifest violation that, in
most of the governments, has been so often made of it, not only by the post-offices,
but by all the other modes of conveyance and communication.
P. R.
N. B.  Experience will soon bring to a general demonstration, that the method here
employed, is not only conducive to secure the secret of any kind of written intercourse,
but is even advantageously applicable to all telegraphic purposes, as well as to all
sorts of reconnoitering signals, either for the sea or land service; however, as this
matter might rather be termed a novelty for the generality of the people, it has been
judged advisable to have every copy of this extract signed by the AUTHOR, so that
he may, at any time, be answerable for the assertions contained in it.
