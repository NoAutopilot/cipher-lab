Giovanni Fontana's "Cipher Disk" (ca. 1430) in an Encrypted Booklet

While Leon Battista Alberti's treatise (ca. 1466) is generally accredited with the invention of polyalphabetic cipher by his cipher disk, there had been related ideas before him (Wikipedia in Italian).

Giovanni Fontana's Disk

In particular, Giovanni Fontana, Secretum de thesauro experimentorum ymaginationis hominum (Secret of the treasure of experiments in man's imagination) (ca. 1430) describes a disk for recording a short word, which could also have been used as a cipher disk.

The disk appears in a booklet, of which a manuscript copy is archived as BnF NAL 635 (Gallica).

Despite the striking similarity, there is no evidence that Alberti knew Fontana's idea. The booklet was only in manuscript form and was forgotten for centuries (Wikipedia in Italian).

Giovanni Fontana's Encrypted Book

Moreover, interestingly, the booklet is written almost entirely in cipher.

The deciphered plaintext in Latin as well as translation in Italian is provided in Eugenio Battisti and Giuseppa Saccaro Battisti (1984), "Le macchine cifrate di Giovanni Fontana", according to Nick Pelling's Cypher Misteries.
