Stager Cipher No.7 Continued in Use after Abandonment

During the US Civil War, the Union army used route transposition ciphers devised by Anson Stager. One early version, No.7, was widely used in the west but was abandoned in August 1862 when its copy was captured in a raid of a Federal outpost. Still its use as late as 29 September 1862 is known (see another article).

Now its use as late as December 1862 is revealed by the successful codebreaking by Richard Bean (with the help of Claude Opus 5) of unsolved telegrams (ibid.) to Brigadier General Robert H. Milroy (Wikipedia) in Indiana State Library Hosted Digital Collections (URL changed from the one given in the previous article).

The receiver uses a grid with 12 lines (specified by the indicator) and 6 columns (fixed) to fill words in cells according to the predetermined order of columns, either upward or downward as predetermined. Then, the plaintext can be read horizontally from to to bottom.

Stager Cipher No.7

When I put the above telegrams in "Unsolved Historical Ciphers", I checked the indicators "Turkey" and "China" were not found in the original cipher books No.9, No.10, No.12, No.1, and No.2, but now I found the route recovered by Richard matches the route of the Cipher No.7. In this early version, the general route (i.e., the pattern of going up/down in a specific order of columns) was fixed, and the indicator specified the number of lines. The choice of indicators ("Turkey" for 12 lines, "China" for 8 lines) is consistent with the known example ("Austria" for 9 lines), which seems to confirm the use of Cipher No.7.
