French Code between General Rochambeau and Admiral de Barras (1781)

During the War of American Independence, General Rochambeau, commanding a French expeditionary force, cooperated with General Washington to make a strategic surprise against the British General Cornwallis. While they pinned Cornwallis at Yorktown, arrival of the West Indian fleet under Admiral de Grasse forced Cornwallis to surrender.

Code used between Rochambeau and Washington (WE001)

The code used between Rochambeau and Admiral de Barras, who commanded the French fleet in the American waters, was the one printed by Ralph E. Weber as WE001 (see another article). It was originally prepared by Benjamin Tallmadge, Washington's director of the secret service, and was a one-part code of 736 entries.

Rochambeau-de Barras Code

A letter from de Barras to Rochambeau dated 17 August 1781 available at Beinecke Rare Book & Manuscript Library, Yale University shows that the French general used a more sophisticated two-part code with his compatriot.

With the highest numbers 841 ("un(e)"), this is only slightly larger than WE001 but is significantly stronger in that the assignment of words/syllables are not in alphabetical order.

Robert Morris' Code (WE011)

The Americans were by no means ignorant of two-part code. WE011, which Washington used with Robert Morris, Superintendent of Finances, was a two-part code and was later provided to Rochambeau (see another article).

Rochambeau Papers

The above letter from de Barras to Rochambeau belongs to the Rochambeau Papers. According to the Full HTML catalogue, there are five manuscripts under "Codes and Ciphers" in Box 5. Of these, Folders 294 and 295 must be WE011 and folders 296 and 297 must be WE001. Folder 298 "Tableau Pour Chiffrer. Tableau pour Dechiffrer" may be the code used between Rochambeau and de Barras.
