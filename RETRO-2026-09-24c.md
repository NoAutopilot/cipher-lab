## Subject 1: the 05:12 doubling and the 05:30-06:10 rate-limit event

Timeline, from ROOM.md and BUDGETS.md: the owner authorised doubling at 05:12 UTC ("2x our efforts overall
keeping our rate limit approach in mind"). All seven lanes (T, G, V, S, N, R, W) raised their ceiling to 16 live
workers at once; LANE N alone reports 15 live by 05:23 and five more scout dispatches by 05:35. The five-hour
window — one account-wide pool, not per-lane (BUDGETS.md) — hit `allowed_warning` at 05:30 UTC, independently
logged within the same few minutes by LANE R, LANE V and LANE N, and by LANE W at 05:33; `rejected` followed at
about 05:50; reset at 06:10 (parent's 06:34 UTC ROOM.md note). Four of seven lanes (R partially, V fully, N, W)
went idle for 40+ minutes.

What went right: in-flight workers kept running through the warning (LANE R: "R1, R3, R4, R5 run on"); no lane
exceeded its own cap; LANE T recognised its own scope was exhausted and declined to fill to the new ceiling on
its own judgement ("lane scope exhausted, so not filling to the new 16-worker ceiling... parent: stop rather
than widen") instead of manufacturing busywork; LANE V used its idle window to write a handoff rather than sit
silent.

What went wrong: the doubling instruction raised every lane's ceiling by the same flat amount, all at once,
with no regard to (a) the window being one shared pool, so seven lanes jumping together multiplies the burst
rather than each lane getting its own independent budget; (b) each lane's actual remaining scope (LANE T had
none); (c) host-level bottlenecks inside a lane (Gallica's two-fetcher cap, Riksarkivet's one-worker-at-a-time
rule) that make several of a lane's 16 "slots" non-network busywork, not real throughput. Eighteen minutes from
authorisation to first warning, then a ~40-minute stall on most lanes. The parent already corrected this at
06:34 UTC, in one ROOM.md line: ceiling to 10/lane, and "an allowed_warning posted by ANY lane stops spawning
in EVERY lane." That fix is right, but a ROOM.md line scrolls out of the 30-line window other workers read and
is not what BUDGETS.md's own rule currently says (BUDGETS.md still reads "`allowed_warning` anywhere: **that
lane** stops spawning", i.e. only the one that saw it). It needs to live in BUDGETS.md, under its own name.

## Subject 2: the nominations lane, 17-for-17 copy-order

LANE N posted 17 stage-2 nominations in roughly its first hour (05:10-05:38 UTC: 4 Riksarkivet, 3 more
Riksarkivet, 6 Italian regional-archive IR rows, 4 English N50-53 rows), and every one is `copy-order`: it
needs the owner's copy request before any recovery or cryptanalysis lane can touch it. LANE R (recovery,
copy-free hosts only per its own lane brief) flagged this directly at 05:22 UTC: "for LANE N: every nomination
so far is copy-order; LANE R has spare capacity for copy-free ones (IIIF or public images...)." LANE N's own
count at 05:31 UTC: "Hour count so far: 5 stage-2 (R1 R2 R3 R5 R7), 0 copy-free."

Root cause, not bad luck: `scout.js`'s scoring formula is `language_fit x3 + material x2 + key_lead x3 + size
x2 + competition x2 + weight + unread x3` (max 48). `material` is where copy-free-ness is actually scored ("3 =
images or full transcription online free" down to "0 = nothing online"), but it carries the same x2 weight as
`size` and `competition`, and less than `key_lead` and `unread` at x3 each. An undigitised item with a strong
key lead and no competition — exactly what a Riksarkivet or an Italian-archive catalogue entry usually is:
interesting, unread, unclaimed by either solver project, physically inaccessible — can score `material` 0 and
still rank tier A on the other five axes. Nothing in the scoring or the dispatch loop reads what the downstream
lane can currently use; the mismatch surfaced only because LANE R happened to say so in ROOM.md an hour in.

## Subject 3: check-solved's blind spot on "unsolved when printed" (evidenced by the same Oxenstierna audit)

`ciphers/oxenstierna-gustav-adolf-1632/AUDIT.md` already writes its own fix, verbatim, in its postmortem: for
any cipher printed "unsolved" in an edition, search the national historical/period journal(s) for the years
after the edition's date for the edition's title and item number, and run a phrase search (normalised **and**
period spelling) on the decoded text before reporting a verdict. Three separate workers (check-solved, the key
hunt, the original-letter hunt) all took the 1888 editor's footnote at face value; the fix that actually found
Torpadie's 1888 solution was a full-text search run only by the verifier, an hour after the reading was posted.
Porting the postmortem's own wording into check-solved.md — which already carries three dated lesson paragraphs
in the same voice — closes the gap at the source instead of relying on the verifier gate to catch it every time.
