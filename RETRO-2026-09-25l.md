## 2. `room.py --push`'s rebase can drop a STATUS.md section the way it cannot drop ROOM.md -- give STATUS.md the same shrink guard

Commit 9a1e34b (19:50, a `room.py --push` rebase merge titled "update") dropped the "LANE B3 handoff" section
from STATUS.md; the LANE B3 orchestrator caught it by chance at 20:23 re-opening its own section ("commit
9a1e34b... dropped the LANE B3 handoff section from STATUS.md -- a room.py --push rebase keeping the wrong side
of a STATUS.md conflict; restored. Anyone who pushed STATUS.md around 19:50: check your own section survived.")
and restored it by hand. `room.py`'s `room_ok()` already refuses to push a ROOM.md that shrank or carries
conflict markers (the exact fix RETRO-2026-09-24b built after ROOM.md itself was overwritten by a stale-clone
stub); STATUS.md, written by every lane orchestrator's "LANE <X> handoff" section and the parent's own "Parent
handoff" section (parent.md "Handing over"), has no equivalent guard, so a non-conflicting 3-way merge that
picks the wrong side silently loses a whole section with no error and no diff anyone is prompted to check. This
is the exact "rules become tools" pattern (CLAUDE.md Usage 8a): a rule that already failed once (B3's own catch
was luck, not process) gets a mechanical check, not a reminder to "re-check your section after each push."
